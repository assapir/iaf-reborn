# IAF radio terms for the four-ship — research

This page records the radio calls of the AI's first milestone, where the player leads a four-ship of F-16I Sufas
(callsign Kidon 1, its jets Kidon 1-1 to 1-4: the callsign row) and no combat takes place. For each call it gives the term the Israeli Air
Force (IAF) uses, as public sources write it, and the English words the voice says. The voices stay English
(synthesised) with Hebrew subtitles, so each row has both. The user asked on 19 November 2026: "I said search for IAF
terms and if not fallback to NATO". Research date: 19 November 2026. It is research only: it changes no code or
strings.

**How to read the table**
- **Hebrew subtitle:** the IAF term in **bold** where a source has it. "Ours" marks plain Hebrew we wrote around a
  term, or for a call with no term found; it is a translation of the sentence, not a new term.
- **Source:** "IAF site, second-hand" means the IAF's site (iaf.org.il) refused automated access (HTTP 403 on
  19 November 2026) and the page is known only from a search engine's quote. "Hebrew Wikipedia" means the term is in
  Hebrew Wikipedia but no IAF source was found for it. "No IAF term found, NATO" means the English is NATO / US
  usage and the Hebrew is ours.
- "Inferred" marks our own reasoning; "open" means no source was found.

**Fourth search (22 November 2026: every call in the sim).** The user: "Do we have the iaf terms for fox 1/2/3 etc?",
then "Same for target attack (I think it calls alpha) and so on". The air-to-air calls, the air-to-ground calls, the
kill, break, ejection, the AWACS and the tower were searched again in Hebrew and English.
- **The language.** IAF crews talk on the radio in Hebrew: in a joint exercise with French Rafales the IAF pilots
  "had to communicate in a common language: English" and "practiced the English phrases and terms they expected to
  need", the example being "I'm firing a missile"[^toi-french]. Inferred: NATO brevity is not their daily radio. Some
  English words live on as loanwords (בינגו, רוג'ר, קלירנס, פיקל[^inn-terms]). No public source shows an IAF crew
  saying "fox", "splash", "winchester" or "engaged". The only Hebrew "פוקס 1" found explains US Navy F-14s to the
  reader[^calcalist-f14].
- **Changed in the game.** Fox one / two / three: **שיגור**, one line for the three (the table's row). Splash:

**Where the sources differ**
- Close formation: the IAF site writes **מבנה צמוד**; the pilot's account writes **מבנה מכונס**[^mustov]. Recommended
  (inferred): מבנה צמוד, the IAF's own and found on several pages.
- Line abreast: Hebrew Wikipedia names it **מבנה מקבילי**; the IAF site describes low flying "בשורה אופקית", which
  is a description, not a name.
- Echelon: Hebrew Wikipedia gives three names (אלכסון, סולם, מדרג) and no IAF source settles between them. Its
  gallery names the shape **מבנה אלכסון**, so the game uses that (20 November 2026; until then מדרג).
- The wingman: **מספר 2** names the position, **בן הזוג** the partner; both are in IAF-related sources. The press
  writes the digit ("מספר 2"); the IAF site and the radio rules spell it out ("מספר שתיים").


**The user's corrections** (20 November 2026; each applies to every Hebrew screen, subtitle and voice line)
- The callsigns: kidon style (the callsign row): "Use kidon style, where kidon 1 is the first formation the second is
  kidon 2 etc".
- A runway's side is the noun: "25 ימין", "26 שמאל" (and "מרכז" for C), not the adjective "ימני" / "שמאלי": "it
  should be 25 ימין. Not 25 ימני".
- The AWACS's clearance to attack ("free to engage") is **רשאי לתקוף**: "מותר לתקוף should be רשאי לתקוף". The
  tower's clearances are "אישור התנעה", "אישור המראה" and the like. No Hebrew string, subtitle or voice line says
  "מותר ל…" now; the manual's tower chapter did, and now says "יש אישור להתניע", "לעזוב", "להיכנס", "להתחיל" (20
  November 2026; "רשאי" would address the reader as a man, and the rule is gender-neutral Hebrew). Sources: the aviation radio guide's clearance examples "רשאי
  להמריא", "רשאי לנחות"[^omni-rt] and the Aviation Regulations' rule that "להמריא" is said only when the take-off is
  permitted[^takanot]. No IAF publication was found, so the manual's prose uses the neutral "יש אישור ל…" (inferred).
- The HUD is **תע"ל**, written with the straight ASCII `"`: "Hud in Hebrew is תע\"ל. You can use this form." Every
  Hebrew string, kneeboard line and manual source says תע"ל; the English "HUD" stays in English text.

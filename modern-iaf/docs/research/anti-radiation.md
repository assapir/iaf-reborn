# Anti-radiation weapons in the Israeli Air Force — research

The user asked on 25 November 2026, about the F-35I Adir: "It still can use harm, no?", then "Yes send a job to
research anti radiation use in iaf". This page holds what public sources say about the anti-radiation weapons the
Israeli Air Force (IAF) has used (§1), how it suppresses air defences (§2), what each of Modern IAF's three jets can
carry (§3), and a recommendation for the sim (§4). Research date: 25 November 2026.

Sources are cited as footnotes. "Inferred" marks our own reasoning; "open" means no source was found;
"second-hand" means the fact is known only from a search engine's quote of a page. FlightGlobal, Janes,
GlobalSecurity and the Pentagon's test office (DOT&E) refused automated access, so they were left alone.

## Summary

- No public source shows an IAF jet carrying the AGM-88 HARM. The US offered it in 2013; whether Israel bought it is
  open.
- The IAF fired the AGM-78 Standard ARM ("Purple Fist") from the F-4 Phantom in 1982. The Phantom left service in 2004.
- Today the IAF suppresses air defences with stealth (the F-35I first), precision bombs, stand-off missiles, drones,
  loitering munitions and electronic warfare. No source names an anti-radiation missile in the strikes on Iran
  (2024 to 2026).
- None of the F-16I Sufa, the F-16D Barak or the F-35I Adir has a sourced anti-radiation missile. The F-35's US
  anti-radiation missile (the AARGM-ER) is not yet integrated on the jet, and no source shows Israel ordering it.

## 1. Anti-radiation weapons the IAF has used or holds

| Weapon | What sources say | Source |
|---|---|---|
| AGM-45 Shrike | Israel is "the only other major user" after the United States. The IDF also fired it from the ground, boosted, from a Sherman chassis (the Kilshon, "trident"). It lists the Yom Kippur War (1973) among the Shrike's wars. Which IAF jets carried it, and when it left service, is open. | [^w-shrike] |
| AGM-78 Standard ARM ("Purple Fist", אגרוף סגול) | Offered by the US in 1975; first missiles delivered in 1976, operational in 1977, fired from the F-4 Phantom. Israel also fired it from a truck (the Keres, קרס). First used in combat on 9 June 1982 (Operation Mole Cricket 19, מבצע ערצב 19): each jet of the first wave carried two, launched tens of kilometres from the batteries. | [^hm-keres][^w-mole] |
| AGM-88 HARM | In April 2013 the US offered Israel "anti-radiation missiles" with KC-135 tankers, V-22s and radars for its fighters; the report names no type. Wikipedia lists the IAF as a HARM operator and names the AGM-88 as the 2013 offer. No delivery, purchase notice, photo or jet is found. | [^ain-2013][^w-harm] |
| STAR-1 (Delilah-AR) | An Israeli Military Industries (IMI) anti-radiation weapon, shown in 1995; an F-16 flew its carriage trials while it was in development. Whether it entered service is open. | [^aft-f16i] |
| Delilah | The name first belonged to an air-launched decoy drone of the late 1970s, and to an anti-radiation drone; CSIS says the Delilah "was modified into a lightweight, anti-radar missile". The missile in service since 1994 is guided by GPS / INS with an electro-optical or imaging-infrared seeker and a two-way data link to the operator; no source gives it a radar-homing seeker. Carried by the F-16D and the F-16I. Fired at Syrian air defence systems on 10 May 2018. | [^csis-delilah][^w-delilah] |
| ROCKS | A Rafael stand-off missile in IAF service since 2019, guided by GPS / INS with an electro-optical seeker and, per Rafael, "anti-radiation homing" as one of its terminal modes. Reported as certified on the F-16I (second-hand). Possibly used against Iran in April 2024. | [^w-rocks][^janes-rocks] |
| Harpy, Harop | IAI's loitering munitions. The Harpy is "designed to attack radar systems"; Israel is an operator. The Harop is launched from a ground canister and guided by an operator through its electro-optical sensor. Neither is carried by a fighter. | [^w-harpy][^w-harop] |

## 2. How the IAF suppresses air defences

| When | How | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 1973, Yom Kippur War | The strike on the Syrian batteries (Dugman 5) lost six Phantoms and destroyed one SA-3 battery; the batteries had moved. The IAF then built a full suppression system of real-time intelligence, drones, anti-radiation missiles and electronic warfare. | [^migflug] |
| 1982, Mole Cricket 19 | Mastiff drones drew the Syrian radars on and Scout drones relayed tracking data; E-2C and Boeing 707 electronic aircraft analysed the signals; F-4 Phantoms fired the AGM-78 and AGM-45 and bombed; jamming pods disrupted the radars. 29 of 30 batteries were destroyed. | [^w-mole][^hm-keres] |
| 2007, Syria (Operation Outside the Box) | Electronic attack and jamming blinded the radar network (reported, not confirmed); a radar site was destroyed with precision bombs. F-15Is bombed, F-16Is escorted. | [^w-orchard] |
| 2018, Syria | Delilah missiles and, per another account, a Harop hit Syrian air defence systems on 10 May. Which weapon hit which system is open. | [^w-delilah][^w-harop] |
| 2024, Iran | S-300 systems were destroyed in April and October with what appear to be air-launched ballistic missiles (ROCKS, Air LORA named as the IAF's stand-off weapons). | [^twz-s300] |
| 2025, Iran (Rising Lion) | The F-35I "stood at the center of the first stages"; other jets did not enter Iran's airspace unless led by F-35s. A covert Mossad operation launched missiles and loitering munitions inside Iran; drones and precision bombs (JDAM, SDB) struck too. The IDF claimed more than 70 batteries struck (second-hand). No source names an anti-radiation missile. | [^fpri][^rusi][^idf-70] |
| 2026, Iran (Roaring Lion) | About 92% of the top-tier mobile SAMs and about 80% of the long-range radars destroyed (IDF figures). No source names an anti-radiation missile. | [^jpost-2026] |

Inferred: since 1982 the IAF finds the emitters with drones, electronic support and its jets' own sensors, and
destroys them with weapons that are not radar-homing (bombs, stand-off missiles, loitering munitions). The F-35I's
sensors take the finding role in 2025.

## 3. Our three jets

| Jet | An anti-radiation weapon in IAF service | Source |
|---|---|---|
| F-16I Sufa | None sourced. Its inboard wing stations carry "anti-radiation missiles" among other air-to-ground weapons (a general list, no type named). The candidates: ROCKS with its anti-radiation mode (certified on the F-16I, second-hand); the AGM-88 (open). | [^aft-f16i][^janes-rocks] |
| F-16D Barak | None sourced. It carries the Delilah (not radar-homing). Whether the IAF's Block 30 / 40 carries the AGM-88 is open. | [^csis-delilah] |
| F-35I Adir | None. The US AARGM-ER (AGM-88G) is sized for the F-35A's and F-35C's weapon bays; the US Navy's first carriers are the F/A-18E/F and EA-18G, with initial operational capability planned for September 2026. External carriage on all F-35 variants is planned, with no date. Sales to Australia, Finland and the Netherlands are approved, and Norway has announced it will buy; five foreign sales cases are not named, and no source names Israel. | [^twz-aargm] |

The F-35I's role against air defences is to find them with its own sensors and to strike them first with the
weapons it already carries (§2, 2025), or to guide other jets to them.[^fpri]

## 4. Recommendation for Modern IAF

Leave anti-radiation missiles out of all three jets: no public source shows the IAF carrying one on any of them.

- **F-35I Adir:** no anti-radiation weapon. Its air-defence work is the stealthy first strike with the GBU-31 and
  GBU-39 it already carries (inferred from §2 and §3).
- **F-16D Barak:** no anti-radiation weapon.
- **F-16I Sufa:** no HARM. If the user wants one radar-homing weapon, the only one with any IAF source is ROCKS on
  the F-16I (§1), and even there the anti-radiation mode in IAF service is open. It would need a powered stand-off
  missile class (the Delilah's record is waiting on the same class), a station (open; inferred 4 / 6, as the
  SPICE 2000), the WPN page for its handoff, and an emitter list for its seeker. The 1998 game's HARM sensor
  (`crates/iaf-avionics/src/harm.rs`: emitters inside ±15°, 20 NM, nearest first) could give that list (inferred).

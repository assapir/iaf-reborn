# F-15I Ra'am: the Ra'am option

This page collects the public data for the IAF's F-15I Ra'am ("Thunder"), the jet of the Extras switch "F-15 version":
Baz (F-15C) / Ra'am (F-15I) ([roadmap.md](roadmap.md) "F-15I Ra'am option"). Part by part, it says what the switch
changes and where each number comes from, the way [f16i.md](f16i.md) does for the Sufa. §10 records the user's
decisions, §11 what is built and what is open. Research date: 2026-11-02; built the same day.

- **Sources:** a footnote names the source of each fact. "Inferred" marks our own reasoning; "open" means no public
  source was found. Where the only figure is the F-15E's, the text says so: the F-15I is "based on the
  F-15E"[^raam-wp-e] with different avionics, but no source says its weights or tankage equal the F-15E's.
- **Terms:** CFT = conformal fuel tank. WSO = weapon systems officer (the back seat). MPD = multipurpose display
  (monochrome), MPCD = multipurpose colour display, UFC = upfront control (the keypad and text panel under the HUD).
  OWS = overload warning system (the F-15's g limiter cue). TO = USAF technical order (flight manual). AIFF / IFF =
  identification friend or foe. MAW = missile approach warner. FS = US Federal Standard 595 colour number.

**Key facts**
- A two-seat strike Eagle built for Israel: intent announced 27 January 1994, 21 jets (Peace Fox V) and an option for
  4 (Peace Fox VI) taken up in November 1995; first flight 12 September 1997; the first jets handed over in St Louis on
  6 November 1997; delivered to 69 Squadron ("The Hammers") at Hatzerim from January 1998 to September
  1999[^raam-baugher][^raam-flight97]. Hebrew Wikipedia dates the first two jets' landing at Hatzerim to 19 January
  1998[^raam-hewiki]. 25 built[^raam-wp-e].
- Engines: two Pratt & Whitney F100-PW-229[^raam-wp-e][^raam-f15einfo][^raam-baugher], the engine of the Sufa
  ([real-aircraft.md](real-aircraft.md) §8.1).
- Israeli avionics in place of parts of the F-15E's: no US radar warning or jamming suite (an Elisra suite instead),
  a new central computer with an Israeli GPS / INS, the Elbit DASH helmet in both cockpits, an LCD upfront control, the
  APG-70I radar with a lower-resolution ground map[^raam-wp-e][^raam-f15einfo][^raam-baugher].
- Later: an upgrade approved in January 2016 (structure, an AESA radar, avionics, weapons)[^raam-wp-e]; from 2017 the
  APG-70 was to be replaced by the APG-82(V)1[^raam-wp-apg63]. In 2024 Israel ordered 25 F-15IA (an F-15EX
  derivative) with an option for 25 more, and the upgrade of the 25 F-15Is to a similar standard[^raam-hewiki]. The
  game models today's upgraded F-15I (the user's decision, §10); the radar stays the APG-70I, the one with a sourced
  range.
- In the game the Ra'am takes the F-15's slot (type code 110), so the missions, AI F-15s and loadouts stay valid (§8).
  Its aircraft definition is `game/extra/planes/f15i/airframe.json` on the F-15 base
  ([adding-a-plane.md](adding-a-plane.md) "Modern planes"): its flight-model row, model, radar range and the AI's,
  gun rounds, stations, Arming art, Jet list name, pilot guide, TSD label and info tag.

## 1. Flight data

`crates/iaf-flight/src/data_set.rs`). A Real row "F-15I" with `base: Some("F-15")` would sit next to it, as the

change 2 of March 1991[^raam-to]. It covers F-15Es with either engine; its -229 performance appendix was still empty

Notes:
- **Weights.** The TO's operating weight includes the crew; the F-15C row's 28,476 lb is the SAC empty weight. The
  two are not the same measure; the 37,000 lb proposal keeps the TO figure as published (inferred). Wikipedia's
  34,600 / 38,990 lb (empty) exceed the TO's operating weights, so they describe heavier jets or count differently
  (open).
- **Fuel.** JP-4 weighs about 6.5 lb per US gal, so 4,875 lb per CFT matches 750 gal (inferred). Wikipedia's total
  implies 9,352 lb for the pair; the TO's loading chart gives the pair 13,667 lb full and 4,206 lb empty, so 9,461 lb
  of fuel (fig. A1-1)[^raam-to]. Three figures, open which is right; the row keeps the user's 9,750 lb (§10). The
  chart's 4,206 lb on the TO's 32,500 lb operating weight without CFTs gives 36,706 lb, the 37,000 lb rounded
  (inferred).
- **Full fuel weight:** 37,000 + 22,665 = 59,665 lb, 42 % more than the F-15C row's 41,931 lb, for 24 % more thrust
  (inferred). The Ra'am will climb and turn less well than the Baz and fly much further, as the Sufa does against the
  Barak.
- **Speed (built).** The F-15C row's speeds come from a SAC chart; the Ra'am's targets are secondary and carry the
  CFTs. With the F-15C row's drag the Ra'am flew Mach 2.39 at 40,000 ft and 1.36 at sea level, faster than the Baz
  with the CFTs on. Only the wave drag is fitted, to the one target the model can meet: 0.04 gives Mach 2.00 at
  40,000 ft. The 700 kt at low altitude is not fitted: no wave drag that keeps Mach 2.0 high gives it (the run: Mach
  1.16, 771 kt), so it reads as a limit like the F-15C's 800 KCAS (inferred). The CFTs' drag number then raised
  `cd0` to 0.042 (2026-11-02): Mach 1.98 and 765 kt. The wave drag was not refitted: 1.98 is within the secondary
  target.
- **Validation** (`IAF_JET=F-15I cargo test --release -p iaf-flight --test validation -- --nocapture`; reference row
  "F-15I"): weights, thrust, fuel flow, stall floor, ceiling and the 40,000 ft speed are within their references; sea
  level 765 kt against 700 is "close". At full fuel the climb at 350 kt is 37,300 ft/min (the F-15C row 42,000) and the
  420 kt turn 16.1 deg/s at 6.4 g (the F-15C row 22.4 at 8.7 g; the stall floor limits it, not the 8.1 g). The F-15E's "50,000+ ft/min" has no weight, so it is
  not a reference. The neutral-stick test from 350 kt keeps a 0.9° climb left by the start at 13° α (a test-point
  effect; real-aircraft.md §3.1). The 312 landing (`crates/iaf-flight/tests/autopilot.rs`) flares and stops the F-15I on the runway.
- **Reading the TO's charts (2026-11-02).** The scan's text layer lacks the figures, but its page images are legible:
  archive.org's single-page JP2 files are 2,470 × 3,244 px (300 dpi), twice the PDF's embedded 150 dpi images. Figs.
  A1-1, A1-6 and 5-7 were read from them: the axes calibrated by their tick marks, the curves found by pixel
  profiles. The method gives the stall chart's own sample problem (fig. A1-5: 40,000 lb, 15° bank, 10,000 ft, 107 kt)
  as 107.5 kt. Readings are good to about ±1 kt and ±0.05 g.
- **g limits.** The row carries one pair, the original's MaxG / MinG. It takes fig. 5-7's OWS-inoperative limits for
  configuration C (CFTs, the F-15I's air-to-air load) at the operating weight, the highest that chart allows with
  CFTs. With the OWS working the jet may pull more (the TO sample's 8.0 g CFT-interface limit at 45,000 lb against
  the chart's 7.0 g); the OWS's own schedule is not published (open). Configurations E / F (air-to-ground stores on
  the CFTs) are 0.5 g lower.
- The game counts no store weight for tanks (docs/weapons.md §2.5 bug c); the CFT fuel is internal fuel, and the empty
  CFTs are in `empty_lb` (inferred, as for the Sufa).

## 2. Radar: AN/APG-70I

- **Lineage.** The APG-70 is a 1980s redesign of the APG-63 for reliability and maintenance; gate arrays added
  modes[^raam-raytheon][^raam-wp-apg63]. Raytheon lists it on "late model F-15C, D, and E aircraft and on the F-15I and
  the F-15S"[^raam-raytheon].
- **APG-70I against the USAF APG-70:** "identical air-to-air capabilities"; the ground-map modes (DBS, SAR) of the USAF
  set have at least three times the resolution[^raam-wp-apg63]; "degraded resolution one-third below the original
  APG-70"[^raam-wp-e]. A fan site suspects that EP, EA and NCTR were deleted too; it says there is "no direct
  proof"[^raam-f15einfo].
- **Range:** "fighter-sized targets at 56 nmi" (104 km), "large airliner-sized targets at 150 nmi", from Aviation Week
  (2010) as cited by Wikipedia[^raam-wp-e][^raam-awst].
- **Modes** named in the F-15E flight manual[^raam-to]: air-to-air with an IFF interrogator (AAI); air-to-ground real
  beam map and high resolution map (HRM) updates, precision velocity update; the crew may freeze a ground map and
  search the air[^raam-wp-e]. The mode details are in TO 1F-15E-34-1-1, which is not public (open).
- **In the game's terms** (built):
  - Weapon data = Real: the definition's `radar.km` = 104 (56 NM), read by `real_weapons.gd` `radar_nm`. The Baz's
    Real range is 135 km (docs/real-weapons.md §1.3: APG-63, "110–160 km tracking a small fighter", a weaker source).
    So with Real the Ra'am's radar sees less than the Baz's. Both figures are sourced but measure different targets;
    the user chose the APG-70I's 104 km (2026-11-02).
  - The mode table (docs/radar.md §1): no new row. The air-to-air modes are the APG-70's, and no source gives a ratio
    to the APG-63 (the Sufa row has Lockheed's "30 % more"). MAP and GMT stay: the game's map has no resolution, so
    the APG-70I's coarser SAR changes nothing. The F-15's row 0 stays.
  - The AI's sensor (`combat.gd` `sensor_nm`, 40 NM for every AI jet in the original): scaled as the Sufa's (40 ×
    1.3 for its 30 % more range), here by the APG-70I's 104 km against the F-15's Real 135 km: 40 × 104 / 135 =
    30.8 NM (`radar.ai_nm`, inferred).

## 3. Weapons and stations

**Stations of the F-15E / F-15I** (TO 1F-15E-1[^raam-to]; f-15e.info[^raam-f15einfo-cft]):
- two wing stations, 2 and 8, each a pylon with two missile rails (2A / 2B, 8A / 8B);
- the centreline, station 5;
- six bomb racks on each CFT (LC-1 … LC-6, RC-1 … RC-6): three on top (BRU-46/A, 1,100 lb each) and three in a row
  below (BRU-47/A; front and rear 2,200 lb together, the middle one 3,300 lb); two LAU-106 missile launchers in each
  CFT's lower row for AIM-7 / AIM-120;
- up to 23,000 lb of fuel and stores[^raam-wp-e]; up to eight AIM-120, or four AIM-9 and four AIM-120[^raam-usaf].

GBU-31. The CFTs' 12 racks have no game stations: the fuselage stations C, D, F, G stand for them (inferred).

## 5. RWR and EW suite

- The F-15I came without the F-15E's TEWS (ALR-56C warner, ALQ-135 jammer, ALQ-128): "The TEWS was deleted
  altogether"[^raam-f15einfo]; "initially lacked Radar Warning Receivers"[^raam-wp-e]; sold without RWR or ECM
  suite[^raam-hewiki]. Baugher: the F-15I lacks the ALQ-128 and the ALQ-135[^raam-baugher]; f-15e.info: no ALQ-128 antenna and no ALQ-135
  blades under the nose, but ALQ-135B antennas in square tail-boom radomes[^raam-f15einfo] (the two disagree: open).
- **What replaced it:** the Elisra SPS-2110 integrated EW system[^raam-baugher], "which includes a missile approach
  warning system", plus "an Elisra SPS-3000 EW system"[^raam-f15einfo]; Wikipedia says "Elisra ASPS"[^raam-wp-e]. Other
  pages name it SPS-2100 (open which designation is right). Extra chaff and flare dispensers on the tail booms or upper
  rear fuselage[^raam-f15einfo][^raam-baugher]; an Israeli chaff and flare system[^raam-hewiki].
- **In the game:** the RWR logic stays (docs/rwr.md). The F-15's built-in ECM in the game (docs/weapons.md §10) stands
  for the jammer (inferred). A missile-approach cue is new; as for the Sufa (f16i.md §6), the RWR's list of missiles
  launched at the jet could feed it (inferred).

## 6. AIFF and voice warnings

- **IFF:** the F-15E has an IFF transponder (modes 1, 2, 3/A, 4, C) and an air-to-air interrogator (AAI), its display
  described in the classified TO 1F-15E-34-1-1[^raam-to]. The F-15I's set and its symbols: open. Planned as for the
  F-15C (real-hud.md "IFF"): interrogate on a key, replies on the radar display (inferred).
- **Voice warnings** (F-15E, TO 1F-15E-1[^raam-to]): "AB BURN THRU LEFT / RIGHT", "BINGO FUEL", "LOW ALTITUDE",
  "OBSTACLE AHEAD", "OVER-G" (the OWS: "OVER G, OVER G" at 100 %, tones at 85 % and 92 %), "TF FAILED", "WARNING, AMAD
  FIRE", "WARNING, FUEL LOW", "WARNING, ENGINE FIRE LEFT / RIGHT", "WARNING, OVERTEMP LEFT / RIGHT", "WARNING,
  TRANSFER PUMP". The F-15 (110) already has the Betty voice (`flight_sounds.gd` `BETTY_TYPES`). Whether the F-15I
  keeps this set and in which language: open (docs/real-betty.md).

## 7. How it looks

Checked on 2026-11-02 against the model notes of aircraft-models.md §6.7 and FlightGear's IAF livery
(`livery.IAF.jpg`, under assets/source/f15) with these photos: 227 in flight, right side, 2015[^raam-c227]; 232
taxiing, left side, Hatzerim 2013[^raam-c232]; 259 at sunset with bombs, September 2024[^raam-c259]; a formation of
seven or more, June 2025[^raam-c2025].

What matches:
- two seats under one canopy; the CFTs with their stub pylons;
- the camouflage: three upper colours, tan-brown, sand and pale green, in large blotches; light grey under the
  fuselage, the CFTs' lower sides and the drop tanks; a darker grey radome (all photos);
- the eagle's head (black and white, beak forward) on each fin's outer face on a tan field; a small shield badge near
  the fin tip; the three-digit tail number (2xx) in black under the eagle; roundels on the forward fuselage below the
  front cockpit[^raam-c227][^raam-c232]. 69 Squadron's FS colours: FS 30219, FS 33531 and FS 36375
  (fan site)[^raam-f15einfo].

Fixed (2026-11-02, `f15.rs` `livery_i`; checked on a render against the photos):
- The fins' inner faces: the livery had a black band with a white eagle's head at the top of each. The inner faces
  of 259's right fin[^raam-c259], 227's left fin[^raam-c227] and 232's right fin[^raam-c232] show plain camouflage
  to the tip, so the bands are painted out (`ERASE_I`: filled from the camouflage round them, a soft edge where green
  meets brown).
- The nose number: on 227, 232 and 259 a small dark blue number stands ahead of and below the roundel. The livery's
  "269" is in that place and of that size on a render (its digits about a quarter of the roundel's height, as on 232)
  but dark grey; it is recoloured to the blue measured on 232's photo (sRGB 52, 80, 102; `NOSE_BLUE`).

Checks (no change made):
- The F-15E's larger main wheels and bulged gear doors (§6.7's own note): 227's main wheels look larger than an
  F-15C's[^raam-c227] (inferred).
- Nozzles: the F-15I keeps the "turkey feather" covers over the nozzle actuators, which the F-15E omits and the IAF's
  earlier F-15s deleted[^raam-f15einfo][^raam-baugher]. Whether the FlightGear nozzles have them: check on a render.
- Fins: the F-15I lacks the ALQ-128 antenna on the left fin and carries a pod there like the one on the right fin;
  square ICS radomes on the tail booms; no ICS blade antennas under the nose[^raam-f15einfo] (check on a render).
- The CFTs: the F-15I's have a cone-shaped heat-exchanger intake aft, as on -229 F-15Es[^raam-f15einfo].
- The livery's tail number 269 is a real F-15I (a photo of 269 exists on airhistory.net, not checked here); the
  camouflage pattern against 269's own: open (§6.7).

## 8. What the F-15 slot fixes

The Ra'am replaces the F-15C everywhere the type code 110 is read (inferred, as for the Sufa in f16i.md §9):
- **Cockpit art:** the 1998 F-15 cockpit (cockpit index 0, its radar row 0 and three MFD portals). It is a
  single-seat panel; the Ra'am's WSO station is not drawn (no rear cockpit in the game, inferred). A 3D F-15
  cockpit does not exist (only the F-16 and F-35I have one).
- **Model:** the F-15 model slot, already switched (`f15i.glb`, aircraft-models.md §6.7).
- **Arming screen (built):** the F-15 art (`f-15.trx`: base 42,000 lb ≈ the F-15C's empty plus internal fuel,
  maximum 78,000 lb, nine stations) becomes the Ra'am's own `x_f15i`: the front view with the CFTs, which setup
  renders from the F-15I model (aircraft-models.md §6.7), the F-15's nine boxes, the title F-15I, base 59,734 lb
  (42,000 + 8,524 heavier empty + 9,210 more fuel, §1) and maximum 81,000 lb (F-15E). Until setup has run again the
  screen shows the F-15's art with the Ra'am's weights.
- **Per-type tables keyed on 110** that the definition overrides (built): the Real row ("F-15I" on the "F-15"
  section), `RADAR_KM` (`radar.km`), `combat.gd` `sensor_nm` (`radar.ai_nm`), `GUN_ROUNDS` (`gun_rounds`), the modern
  `LOADS` (`weapons.loads`), `JET_ART` (`weapons.arm_art`), the guide, the TSD label and the info tag. The engine spool
  (`spool.rs`) stays keyed on 110: the F100's times.
- **The AI:** every AI F-15 becomes a Ra'am with the switch, as the AI F-16s do with the Sufa: the same flight row
  and radar (built; `test_raam.gd` checks the AI F-15Is' fuel). The missions' F-15 loads stay valid because the
  stations stay.

## 10. The user's decisions (2026-11-02)

1. **Today's upgraded F-15I**, not the 1998 one: the weapons sourced on the F-15I since (Python 5, AIM-120, JDAM with
   BLU-109, SPICE 1000, GBU-28) are in scope (§3).
2. **The F-15E's figures where the F-15I has none**, labelled "F-15E" in the data comments, this page and
   deviations.md. The engines are the F-15I's own (2 × F100-PW-229).
3. **Radar range 104 km** (the APG-70I), though the Baz's APG-63 is 135 km on Real data.
4. **Fuel 22,665 lb in all:** 12,915 lb internal plus two 4,875 lb conformal tanks.

The standing rule: no value is filled with another type's number unless a source says it is the same or decision 2
covers it; otherwise the F-15C row's value stays, marked open.

## 11. What is built

1. **Flight row** (§1; real-aircraft.md §3.1): the Real row "F-15I" on the F-15 section (`NO_ORIGINAL`, as the
   F-16I); `data_set::variant` maps an F-15 with the switch on Ra'am to it through the definition's `fm`. The wave drag
   is fitted to Mach 2.0 at altitude; the 700 kt at sea level is not fitted (§1 "Speed"). From the TO's charts
   (2026-11-02): `cd0` 0.042 with the CFTs' drag number, the stall floor 155 kt, g +8.1 / −2.0. Validation reference
   row "F-15I"; the 312 landing test flies it.
2. **Radar and gun** (§2, §3): `radar.km` 104, `radar.ai_nm` 30.8, `gun_rounds` 500 (F-15E); no radar row.
3. **Weapons** (§3): `weapons.loads` with the Python 5 on A / I, the GBU-31(V)3 on B, C, D, F, G, H and the SPICE
   1000 on B / H, always. Not built: the GBU-28 (§3).
4. **Arming screen** (§8): `x_f15i`, rendered by setup from the model (aircraft-models.md §6.7).
5. **Look** (§7): the inner fins' bands painted out, the nose numbers blue.
6. **Labels and guide:** the TSD label "F-15I", the info tag RAAM, the Jet list "F-15I" / "אף 15I / רעם", the pilot
   guide [guides/f15i.md](guides/f15i.md) (the Reference screen's F-15 guide with the switch on); `test_raam.gd`.
7. **Engine spool** (better physics): the -229's from the definition's `engine_spool`, before type 110's -220
   (§1; flight-model.md §10.2).
8. **Later:** the HUD field of view (21° × 28°) on the Real HUD, the F-15E display formats, the missile-approach cue,
   IFF, the rear seat, the -229's thrust lapse.

# F-4E pilot guide (realistic settings)

This guide covers what changes in the F-4E Kurnass when you switch on the realistic options: the real jet's
AN/ASG-26 gunsight in place of the game's HUD (head-up display), the real jet's numbers, the real weapons and the
drag chute's drag. The F-4E is the two-seat Phantom II. Each sight mode has its own section: when to use it, the keys,
a picture with numbered callouts, what each symbol means, and a worked example. The numbers come from the game's own
data and tests. Section 12 explains the abbreviations.

How to read the pictures: each yellow number points at one symbol. The list under the picture explains it.

---

## 1. What's different in this jet

| Area | The 1998 game | With the realistic options |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sight | One simplified HUD for every jet: boxed speed and altitude, a heading tape, three text rows each side | The F-4E's AN/ASG-26 LCOSS (lead-computing optical sight system), drawn red: a pipper and two rings. Speed, altitude and heading are on the panel's gauges |
| Range | A range scale on the HUD | The LCOSS range bar inside the outer ring, with a radar lock |
| Bombs | A CCIP pipper with a fall line | The reticle itself on the predicted impact |
| Flight data | The game's F-4: subsonic at sea level, 80°/s roll, 15,000 / 25,000 lbf | F-4E (2 × J79-GE-17): Mach 1.18 at sea level, 150°/s roll, 23,740 / 35,800 lbf, +7.33 / −3 g |
| Drag chute | Drawn, with zero drag | The 16 ft ring-slot chute adds real drag |
| Weapon data | The game's own values | APQ-120 radar out to 61 km, 639 gun rounds at 6,000 rounds/min, real missile weights, speeds and ranges; the AIM-9D and Shafrir 2 lock from behind |
| Physics | The original's quirks | Real touchdown limits, more realistic spins, ground effect, a flight-path hold with the stick centred |

![The 1998 HUD at the start of mission 231](shots/f4/hud_original.png)

The same moment with the Real sight is the first picture of section 3.

**HUD warnings.** The F-4E has no Betty voice. The 1998 HUD draws a large X across its centre in the A-G gun and bomb
modes while the dive is steeper than 1° for each 100 ft above the ground (more than 20° at 2,000 ft), and when the
locked target is closer than the selected missile's minimum range (Rmin): then it hides the missile circle. The Real
gunsight has no such mark.

## 2. Settings

Set these on the Preferences screen before the flight. Every row starts at the 1998 game's setting.

| Tab | Row | Set to | What it changes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Extras | Flight data | Real aircraft | The F-4E numbers (section 9) and the drag chute's drag |
| Extras | Weapon data | Real weapons | Radar range, gun rounds, missile weights, speeds, ranges and rear-aspect seekers |
| Extras | HUD | Real | The AN/ASG-26 LCOSS |
| Extras | Better cockpit | Better | The panel compass turns with the heading; live engine and fuel needles, a sharp MFD page (section 3) |
| Graphics → MORE | Sky, Terrain close up, Imagery | Atmospheric, Detailed, Survey 2 m / Sentinel-2 | A physical sky, ground detail and photo imagery, if setup converted it |
| Graphics → MORE | Airbases | Detailed | Runways, taxiways and aprons with real markings near the ground |
| Physics | every row | on | Section 9 lists the ones that change how the F-4E flies |
| Gameplay | Easy landing, Easy aiming, No blackouts / spins / stalls | off | Real landing limits, real launch rules, g-effects, spins and stalls |

A greyed-out imagery choice needs its data: convert it with `tools/setup-cli.sh --imagery`.

## 3. The LCOSS reticle (NAV)

**When**: always; the reticle is the whole sight. In NAV it sits on the gun line. **Keys**: **N** returns to NAV.
Fly on the panel: airspeed, altitude, heading and the attitude indicator.

The real sight comes from T.O. 1F-4E-1 / -34 (via the Heatblur F-4E manual). Missile status, IN RANGE and SHOOT are
lights in the real cockpit.

![The LCOSS in NAV at the start of mission 231](shots/f4/hud_nav.png)

1. **Pipper**: a 2 mr (milliradian) dot, the aim point.
2. **Inner ring**: dashed, 25 mr across.
3. **Outer ring**: solid, 50 mr across.
4. **Roll tabs**: at 9, 12 and 3 o'clock with the wings level; they turn with your bank. The sight's glass cuts the
   ring's top here.

**Example**: 1 mr spans 1 m at 1 km. A MiG-21 (7 m wingspan) at 280 m fills the inner ring's 25 mr; at 140 m the
outer ring's 50 mr. At gun range (800 m) it spans 9 mr: about a third of the inner ring.

### The panel compass

With Extras → Better cockpit on, the compass at the bottom of the panel, under the attitude indicator, turns with the
heading (in the 1998 game it is a still picture). **V** brings the panel up into view, **PgUp** / **PgDn** slide it.

![The panel compass with Better cockpit on](shots/f4/compass.png)

1. **Lubber mark**: the heading reads on the card under it, here about 320°.
2. **Compass card**: it turns as the jet turns. The letters are the cardinal points (N, E, S, W), the numbers tens of
   degrees (`33` = 330°).
3. **Aircraft symbol**: fixed; it stands for your jet, its nose toward the lubber mark.

**Example**: the sight shows no heading; the compass shows the whole circle. To fly 090° from 320°, find 9 on the
card: it sits on the card's right half, so turn right and roll out when 9 reaches the lubber mark.

### Better cockpit: the panel

With Extras → Better cockpit on, the engine and fuel needles show the flight model's own values (in the 1998 game they
read rough values). The left column is the left engine, the right column the right engine. The MFD page is drawn
sharp at the screen's resolution.

![The engine and fuel gauges with Better cockpit on](shots/f4/better_panel.png)

1. **Fuel needle**: all the fuel you carry, the drop tanks too, over the most the jet holds with them. It falls from
   the first pound burnt (in the 1998 game it stays full until the tanks are empty).
2. **RPM**: the engine's real RPM, 60 % at idle, 100 % at military. It needs about 3 s from idle to military.
3. **Exhaust temperature**: about 450 °C at idle and 650 °C at military, a little more in AB; 900 °C with an engine
   fire. It rises in about 2 s and falls slowly.
4. **Throttle** (the dials marked FUEL FLOW): military at the full mark; in AB it moves on past military, further in
   AB 2.

**Example**: in AB both throttle needles stand past military and both temperatures a little above 650 °C. One
temperature needle at 900 °C means a fire in that engine.

## 4. Gun (M61A1)

**When**: a target inside about 6,000 ft. **Keys**: **]** to the gun, **Tab** fires. With Real data the gun has
**639 rounds** at 6,000 rounds/min and 1,030 m/s: about 6 s of firing.

The sight's gyro leads the target with the radar range when you have a lock (a fixed 1,000 ft while unlocked), and the range
bar shows that range.

![The gun locked on a MiG-23 800 m ahead](shots/f4/sight_gun.png)

1. **Pipper**, moved by the lead computation: put it on the target.
2. **Range bar**: from 6 o'clock counter-clockwise, 1,000 ft at 6 o'clock plus 1,000 ft per clock hour, up to
   6,667 ft.
3. **Outer ring**.
4. **Target**: a MiG-23.

**Example**: the MiG is 800 m (2,600 ft) ahead. The bar runs from 6 o'clock 1.6 hours counter-clockwise, to about
4:30. Put the pipper on the MiG and hold **Tab** for a 1 s burst (100 rounds).

## 5. IR missiles: AIM-9D, Shafrir 2

**When**: behind a target inside a few miles. **Keys**: **]** until the IR missile, **Space** to fire. With a radar
lock (**R**, then **Return**) the seeker is slaved to the locked target inside its cone off the nose.

| Missile | Real data |
| --- | --- |
| AIM-9D | 88.5 kg, Mach 2.5, 18 km, rear-aspect, 12 g |
| Shafrir 2 | 93 kg, Mach 2.1, 5 km, rear-aspect |

![An AIM-9D with a radar lock on a MiG-23 2,500 m ahead](shots/f4/sight_srm.png)

1. **Range bar** for missiles: from 6 o'clock counter-clockwise, 3,000 ft at 6 o'clock plus 3,000 ft per clock hour,
   up to 20,000 ft.
2. **Target**: a MiG-23, under the reticle.
3. **Pipper** on the gun line.

**Example**: the MiG is 2,500 m (8,200 ft) ahead: the bar reaches about 4:15. The AIM-9D's launch zone against it runs
from 1.35 to 16.3 km, so the range is good. Both seekers lock from behind: they track a target that flies away from
you. The guide's MiG holds still, so the seeker stays silent. Get behind a moving MiG, wait for the growl to turn into
the high tone, and fire (**Space**). Beyond half its range the seeker sees a jet with its afterburner lit.

## 6. Radar missile: AIM-7B Sparrow

**When**: a target ahead inside the Sparrow's short reach. **Keys**: radar on (**R**), lock (**Return** in LRS
(long-range search), or click its blip), **]** until the Sparrow, **Space** to fire.

Real data: 176 kg, Mach 2.5, 7 km (both uncertain, early-variant figures), **semi-active**: it needs your lock until
it hits.

![An AIM-7B with a radar lock on a MiG-23 5,000 m ahead](shots/f4/sight_mrm.png)

1. **Range bar** (missile scale).
2. **Target**.
3. **Pipper**.

**Example**: the MiG is 5,000 m (16,400 ft) ahead: the bar runs 4.5 hours, to about 1:30. The AIM-7B's zone against it runs from 1.4 to 5.7 km: fire inside 5.7 km and hold
the lock until the hit: **Q**, **R**, **S**, **Return**, a click or **Backspace** breaks the Sparrow's guidance.

## 7. Bombs and rockets

**When**: MK-82 / MK-83 / MK-84, the CBU-87 cluster and ZUNNI rockets. **Keys**: **[** to the store; hold **Space**
to release. The stores page sets the ripple (default 2 bombs, 10 m apart).

The real sight takes a manual depression from bombing tables. The game sets it for you: the reticle sits on the
predicted impact (a reconstruction).

![A 45° dive with MK-82s, the reticle on a tank](shots/f4/sight_bomb.png)

1. **Pipper** on the predicted impact: the tank sits under it.
2. **Outer ring**.
3. **Stores count** on the radar scope: 6 MK-82s.

**Example**: six MK-82s on the centreline, a 45° dive at 220 m/s from 700 m above the ground. Let the target slide
under the pipper and press **Space**. The game's training warns against GP (general-purpose) bombs below 2,000 ft
above the ground. When the impact lies below the sight's field, hold **Space** with the target under the pipper at
the edge; the bombs go at 0.9 s to go (positive g, less than 90° of bank).

## 8. AGM-45 Shrike

**When**: a radar site ahead. **Keys**: **[** to the Shrike brings up the HARM page on the radar scope;
**Ctrl+Return** steps to the next site; **Space** fires.

![The HARM page with a radar site in range](shots/f4/mfd_shrike.png)

1. **HARM**: the page.
2. **IN RANGE**: the selected site is inside the Shrike's range.
3. **Target**: the radar site's symbol.

**Example**: the Shrike (177 kg, Mach 2, 40 km) lists the radar sites within ±15° of the nose out to 20 NM, nearest
first. Turn until the site is near the sight's centre and fire: the Shrike chases its site to the end, emitting or
silent.

## 9. Flying the F-4E with Real flight data

With Flight data = Real the F-4E row comes from public figures (Jane's, T.O. (technical order) 1F-4E-1 limits, GE
engine data), checked by the game's validation test.

| Item | In the game | Real F-4E |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Empty weight | 30,330 lb | about 30,330 lb (Jane's) |
| Internal fuel | 12,060 lb | 1,855 gal = 12,060 lb (block 41+) |
| Thrust, military / full afterburner | 23,740 / 35,800 lbf | 2 × 11,870 / 2 × 17,900 lbf (J79-GE-17) |
| 1 g stall speed | 145 kt (the original's) | about 150–165 kt at 40,000 lb (uncertain) |
| g limits | +7.33 / −3 | +7.33 at 37,500 lb or less / −3 (T.O. 1F-4E-1) |
| Maximum roll rate | 150°/s (uncertain) | about 120–180°/s at 350–450 kt (uncertain) |
| Maximum speed, sea level / 40,000 ft | 780 kt (Mach 1.18) / 1,239 kt (Mach 2.16) | about 750 KIAS placard / Mach 2.2–2.23 |
| Climb at 350 kt, sea level | about 28,300 ft/min | 41,300 ft/min |
| Fuel flow, military / full afterburner | 20,000 / 71,000 lb/h | about 20,000 / 71,000 lb/h |
| Service ceiling | 58,750 ft | 58,750 ft at maximum power |

What changes in the cockpit:

- **Fuel**: about 10 minutes at full afterburner, about 36 minutes at military power. The 3,700 lb tanks go on
  stations 1, 5 and 9.
- **Mechanical flight controls**: the stick moves the surfaces directly, so you own the limits. At low speed a hard
  pull stalls the wings, and with Realistic spins on the jet can spin. Recover with the stick forward and the rudder
  against the spin, then pull out of the dive.
- **Flight-path hold** (Physics): with the stick centred the jet holds its flight path.
- **Two engines**: two sets of RPM, temperature and throttle needles on the panel.
- **Better cockpit** (Extras): the engine and fuel needles show the flight model's values (section 3).
- **Ground effect** (Physics): a longer float in the flare.
- **The map edge**: about 12 km (6.5 NM) before the map's edge the EndWorld warning sounds: turn back. Past it the
  game pushes the jet back for about 8 s: the nose rises and the jet turns back toward the map.

### Take-off and landing numbers

| Item | F-4E with Real flight data |
| --- | --- |
| Rotate | 145 kt |
| Gear up below | 300 kt |
| Downwind / final | 200 kt at 2,000 ft above the ground / 165 kt (the game's lessons) |
| 1 g stall | 145 kt |
| Touchdown limits (Real landing limits, Easy landing off) | sink below 4 m/s (790 ft/min), pitch below 15° |
| Drag chute (**Shift+B**) | real drag; deploy below 200 KIAS (the T.O. limit). From about 90 kt at idle it slows the jet about ten times faster than rolling alone |
| Nose-wheel steering | rudder pedals, ±70° (below about 70 kt on the real jet) |

The sight shows the reticle: fly the approach on the runway and the panel's gauges.

## 10. Radar and self-protection

With Weapon data = Real the AN/APQ-120 detects at **61 km** (33 NM, nautical miles). The radar scope is the jet's
one display; **T** brings up the TSD (tactical map) on it, **D** the damage report.

| Key | Action |
| --- | --- |
| **R** / **Q** / **S** | Radar on, A-A (air-to-air) ↔ A-G (air-to-ground) / mode (LRS → TWS → ACM; GMT ↔ MAP) / standby |
| **.** / **,** | Range scale up / down |
| **Return** / **Shift+Return** | Next / previous contact (in LRS this locks it: STT) |
| **Backspace** | Drop the lock |
| **\\** (hold) | Boresight: a ±12° lock cone straight ahead |

- **RWR** (radar warning receiver): a dial on the panel; a new threat sounds a tone, a missile launch at you the
  launch warning.
- **Chaff (Insert)** and **flares (Delete)**: 90 chaff and 60 flares. A flare fools an IR missile with 33 % (50 %
  above 4 g) while the afterburner is off.
- **ECM (J)** (electronic countermeasures): with the ECM pod on station 4 or 5. Each radar-guided missile already
  flying at you is jammed with a 60 % chance; SAMs (surface-to-air missiles) keep guiding.
- **AWACS calls** (AWACS: the airborne early-warning controller): from 60 s into the flight the controller calls the
  enemy aircraft within 35 NM, one scan every 12 s. "Alpha, bearing 090, 15 miles, target, heading 270, free to
  engage" gives the contact's bearing and distance from you, then the heading it flies; sometimes "targets are already
  engaged" takes the place of "free to engage". "Unidentified contacts, please identify" is a contact your radar has
  not seen yet: find it on the radar before you engage. A contact is called again after 60 s, or at once when it moved
  more than 5 NM; the repeat is short ("bearing 090, 15 miles, target"). The controller is silent while you fly less
  than 50 m (165 ft) above the ground.
- **Flight reports**: the radio also says when a jet of your side is airborne, has landed, crashed or ejected, and
  calls each kill 5 s after it ("Alpha leader shot down MiG 29").

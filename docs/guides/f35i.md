# F-35I pilot guide (realistic settings)

This guide covers what changes when you fly our **F-35I Adir** with the realistic options on. The F-35I is our addition
to the game (docs/f35i.md): an F-35A airframe with the F135-PW-100 engine in IAF (Israeli Air Force) markings (tail 937,
116 Squadron). Its HUD (head-up display) is the helmet display, and its panel is one panoramic display. Each helmet mode
has its own section: when to use it, the keys, a picture whose numbered yellow callouts the list under it explains, and
a worked example. **(U)** marks an estimate. Section 15 explains the abbreviations.

**To fly it**: Preferences → Extras → **F-35I replaces** → pick a Jet list button (the Lavi's, for example). That
button is relabelled F-35I (Hebrew אדיר) and flies the F-35I in every training and Jet list mission.

## 1. What's different in this jet

| Area | The 1998 symbology | The F-35I with the realistic options |
| --- | --- | --- |

| Field of view | The cockpit's HUD glass | The helmet's 40° × 30° field, drawn over the whole view, panel included |
| Looking aside | The 1998 helmet repeater | A head-stabilised set: a cross, the head's heading with the steering caret, speed, altitude, the steerpoint and the target |
| Missiles | A circle and a predicted point | The target designator (an X over a circle), the steering circle and dot, a DLZ (dynamic launch zone) bracket with the range |
| Gun and bombs | The 1998 pipper | The F-16's funnel and CCIP pipper in green: a reconstruction [R] |
| Panel | The slot jet's MFDs (multi-function displays) | One panoramic display with four portals |
| Weapons | Pylons | Weapon bays: bay stores show while the doors are open |
| Flight data | The slot's own jet | F-35A numbers (F135-PW-100): 18,250 lb internal fuel, 43,000 lbf in afterburner, always on |

![The 1998 symbology in the F-35I's helmet at the start of mission 231](shots/f35i/hud_original.png)

The same moment with the Real HUD is the first picture of section 3.

**Voice and HUD warnings.** With the 1998 HUD, Betty (the cockpit voice) calls "Pull up" every 4 s in the A-G gun and
bomb modes while the dive is steeper than 1° per 100 ft above the ground (20° at 2,000 ft), and "Altitude" every 4 s
below 100 ft with the gear up. The HUD draws a large X in the same dive, and in place of the missile circle when the
locked target is inside the missile's minimum range (Rmin). With the Real HUD: one "Pull up" when a 4 g pull-up after
the pilot's reaction only just clears the ground (the F-16's rule: a 45° dive at 413 kt calls at about 2,270 ft), no
"Altitude", and no X (the helmet's marks are not public).

## 3. NAV: the virtual HUD

**When**: cruising, navigating, between attacks. **Keys**: **N** returns to NAV, **W** / **Shift+W** pick the next /
previous steerpoint. The virtual HUD is fixed to the airframe: it shows while you look forward.

![The virtual HUD in NAV at 10,000 ft and 427 kt](shots/f35i/hud_nav.png)

1. **Heading**, boxed, on a tape at the top.
2. **Pitch ladder**: climb rungs solid with a plain number (`5`), dive rungs dashed with a minus (`-5`, `-10`).
3. **Horizon line**, across the whole field.
4. **Flight path marker**: where the jet is going.
5. **Waterline** (`-W-`): where the nose points.
6. **Airspeed**: calibrated airspeed in knots, a boxed number.
7. **GS**: ground speed in knots.
8. **M**: Mach number.
9. **G**: the load factor now.
10. **α**: angle of attack in degrees.
11. **Maximum g** of the flight.
12. **Altitude** above sea level in feet, a boxed number.
13. **Vertical velocity** in ft/min (0: level).
14. **Radar altitude**: `R9841` = 9,841 ft above the ground.
15. **Master mode**: `NAV`; `AA1` air-to-air, `AG1` air-to-ground.
16. **Steerpoint**: number, bearing / range: `001 270/4.7` = steerpoint 1, bearing 270°, 4.7 NM (nautical miles).
17. **Time to the steerpoint**, hours:minutes:seconds.

**Example**: at the start of mission 231 steerpoint 1 lies at 270°, your heading, 4.7 NM away: 35 s at 486 kt
ground speed. The flight path marker sits on the horizon and the vertical velocity reads 0: level flight. A bank
scale sits at the bottom of the field, over the panel.

### Finding the steerpoint

Two symbols show where the selected steerpoint is. Steer to put the caret under the boxed heading.

![NAV with steerpoint 2 at 046°, 10° right of the nose](shots/f35i/hud_nav_steer.png)

1. **Steering caret** (`^`): it sits under the heading tape at the steerpoint's bearing.
2. **Steerpoint square**: a small square on the steerpoint's spot on the ground.

When the steerpoint is outside the helmet's field, the square waits at the field's edge, on the line from the
waterline toward the steerpoint. When the bearing is off the heading tape (more than 17° either side), the caret
waits at the tape's end on that side.

![Steerpoint 2 40° to the left: the square and the caret wait at the left edge](shots/f35i/hud_nav_aside.png)

When the steerpoint is behind you, only the caret shows, at the end of the tape on the shorter way round.

![Steerpoint 1 behind: only the caret, at the left end of the tape](shots/f35i/hud_nav_behind.png)

## 4. Flying the F-35I

The F-35I row (docs/f35i.md §2) is checked by the game's validation test.

| Item | In the game | Real F-35A / F-35I |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Empty weight | 29,300 lb | 29,300 lb |
| Internal fuel | 18,250 lb | 18,250 lb |
| Thrust, military / full afterburner | 28,000 / 43,000 lbf | 28,000 / 43,000 lbf uninstalled (F135) |
| 1 g stall speed, sea level | 122 kt | about 120–125 kt (U) |
| g limits | +9 / −3 | +9 / −3 (U) |
| Maximum speed, sea level / 40,000 ft | 708 kt (Mach 1.07) / 905 kt (Mach 1.58) | about 700 kt / Mach 1.6 |
| Climb at 350 kt, sea level, full afterburner | about 32,700 ft/min | unpublished |
| Instantaneous turn, 420 kt at 10,000 ft | 21.1°/s at 6.9 g | unpublished |
| Fuel flow, military / full afterburner | 22,000 / 86,000 lb/h | about 20,000–25,000 / 86,000 lb/h (U) |
| Service ceiling | 50,000 ft | 50,000 ft |

- **Fuel**: about 13 minutes at full afterburner, about 50 minutes at military power at sea level: two to three times
  a 1998 jet's.
- **Fly-by-wire** and **Flight-path hold** (Physics): the F-16's flight controls; with the stick centred the jet holds
  its flight path.
- **Deep stall** (Physics: Realistic spins, Fly-by-wire deep stall): the F-16's, a stand-in from the closest public
  data. Zoom nose-high until the speed is gone and the jet hangs nose-high, sinking; recover by rocking the stick in
  phase with the nose.
- **Ground effect** (Physics): a longer float in the flare.
- **The stick on the keyboard** gives full deflection at once; a joystick is smoother.
- **Engine spool-up per real engine** (Physics): the thrust follows the F135's spool, slowest at first: 4 s from idle to
  military; the afterburner lights 0.8 s after the core reaches military and fills in 1.2 s. Out of afterburner the
  thrust drops at once. Pratt & Whitney gives about 6 s from flight idle to maximum power[^pw135]; the split between the
  core and the afterburner is inferred.
- **Wind and turbulence** (Physics): the air moves with the tower's wind. The jet crabs into a crosswind and drifts
  downwind, a headwind shortens the take-off run, and light gusts shake it, strongest below 300 m (1,000 ft). The AI
  jets fly in the same air, bombs drift downwind (the bombing computer allows for it: the impact pipper moves downwind),
  and smoke, fire and trails drift with it. With a wind of 5 kt or more from behind the main runway the tower turns the
  base to the runway's other end: the starts, the tower's calls, the taxi signs, the autopilot, the AI and the ILS use
  that end; training missions keep their runway. Gameplay → No wind calms the air.
- **Nose dips under braking** (Physics): the nose dips up to 2° under the wheel brakes and squats up to 1° at full
  thrust on the brakes; it levels within about a second once the jet stops.
- **Unbalanced stores roll the jet** (Physics): stores left on one wing roll the jet toward it: hold that wing up with
  the stick, trim it out (**Alt+Left / Right**, or the throttle rocker with Devices → Throttle rocker = Roll trim),
  or let the autopilot (**A**) hold it while it flies. One 2,000 lb bomb on an
  inboard wing pylon, 2.9 m out, takes about 11 % stick at 290 kt and 1 g; the bays' stores sit 0.5–1 m out and roll it
  far less. It grows with the g in a pull, and at half the speed it takes four times as much.
- **The map edge**: 12 km (6.5 NM) before it the EndWorld warning sounds: turn back. Past it the game pushes the jet
  back for about 8 s (the nose rises and the jet turns).

### Take-off and landing numbers

## 5. The panoramic display and the bays

One wide touchscreen laid out as the real jet's (20 × 8 in, two halves; docs/real-mfd.md): the Function Access
Button strip along the top, then four portals. Each portal shows one format; click its name for the MENU of formats,
or one of its two tabs to swap that format in. **T** brings up the TSD, **D** the cautions, **V** / **PgUp** / **PgDn**
slide the panel. Colours follow the real jet's rule on a black screen: green good or friend, yellow caution or
unknown, red warning or hostile, cyan for the options you can press, white for what is selected.

![The panoramic display](shots/f35i/panel.png)

1. **Engine and fuel**: the RPM dial (AB boxed in afterburner); total, internal and external fuel in thousands of
   lb with purple bars; JKR, the joker fuel.
2. **Weapons, gear and ICAWS**: the missile and bomb counts, chaff, flares and gun rounds; the jet's outline green
   with the gear down and its three leg lights; ICAWS (integrated caution, advisory and warning system) with the
   worst caution, in its colour.
3. **AP / AT, SWAP, radios, recorder, MENU**, then speed, altitude, Mach, the alerts (CABIN, GCAS, ALOW, yellow
   below 500 ft) and the time.
4. **Portal header**: the format's name over the master mode (NAV, A-A, A-S). Click it for the MENU of formats.
5. **TSD** (tactical situation display): the compass rose about your jet, heading up, its radius the range (the arrows
   on the left change it, MAP shows the coastline and borders); the route with the steerpoint; tracks as on the real
   jet: a red diamond hostile, a green circle friend, a white ring on the lock; SAM sites with their ranges in red.
6. **SRCH**, the radar (ASR in its air-to-ground modes): the scan as a fan, the tracks with their heading lines, the
   lock ringed with its range (NM), altitude (thousands of ft) and closure (kt) on top. The arrows change the range,
   MODE the radar mode.
7. **SMS** (stores): the jet from above with each station's store in green (the selected one boxed), the selected
   weapon and its count, chaff and flares, gun rounds.
8. **TWD** (threat warning display): each radar the RWR hears at its bearing, by its code (2, 3, 6, 8 SAMs, A for AAA
   radars, 29, 23 … fighters); red locking you, red circled and flashing for a launch, yellow searching (Extras RWR:
   Real).
9. **Tabs**: the two formats waiting under each portal; click one to swap it in.
10. **Standby display** (ESIS): attitude, airspeed, altitude, heading.
11. **Landing gear handle**: its red lamp lights while the gear moves.
12. **MASTER CAUTION** (amber) and **ENG FIRE** (red, blinking) lights.

The other formats, from the tabs or the MENU: **TSD-2 / TSD-3** (more TSDs, each with its own range), **TFLIR** (the
EOTS camera's picture, its zoom, laser and range), **EFI** (attitude, speed, altitude, Mach, g and heading),
**ICAWS** (every caution, warnings first), **FUEL** (total, internal, external, the joker and bingo marks) and **ENG**
(RPM, throttle, afterburner):

![Other formats](shots/f35i/panel_formats.png)

At night the labels glow green and floodlights light the panel:

![The cockpit at night](shots/f35i/panel_night.png)

| Store (the game's stand-in) | Stations |
| --- | --- |
| AMRAAM (AIM-120) | 2, 3, 4, 6, 7, 8 |
| AIM-9M (for the AIM-9X), Python 4 | 1, 9 |
| MK-84L / MK-83L laser bombs (for GBU-31 / GBU-32) | 2, 3, 7, 8 |
| MK-84 / MK-83 | 2, 3, 7, 8 |
| GBU-15 (TV; for SPICE 1000) | 2, 8 |

Stations 1 and 9 are the wingtips, 2 and 8 the inner-wing stations, **3 / 7 and 4 / 6 the weapon bays** (outboard /
inboard). Default load: 2 AMRAAMs in the inboard bays, 2 MK-84Ls in the outboard bays.

**Radar**: with Weapon data = Real the APG-81 detects at 148 km (U). **R** on / A-A ↔ A-G, **Q** mode, **Return**
lock, **Backspace** drop. **Self-protection**: 60 chaff (**Insert**) and 60 flares (**Delete**); a flare fools an IR
missile with 33 % (50 % above 4 g) while the afterburner is off.
With Extras → *RWR: Real* the RWR also shows the SAM and AAA radars searching you before they lock: a steady symbol and
the new-threat tone. Drop behind a ridge and the symbol goes.

- **AWACS calls**: from 60 s into the flight the airborne controller calls the enemy aircraft within 35 NM, one scan
  every 12 s. "Alpha, bearing 090, 15 miles, target, heading 270, free to engage": the contact's bearing and distance
  from you, then its heading ("targets are already engaged" in place of "free to engage").
  "Unidentified contacts, please identify": your radar has not seen it yet; find it before you engage. A contact is
  called again, in short, after 60 s or when it moved more than 5 NM. Below 50 m (165 ft) the controller is silent.
- **Flight reports**: the radio reports your side's jets airborne, landed, crashed or ejected, and each kill 5 s after
  it ("Alpha leader shot down MiG 29").

## 6. Looking aside: the head-stabilised set

**When**: a target off the nose. **Keys**: **Shift+arrows**, **Numpad** snap views, padlock **F3** (the view follows
the radar target). The virtual HUD stays with the airframe; a reduced set rides your head.

![Looking 40° left at a locked MiG-23](shots/f35i/head_set.png)

1. **Target designator**: an X over a circle on the radar target.
2. **Line-of-sight cross**: where your head points.
3. **Heading your head looks at**: 230°, 40° left of the jet's 270°.
4. **Airspeed**.
5. **Altitude**.
6. **Steerpoint**: number, bearing / range.
7. **Time to the steerpoint**.
8. **Steering caret**: the steerpoint's bearing on the head's heading tape, here held at the right end.

**Example**: a MiG-23 is 40° left, 3,000 m away. Lock it (**Return**), select the AIM-9M or Python 4 (**]**) and look
at it (**F3**): the helmet cues the seeker to the target you look at, inside the missile's cone (Python 4 60°,
AIM-9M 35°). Fire (**Space**) on the high tone.

## 8. SRM: AIM-9M, Python 4

**When**: a target inside about 10 NM. **Keys**: **]** until `AIM-9`, **Space** to fire. With a radar lock the
seeker is slaved to the locked target. Stations 1 and 9, the wingtips.

![An AIM-9M locked on a MiG-23 1.6 NM ahead](shots/f35i/hud_srm.png)

1. **Target designator** on the radar target.
2. **Seeker circle**: on what the seeker tracks.
3. **Flight path marker**.
4. **DLZ scale top**: 10 NM. The ticks below are the missile's Rmax and Rmin.
5. **Range caret** with the closure in knots.
6. **Range** to the target: 1.6 NM.
7. **Weapon**: `2 AIM-9`, with `AA1` above and `ARM` below.

| Missile | Real data |
| --- | --- |
| AIM-9M (for the AIM-9X) | 86 kg, Mach 2.5, 17.7 km |
| Python 4 | 120 kg, Mach 3.5, 15 km, 60° off the nose |

**Example**: the MiG hangs still 3,000 m ahead, 5° right; the closure is your own speed. The AIM-9M's zone runs from
16.1 km (Rmax) to 1.4 km (Rmin): the caret at 1.6 NM sits low on the 10 NM scale, just above Rmin. The
seeker circle sits on the designator and the tone rises: fire. Beyond half its range the seeker sees a jet with its
afterburner lit.

## 9. MRM: AMRAAM

**When**: beyond the IR missiles' reach. **Keys**: radar on (**R**), lock (**Return** in LRS (long-range search)),
**]** until `AIM-A`, **Space** to fire: the bay opens (0.5 s), the missile drops, the doors close 2 s later.

![An AMRAAM locked on a MiG-23 8 NM ahead](shots/f35i/hud_mrm.png)

1. **Target designator**.
2. **Steering dot**: steer it into the circle.
3. **Steering circle**: it grows as you close from Rmax toward Rmin.
4. **DLZ scale top**: 40 NM.
5. **Rmax**: the AMRAAM's maximum range against this target.
6. **Rmin**: its minimum range.
7. **Range caret** with the closure (`480>`).
8. **Range**: 8.0 NM.
9. **Weapon**: `2 AIM-A`, two AMRAAMs in the bays.

Real data: AMRAAM (AIM-120) 157 kg, Mach 4, 70 km, **active**: it guides itself after the launch.

**Example**: lock the MiG at 8 NM (14.8 km). Rmax reads 36 NM (67.3 km) against this still target and Rmin 1.2 NM
(2.2 km); the caret at 8 NM sits low on the 40 NM scale, well inside the zone. The dot sits up and right of the
circle: turn right and raise the nose until the dot drops into the circle, then fire.

## 10. A-A gun

**When**: a target inside about 3,000 ft. **Keys**: **]** to the gun (`GUN`), **Tab** fires. The game's 20 mm gun
stands in for the 25 mm GAU-22/A, with the real **180 rounds**: under 2 s of firing.

![The A-A gun locked on a MiG-23 at 2,600 ft](shots/f35i/hud_gun.png)

1. **Target designator** on the MiG.
2. **Funnel**: the bullets' path from 600 to 3,000 ft. Its width at any height is a fighter's wingspan at that range
   [R: the F-35's gun graphics are unpublished; this is the F-16's funnel].
3. **Weapon**: `GUN`.
4. **Master mode**: `AA1`.

**Example**: the MiG is 800 m (2,600 ft) ahead. Pull it into the funnel until its wings touch both lines and fire a
short burst: 180 rounds go fast.

## 11. Bombs: CCIP

**When**: MK-84 / MK-83 and the laser bombs (the game's stand-ins for GBU-31 / GBU-32). **Keys**: **[** to the bomb,
hold **Space**. The laser bombs need no pod: the built-in EOTS (electro-optical targeting system) gives the FLIR page
(**I**) and the laser (**L**). Slew the FLIR cross onto the target (**Ctrl+arrows**), press **L** and release: the bomb
flies to the spot. With the laser off it falls on the CCIP pipper.

![CCIP with an MK-84 in a 45° dive](shots/f35i/hud_bomb.png)

1. **CCIP pipper** (continuously computed impact point): where the bomb lands now [R: the F-16's].
2. **Bomb fall line** from the marker to the pipper.
3. **Flight path marker**.
4. **Ladder**: −45°, the dive angle.
5. **Vertical velocity**: −30,620 ft/min.
6. **Master mode**: `AG1`.
7. **Weapon**: `2 AG`, two A-G stores.

**Example**: a 45° dive at 409 kt, 2,953 ft above the ground, sinking 30,620 ft/min: about 6 s to the ground. Let the
fall line carry the target into the pipper, press **Space**, and pull out at once.

## 12. Landing

**When**: on final approach, gear handle down (**G**). **Keys**: **G** gear, **F** flaps, **B** wheel brakes, **Ctrl+T**
calls the tower near an Israeli base. The helmet's field reaches over the panel. The approach speeds and touchdown
limits are in section 4.

![Final approach at 6° AoA, 3.6 NM out](shots/f35i/landing_final.png)

1. **Flight path marker**: on the runway threshold. Keep it there.
2. **Waterline**: the nose, 6° above the marker (the AoA).
3. **ILS localizer line** (instrument landing system): it moves toward the runway's side of the centre line.
4. **ILS glide-slope line**: it drops when you are high. Both lines cross at the HUD's centre on the runway's centre
   line and a 5° path.
5. **α**: 6.0°.
6. **Vertical velocity**: −775 ft/min.
7. **Radar altitude**: about 700 ft.
8. **Airspeed**: 141 kt.

**Example**: here the jet flies a 3° path at 141 kt, sinking 775 ft/min. The glide-slope line above the centre says
you are under the 5° path. The real F-35A approaches at about 150 kt and 13° AoA (U): raise the nose until α reads
13 and hold the marker on the threshold. The ILS lines are in the helmet's green, like the rest of
its symbols.

## 13. Better cockpit

**When**: any flight.

The panoramic display draws its formats sharp, at the screen's resolution, with or without Better cockpit (section 5

## 14. Night vision

**When**: night missions (the game's night is 20:00–05:30; mission 214 starts at 01:00), with Graphics → MORE →
Night vision = Goggles. **Keys**: **Shift+N** puts the goggles on and takes them off.

The goggles are ours: the 1998 game has none. They turn the outside view into a brightened green picture; the
cockpit and the HUD stay as you see them under the goggles. Set Graphics → MORE → Night lights = Detailed too, for
the moon, the stars and the lights on the ground.

![01:00 without goggles](shots/f35i/night.png)

![The same moment through the goggles](shots/f35i/night_nvg.png)

- The ground's relief and texture show, and the horizon is clear: fly by the outside view.
- The goggles set their own gain: the darker the scene, the more they brighten it, and the grainier it gets.
- The HUD shows in its brightest green while the goggles are on; your own level comes back when they come off.
- A view full of light (a close explosion, daylight) darkens the goggles: take them off by day.
- The goggles follow the mission's year: none before 1972, a grainy Gen II up to 1981, a Gen III from 1982, the
  sharpest from 1996 (the 1998 missions).
- The F-35I has no goggles: its helmet shows a night camera's picture, drawn here as the newest goggles in any
  year.
- A bright light (a fire, a flare, a runway light) gets a halo around it.
- The picture darkens toward its edges.

# F-16I Sufa pilot guide (realistic settings)

This guide covers our **F-16I Sufa** ("Storm"), the IAF's (Israeli Air Force) two-seat F-16D Block 52+ with conformal
fuel tanks, flown with the realistic options on. The Sufa is our addition to the game (docs/f16i.md): the 1998 game has
no Sufa. It uses the F-16's cockpit and the F-16C HUD (head-up display), so this guide covers what differs from the
F-16D Barak and points to the [F-16 guide](f16.md) for the rest: the HUD modes, the DASH helmet, the gun, CCIP, the
laser, TV and HARM weapons. Each new weapon has its keys, a picture whose numbered yellow callouts the list under it
explains, and a worked example. Section 11 lists the keys and abbreviations.

**To fly it**: Preferences → Extras → **F-16 version** → **Sufa (F-16I)**. Every F-16 becomes an F-16I: yours, your
wingmen's, the enemy's and the parked ones, in every mission. With the switch on, the Reference screen's **PILOT
GUIDE** for the F-16 shows this guide; set the switch back to Barak (F-16D) to read the F-16 guide there.

## 1. What's different in this jet

| Area | F-16D Barak (realistic options) | F-16I Sufa |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Airframe | Two seats, the GE F110 nozzle | Two seats, conformal fuel tanks (CFTs) over the wing roots, the Pratt & Whitney nozzle |
| Engine | F110-GE-100: 17,400 / 29,000 lbf | F100-PW-229: 17,800 / 29,160 lbf |
| Fuel | 5,800 lb internal | 8,850 lb: 5,800 lb internal and 3,050 lb in the CFTs |
| Weight | 19,100 lb empty | 20,500 lb empty, 52,000 lb maximum |
| Radar | AN/APG-68: 80 km (43 NM) | AN/APG-68(V)9: 104 km (56 NM), 30 % further |
| IR missiles | AIM-9L / M, Python 3 / 4 | Also the **AIM-9X** and **Python 5**: 90° / 100° off the nose, lock-on after launch |
| Bombs | Free-fall, laser and TV weapons | Also the GPS bombs: **GBU-31**, **GBU-31V3**, **GBU-32**, **GBU-38** (JDAM) and the **GBU-39** |
| HUD and helmet | The F-16C HUD, the DASH | The same F-16C HUD and DASH; the Sufa's own HUD software and DASH IV symbols: open |
| Flight data | Original or Real | Always the Sufa's real numbers, whatever Flight data says |

![The F-16I Sufa: the conformal fuel tanks over the wing roots, 107 Squadron's orange fin band](shots/f16i/external.png)

The CFTs carry no stores: "nothing is suspended from them"[^lm-cft01]. They free stations 4 and 6 from wing tanks, so
those take bombs.

**Voice and HUD warnings**: as on the F-16 ([F-16 guide §1](f16.md#1-whats-different-in-this-jet)).

## 4. Flying the F-16I

These are the game's values, checked by its validation test (the F-16I row) against public figures. The F-16D column
is the F-16 with Flight data = Real ([F-16 guide §4](f16.md#4-flying-the-f-16-with-real-flight-data)).

| Item | F-16I in the game | F-16D in the game |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Empty weight | 20,500 lb (with 900 lb of empty CFTs) | 19,100 lb |
| Internal fuel | 8,850 lb | 5,800 lb |
| Weight with full fuel, no stores | 29,350 lb | 24,900 lb |
| Thrust, military / full afterburner | 17,800 / 29,160 lbf | 17,400 / 29,000 lbf |
| 1 g stall speed, sea level, full fuel | 128 kt | 118 kt |
| Corner speed (9 g), full fuel | about 385 kt (inferred: 3 × the stall speed) | about 355 kt |
| g limit | 9 g | 9 g |
| Maximum roll rate | 280°/s (0 to 90° in 0.55 s) | 280°/s |
| Maximum speed, sea level / 40,000 ft | 782 kt (Mach 1.18) / 1,075 kt (Mach 1.87) | 782 kt / 1,074 kt |
| Climb at 350 kt, sea level, full afterburner | about 36,000 ft/min | about 43,000 ft/min |
| Instantaneous turn, 420 kt at 10,000 ft | 21.7°/s at 7.8 g | 21.7°/s at 8.1 g |
| Fuel flow, military / full afterburner | 13,500 / 56,600 lb/h | 11,000 / 59,400 lb/h |
| Service ceiling | 50,000 ft | 50,000 ft |

The Sufa's numbers and their sources: docs/f16i.md §1. The CFTs keep the full 9 g and flight envelope[^lm-cft01] and
add less than 1 % drag at cruise[^lm-cft01].

What this means in the cockpit:

- **Fuel**: about 9 minutes at full afterburner and about 39 minutes at military power at sea level (the F-16D: 6 and
  31). You can fight longer, or fly further to the target.
- **Weight**: at full fuel the Sufa is about 18 % heavier for the same thrust. It climbs about 16 % slower, accelerates
  more slowly, and turns hardest about 30 kt faster than the F-16D. As the fuel burns down it comes closer to the
  F-16D.
- **Turns**: fight near 385 kt with full fuel; near 360 kt with little fuel left (inferred).
- **Top speed**: the same as the F-16D's. It takes longer to get there.
- **Unbalanced stores roll the jet** (Physics): stores left on one wing roll the jet toward it: hold that wing up with
  the stick, trim it out (**Alt+Left / Right**, or the throttle rocker with Devices → Throttle rocker = Roll trim), or
  let the autopilot (**A**) hold it while it flies. One GBU-31 on station 3 (2.98 m out): hands off, the flight
  controls feed the roll rate back and slow the drift to about 19°/s toward the heavy wing. At 290 kt and 1 g about
  10 % stick holds it; full rocker trims that in about 1 s. It grows with the g in a pull (four times the stick at 4 g)
  and as the speed falls: on a 165 kt final it takes about 33 % stick and the drift is 32°/s. The trim reaches its
  limit, 22 % of the stick, in 2 s: hold the rest, about 11 %, with the stick.
  The release order keeps a symmetric load balanced: after a release the next store of that weapon comes from the
  station farthest from the last one, and the first press takes the selected station, usually the left wing's outer
  one. In pairs (the stores page's ripple quantity 2, the default) the bombs go left + right, a fraction of a second
  apart: level after every pair. Single drops leave the jet unbalanced after every odd one, and the next press takes
  the other wing. When the load is even again the roll stops at once, but the trim stays where
  you set it: take it back off (**Alt** + the other arrow, or the rocker) until the `TRIM L / R` reading on the
  **Ctrl+F12** info line is gone.
- **Bomb release: Real** (Extras): bombs leave the way the real stores management releases them. Stores page OSB 7
  (bottom row, second from left) switches PAIR / SGL; the page shows it as `1 PAIR` or `1 SGL`. The number is the
  release pulses per press and starts at 1. In PAIR each pulse drops one bomb off each wing at the same instant,
  outboard pair first, so the jet stays balanced: six bombs on six stations go in three presses, or set 3 to drop them
  all in one. In SGL each pulse drops one bomb, alternating wings, left first. Rockets work as before.
- **Everything else** is the F-16's: the flight-path hold, the deep stall and its recovery, ground effect, the engine
  spool-up, the wind and the brakes ([F-16 guide §4](f16.md#4-flying-the-f-16-with-real-flight-data)).

### Take-off and landing numbers

| Item | F-16I |
| --- | --- |
| Rotate | about 155 kt at full fuel (inferred: the F-16's 145 kt scaled by the weight) |
| Gear up below | 300 kt |
| Downwind / final | 200 kt at 2,000 ft above the ground / about 170 kt with 2,000 lb of fuel left (inferred) |
| 1 g stall, sea level, full fuel | 128 kt |
| Touchdown limits (Real landing limits, Easy landing off) | sink below 4 m/s (790 ft/min), pitch below 15° |
| Drag chute (**Shift+B**) | drawn, with zero drag |
| Nose-wheel steering | rudder pedals, ±32° |

On final, fly the AoA (angle of attack): the donut on the indexer and 13° in the HUD's bracket. It gives the right
speed for the jet's weight.

## 5. Radar and self-protection

The Sufa's AN/APG-68(V)9 sees 30 % further than the F-16D's radar[^lm-radar]. The keys, the RWR, chaff, flares, ECM
and the AWACS calls are the F-16's ([F-16 guide §5](f16.md#5-radar-and-self-protection)).

| Mode | F-16I | F-16D |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Detection, Weapon data = Real (LRS and STT) | 104 km (56 NM) | 80 km (43 NM) |
| LRS detection, Weapon data = Original | 58 NM | 45 NM |
| TWS | 45 NM | 35 NM |
| ACM, BORE | 10 NM | 10 NM |
| MAP / GMT | 40 / 30 NM | 40 / 30 NM |

![The radar locked on a MiG-23 51 NM ahead](shots/f16i/mfd_radar.png)

1. **The locked target**, 51 NM ahead (the HUD shows `F51.3`).
2. **Range scale**: 80 NM.
3. **Mode**: STT (single-target track).
4. **Closure**: 486 kt, your own speed against this still MiG.

**Example**: a MiG-23 flies 95 km (51 NM) ahead, beyond the F-16D radar's 80 km. The Sufa's radar locks it
(**Return** in LRS). The radar range figures for the (V)9 are not public in nautical miles: the game takes the
F-16D's × 1.3 (inferred). An enemy AI F-16I sees you from 52 NM, not 40 NM.

### The real MFDs

The Sufa always has its colour MFDs, whatever Extras **MFDs (F-16)** says. Each MFD then shows the real F-16's formats in place of the 1998 pages:

| Format | Shows | Stands for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| FCR | the radar: targets, the lock's data line, the launch zone; GM / GMT on the ground | the radar page |
| SMS | the stores: the inventory (OSB INV) or the selected weapon's page | the stores page |
| HSD | the route, the SAM rings and the radar's scan around your jet, over a colour map | the TSD |
| WPN | the Maverick's picture, the HARM's threats, a GPS bomb's target and times | the TV and HARM pages |
| TGP | the targeting pod's picture | the FLIR page |
| TEST | the failed systems | the damage page |

- **The bottom row** of every MFD: SWAP (trade the two MFDs), the MFD's three formats (the one shown in a box: click
  another to show it, click the boxed one for the menu of all formats), and DCLT (hide the labels).
- **Ranges**: the triangles at the top left step the FCR's and the HSD's range (15, 30, 60, 120 NM).
- **GPS bombs**: on the SMS and WPN, the button labelled PRE / VIS switches the mode (as K does); the target shows as
  STPT and its number, with its range and bearing, TMR or IN RNG, and TTI after the release.
- The waypoint list, the RWR page and the ADI page are not offered: the real F-16 shows them elsewhere.

- **Colours**: tracks and threats yellow (red when you are inside a SAM ring), friends green, your jet and the radar's
  scan cyan, the route and the text white.

The older weapons fly as on the F-16D: the SRMs (AIM-9L / M, Python 3 / 4), the AMRAAM and AIM-7M, the gun (511

### AIM-9X and Python 5

These two need Extras → Weapon data = Real: with Original the Arming screen leaves them out.

**When**: a target well off the nose, or one the seeker cannot lock yet. **Keys**: **]** until `SRM`, look at the
target (**Shift+arrows**, the **Numpad** snap views, or **F3** padlock: it follows only a radar target, inside the
radar's ±60°), **Space** to fire.

| Missile | Real data | Off the nose | Seeker lock: from behind / head-on / head-on against an afterburner |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| AIM-9X Block II | 85 kg, Mach 2.5, 17.7 km | 90° | 16 / 4.5 / 12.7 km |
| Python 5 | 105 kg, Mach 4, over 20 km | 100° | 20 / 6 / 16 km |

The AIM-9X sees 90° off boresight and turns at 60 g[^wiki-9]; the Python 5 locks after launch up to 100° off
boresight[^python]. Their seeker lock ranges are not public: the game uses the Python 4's for the AIM-9X and 1.25 ×
that for the Python 5 (inferred).

They fly like the other SRMs, with three differences:

1. **Wide seeker**: the seeker follows your head (the DASH) or a radar lock up to 90° (AIM-9X) or 100° (Python 5) off
   the nose. The radar locks only inside ±60°, so beyond that, look at the target.
2. **Turn off the rail**: the missile steers at once and turns hard toward a target beside or behind your wing line.
3. **Lock-on after launch** (LOAL): fire without a seeker lock and the missile flies along your line of sight (where
   you look, or the nose), then locks the first target near that line that its seeker can lock.

![Looking 70° right at a MiG-23 with the AIM-9X](shots/f16i/dash_9x.png)

1. **Aiming cross**: where your head points.
2. **Seeker diamond**: the AIM-9X's seeker on the MiG.
3. **Heading your head looks at**: 340°, 70° right of the jet's 270°.
4. **ARM**: weapons live.
5. **Weapon**: two short-range missiles.

**Example: off the nose.** A MiG-23 is 70° right, 3,000 m away, outside the radar's ±60°. Select the AIM-9X (**]**)
and look right (**Shift+Right**, or a **Numpad** snap view and then **Shift+Right**). The diamond settles on the MiG and
the tone rises. Fire (**Space**): the missile turns hard right off the rail and hits. No radar lock is needed.
With HUD = Real, **SHOOT** shows under the cross once the seeker locks in range; looking past 90° (100° for the Python
5) puts the diamond on the field's edge with an X over it: the seeker has stopped at its limit.

**Example: lock-on after launch.** A MiG-29 comes at you head-on, 9 km ahead, its afterburner off. The Python 5's
seeker locks a head-on target only inside 6 km, so there is no tone. Put the nose on the MiG and fire: the Python 5
flies along the line, and once it is inside 6 km of the MiG it locks and chases it. While it searches the DASH
flashes **LOAL** under the cross.

### JDAM and GBU-39: GPS bombs

**When**: a fixed ground target, from a safe height or distance, in any weather. **Keys**: **[** until the bomb;
**K** PRE ↔ VIS, **Shift+K** the next PRE target; **Space** releases.

| Bomb | Weight | Reach from 10,000 ft / 30,000 ft / 40,000 ft (486 kt, Mach 0.9 at 40,000 ft) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| GBU-31 (Mk 84) | 946 kg | 8 / 18 / 23 km |
| GBU-31V3 (BLU-109 penetrator) | 981 kg | as the GBU-31 |
| GBU-32 (Mk 83) | 468 kg | as the GBU-31 |
| GBU-38 (Mk 82) | 253 kg | as the GBU-31 |
| GBU-39 (SDB, with wings) | 129 kg each | 39 / 85 / 111 km |

The weights are the real bombs'[^dsjdam][^sdb]. The reaches are the game's: a JDAM glides 1.6 m for each metre it
loses, the GBU-39 7.6 m, set so that the reaches from 40,000 ft match the published 24 km and "over 60 nmi" (inferred
conditions)[^dsjdam][^sdb]. The reach is from the height above the target: the more height and speed you bring, the
further it goes.

A GPS bomb has no seeker and never follows a laser spot. It flies to the coordinates it gets when it leaves the
jet. **K** switches the selected bomb between two ways of giving them; a subtitle shows the new mode:

- **PRE** (pre-planned, the default): each station's target, set on the briefing TSD before the flight (below). A
  station without one takes the route's target waypoint. **Shift+K** steps through the targets: the station's own,
  then the other stations' and the route's steerpoints, each once; a subtitle shows `PRE 2/5`.
- **VIS** (visual): a point you pick in flight with the radar or the FLIR pod (below).

With no point (PRE without a target, VIS without a designation) the bomb falls on the CCIP pipper's point.

### VIS: a target you pick in flight

In VIS you pick the target in flight with a sensor. At the moment you release, the bomb takes that point's coordinates
and flies there. After the release it follows neither the radar nor the laser: you can turn away, drop the radar point
or switch the laser off.

**With the radar** (no pod needed):
1. Select the GPS bomb (**[**).
2. Press **K** until the weapon window (the last line of the HUD's lower-left column) reads `VIS`.
3. Put the radar in air-to-ground: press **R** until the radar page shows GMT or MAP, then **Q** until it shows MAP.
4. Click the target's spot on the MAP page, off the contacts (a double-click on a contact locks it instead): the HUD's
   TD box jumps onto it and the steering line runs through it.
5. Put the flight path marker on the steering line.
6. When `IN RNG` shows, press **Space**.

**Backspace** clears the point.

**With the FLIR pod** (on station 3):
1. Select the GPS bomb and press **K** for `VIS`, as above. Selecting a GPS bomb opens the stores page, not the FLIR
   pod.
2. Press **I**: the FLIR page and the pod's camera (the pod: [F-16 guide
   §12](f16.md#12-laser-bombs-tv-weapons-and-harm)).
3. Slew the cross onto the target with **Ctrl+arrows** (**Cmd+arrows** on a Mac); **Numpad + / −** zooms.
4. Press **L**, laser on: the FLIR pod's point now wins over the radar's, and the TD box moves onto it. The point counts
   only on the ground: a cross on the sky gives none.
5. Steer and release as above. You may switch the laser off once the bomb has left.

**Which point the bomb takes**: the FLIR pod's point with the laser on, else the radar's designated point, else the CCIP
pipper's point (then no TD box: the CCIP symbols show).

**Before you release, check** that the weapon window (the last line of the HUD's lower-left column) reads `VIS` and that
the TD box sits on your target.

### Setting the PRE targets on the briefing TSD

![The briefing TSD's GPS targets panel: station 6's row armed](shots/f16i/tsd_gps.png)

1. **Panel header**: `GPS TARGETS`; with a row armed it asks for the target (`CLICK A TARGET FOR STA 6`).
2. **Armed row**, boxed in yellow: the next click on the map sets its target.
3. **Default target**: `WP3 (TGT WP)`, the route's target waypoint, for a station you have not set.
4. **A unit as the target**: stations 3, 4 and 6 aim at the tank, which stands on the target waypoint.
5. **A map point as the target**: station 7 (`PT`).

The panel sits in the TSD map's lower-right corner while your flight carries GPS bombs (the Arming screen's load).
Click a station's row, then click the map: a unit takes its type name (`Tank`), a waypoint of your flight its number
(`WP4`), anywhere else the point itself (`PT`). Click the armed row again to disarm it; right-click a row to put the
station back on the default. A GBU-39 rack's row also opens a row per bomb (`STA 3-1` .. `STA 3-4`, bomb 1 the first
to leave): the station's row sets all four, a bomb's row that bomb only, so bombs 1–2 can go to one target and 3–4 to
another. Each target is a yellow box on the map with the stations (or bombs, `3-1,3-2`) aimed at it.
The targets are kept until a mission loads.

### GPS bombs on the HUD

![A GBU-31 in PRE 18 km from its target: the LAR staple and the time to max range](shots/f16i/hud_jdam.png)

1. **TD box**: the bomb's target. Outside the HUD's view it sticks to the edge with an X over it.
2. **Azimuth steering line** through the target: put the flight path marker on it.
3. **Flight path marker**.
4. **LAR staple** (launch acceptability region): the ranges the bomb can reach, Rmin at its bottom, Rmax at its top.
5. **Range caret**: your ground distance to the target, 9.7 NM.
6. **Scale**: 10 NM at the top, 0 at the bottom.
7. **TMR 0:45**: the time to max range at your closing speed; inside the LAR it reads `IN RNG`.
8. **PRE**: the mode, in the weapon window.

There is no single release point: release anywhere while the caret sits inside the staple, without waiting for a
cue. The bomb leaves at once when you press **Space** and turns toward the target at up to 3 g, so the target can lie
off your track. The CCIP pipper is not drawn while the bomb has a target. The F-16's JDAM symbols come from
simulators' manuals; the game's layout is inferred (docs/real-hud.md "GPS bombs").

- **Rmax** is 90 % of the bomb's reach, less the arc of the turn toward a target off your track.
- **Rmin**: the bomb can't turn down steeply enough onto a target right below it. From 3,000 m at 486 kt it is about
  1.9 km; from twice the turn radius up it is 0.
- **HUD = Original**: a diamond on the target, a line from the flight path marker to it, a dot in the diamond and
  `IN RNG` (row 5) inside the LAR, and the range to the target in nautical miles (`R 3.2`, row 3).
- **After the release**: `TTI m:ss` counts down the last released bomb's time to impact until it bursts, in the right
  column's time row (HUD = Original: a fourth row under the right text column). To watch the bomb from outside, press
  **F11** (weapon view): it follows the bomb down; **F1** brings back the cockpit.

**Example: a JDAM in PRE.** The picture: a GBU-31's PRE target lies 18 km ahead, 4° right. You dive gently at 486 kt
(427 kt calibrated) from 10,050 ft. The reach from here is about 8 km, so Rmax is about 7 km (3.9 NM): the caret at
9.7 NM sits above the staple and `TMR 0:45` counts down. Put the flight path marker on the steering line and hold the
dive; at `IN RNG` press **Space**: the GBU-31 glides onto the target. Pull out and turn away; the bomb needs neither
the radar nor you any more.

**Example: a target of opportunity.** A SAM site you had not planned for shows up 6 km ahead. Press **K** until the
weapon window reads `VIS`; radar **R** to air-to-ground, **Q** to MAP; click the site on the MAP page: the TD box
jumps onto it. Put the flight path marker on the steering line and at `IN RNG` press **Space**. With the pod instead:
**I**, the cross on the site, **L**: the bomb takes the pod's point.

**Example: a GBU-39 from stand-off range.** A radar site lies 40 NM (74 km) ahead, outside the SAMs' reach. Set it
as the GBU-39 station's target on the briefing TSD. Climb to 30,000 ft at 486 kt: the GBU-39 reaches 85 km, Rmax
76 km. When the caret drops into the staple and `IN RNG` shows, release the four bombs of one rack (the stores page
sets the ripple), and turn away while they glide for about five minutes.

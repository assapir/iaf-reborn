# F-15 pilot guide (realistic settings)

This guide covers what changes in the F-15C Baz when you switch on the realistic options: the HUD (head-up display) of
the real F-15A/C, the DASH helmet sight, the real jet's numbers and the real weapons. Each HUD mode has its own section:
when to use it, the keys, a picture whose numbered yellow callouts the list under it explains, and a worked example. The
numbers come from the game's own data and tests. Section 14 explains the abbreviations.

---

## 1. What's different in this jet

| Area | The 1998 game | With the realistic options |
| --- | --- | --- |
| HUD | One simplified HUD for every jet: boxed speed and altitude, a heading tape at the top, three text rows each side | The F-15A/C HUD from the flight manual (TO 1F-15A-1): moving tapes read at fixed carets, airspeed increasing downward, the `-W-` aircraft symbol, a three-line NAV window |
| Gun sight | A lead-computing pipper with a range arc | The F-15's **LCOS** (lead-computing optical sight): a ring with 12 ticks, a dashed inner circle, a pipper and a thick range arc |
| IR missiles | A circle and a seeker cue | A 60 mr missile circle, the seeker circle, the range scale with **IN RNG** |
| Radar missiles | A circle and a predicted point | The **ASE** (allowable steering error) circle, the steering dot, the range scale with MAX / MIN ticks, **IN RNG** and the breakaway X |
| Bombs | A pipper circle with a fall line | The F-15A/C's bomb fall line from the velocity vector to a target square |
| Looking aside | The original's helmet repeater | The Elbit **DASH** helmet display: aim a Python 4 by turning your head |
| Flight data | The game's own F-15 | F-15C (2 × F100-PW-220) from the USAF chart: 13,455 lb internal fuel, 180°/s roll, 130 kt stall |
| Weapon data | The game's own values | APG-63 radar out to 135 km, 940 gun rounds at 6,000 rounds/min, real missile weights, speeds and ranges |
| Physics | The original's quirks: still air, instant thrust | Real touchdown limits, ground effect, spins with mechanical flight controls, a flight-path hold with the stick centred, the tower's wind, the engine's real spool-up |

![The 1998 HUD at the start of mission 231](shots/f15/hud_original.png)

The same moment with the Real HUD is the first picture of section 3.

**Voice and HUD warnings.** With the 1998 HUD, Betty (the cockpit voice) calls "Pull up" every 4 s in the A-G gun and
bomb modes while the dive is steeper than 1° per 100 ft above the ground (20° at 2,000 ft), and "Altitude" every 4 s
below 100 ft with the gear up. The HUD draws a large X in the same dive, and in place of the missile circle when the
locked target is inside the missile's minimum range (Rmin). With the Real HUD: the F-15's breakaway X inside Rmin (SRM /
MRM), and no voice for the dive or the height (the real F-15C's voice set has none in the sources).

## 3. NAV: reading the HUD

**When**: cruising, navigating to a steerpoint, and between attacks. **Keys**: **N** returns to NAV from any weapon
mode. **W** / **Shift+W** pick the next / previous steerpoint. **H** changes the HUD colour.

The F-15A/C HUD reads every value off a moving tape against a fixed caret.

![NAV at 10,000 ft, steerpoint 1 ahead](shots/f15/hud_nav.png)

1. **Airspeed caret** on the airspeed tape. The tape increases **downward** (350 at the top, 500 at the bottom) and
   shows calibrated airspeed in knots; here about 427 kt.
2. **Altitude caret** on the altitude tape: barometric altitude above sea level, increasing upward, labels every
   500 ft. This tape is the HUD's one altitude.
3. **Heading caret** under the heading scale: a 30° window with two-digit labels (`27` = 270°).
4. **Bank steering bar**: a vertical line beside the aircraft symbol. Bank toward it to fly to the steerpoint; on the
   symbol, the steerpoint is straight ahead.
5. **Aircraft symbol** (`-W-`): where the nose points.
6. **Velocity vector**: where the jet is going (a circle with wings and a fin).
7. **Horizon line**.
8. **Pitch ladder**: 5° rungs, climb rungs solid, dive rungs dashed (this one is 5° below the horizon). A ⊖ "90" marks
   straight up, a ⊗ straight down.
9. **G**: the load factor.
10. **Steerpoint and mode**: steerpoint 1, `NAV`.
11. **Range** to the steerpoint: `N 4.7` = 4.7 NM (nautical miles).
12. **Time to go**, in whole minutes.

**Example**: at the start of mission 231 steerpoint 1 lies 4.7 NM ahead, about 1 minute away at 427 kt. The steering
bar sits on the aircraft symbol: hold the heading. When the bar moves right, bank right until it comes back.

## 4. Flying the F-15 with Real flight data

With Flight data = Real the F-15C row comes from the USAF Standard Aircraft Characteristics chart ("F-15C Eagle, two
F100-PW-220", Feb 1992), checked by the game's validation test.

| Item | In the game | Real F-15C |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Empty weight | 28,476 lb | 28,476 lb |
| Internal fuel | 13,455 lb | 13,455 lb (JP-4) |
| Thrust, military / full afterburner | 28,740 / 46,900 lbf | 2 × 14,370 / 2 × 23,450 lbf |
| 1 g stall speed | 130 kt (full internal fuel) | 135 kt at 45,713 lb, power off |
| g limits | +9 / −3 | +7.33 at 37,400 lb, up to +9 light; −3 |
| Maximum roll rate | 180°/s | about 180–220°/s |
| Maximum speed, sea level | 805 kt | about 800 KCAS |
| Maximum speed, 40,000 ft | 1,238 kt (Mach 2.16) | 1,309 kt (Mach 2.28) at 35,000 ft |
| Climb at 350 kt, sea level | about 42,000 ft/min | 55,960 ft/min at 41,286 lb |
| Fuel flow, military / full afterburner | 21,000 / 98,000 lb/h | about 21,000 / about 98,000 lb/h |
| Service ceiling | 65,000 ft | 65,000 ft |

What changes in the cockpit:

- **Fuel**: about 8 minutes at full afterburner, about 38 minutes at military power. The 600-gallon tanks
  ("4000 LB") go on stations 2, 5 and 8.
- **Mechanical flight controls**: the stick moves the surfaces directly, so the limits are yours. At low speed a hard
  pull stalls the wings, and with Realistic spins on the jet can spin. **Recovery**: stick forward, rudder against the
  spin, then pull out of the dive. The Fly-by-wire deep stall switch acts on the fly-by-wire jets (F-16, Lavi, F-35I).
- **Flight-path hold** (Physics): with the stick centred the jet holds its flight path.
- **Ground effect** (Physics): a longer float in the flare. **Real idle thrust on the ground**: at idle the jet taxis at
  about 35–45 kt.
- **The stick on the keyboard** gives full deflection at once, as in the original; a joystick is smoother.
- **Engine spool-up per real engine** (Physics): the thrust follows the F100's spool, slowest at first: 3 s from idle to
  military; the afterburner lights 0.5 s after the core reaches military and fills in 2 s. Out of afterburner the thrust
  drops at once. NASA measured 3 s from idle to intermediate on the F-15's F100[^tm4320]; its afterburner holds each
  segment 1 s[^tm85902].
- **Wind and turbulence** (Physics): the air moves with the tower's wind. The jet crabs into a crosswind and drifts
  downwind, a headwind shortens the take-off run, and light gusts shake it, strongest below 300 m (1,000 ft). The AI
  jets fly in the same air, bombs drift downwind (the bombing computer allows for it: the impact pipper moves downwind),
  and smoke, fire and trails drift with it. With a wind of 5 kt or more from behind the main runway the tower turns the
  base to the runway's other end: the starts, the tower's calls, the taxi signs, the autopilot, the AI and the ILS use
  that end; training missions keep their runway. Gameplay → No wind calms the air.
- **Nose dips under braking** (Physics): the nose dips up to 2° under the wheel brakes and squats up to 1° at full
  thrust on the brakes; it levels within about a second once the jet stops.
- **Unbalanced stores roll the jet** (Physics): stores left on one wing roll the jet toward it: hold that wing up with
  the stick, trim it out (**Alt+Left / Right**, or the throttle rocker with Devices → Throttle rocker = Roll trim), or
  let the autopilot (**A**) hold it while it flies. One 2,000 lb bomb on a wing pylon, 3 m out: hands off the jet drifts
  toward it at about 10°/s; the stick moves the ailerons and differential tail directly, so the stick that holds it is
  the bomb's share of their power. At 290 kt and 1 g about 9 % stick holds it; full rocker trims that in 1 s. It grows
  with the g in a pull (four times the stick at 4 g) and as the speed falls: on a 150 kt final it takes 25 % stick, the
  drift is 14°/s and the trim takes 2.5 s. The big wing hardly feels it. The release order keeps a symmetric load
  balanced: after a release the next store of that weapon comes from the station farthest from the last one, and the
  first press takes the selected station, usually the left wing's outer one. In pairs (the stores page's ripple quantity
  2, the default) three bombs per wing go left outer + right outer, left middle + right middle, left inner + right
  inner, a fraction of a second apart: level after every pair. Single drops leave the jet unbalanced after every odd
  one, and the next press takes the other wing. When the load is even again the roll stops at once, but the trim stays
  where you set it: take it back off (**Alt** + the other arrow, or the rocker) until the `TRIM L / R` reading on the
  **Ctrl+F12** info line is gone.
- **Bomb release: Real** (Extras): bombs leave the way the real stores management releases them. Stores page OSB 7
  (bottom row, second from left) switches PAIR / SGL; the page shows it as `1 PAIR` or `1 SGL`. The number is the
  release pulses per press and starts at 1. In PAIR each pulse drops one bomb off each wing at the same instant,
  outboard pair first, so the jet stays balanced: six bombs on six stations go in three presses, or set 3 to drop
  them all in one. In SGL each pulse drops one bomb, alternating wings, left first. Rockets work as before.
  This jet has no published release-system source, so it follows the F-16's rules.
- **The map edge**: 12 km (6.5 NM) before it the EndWorld warning sounds: turn back. Past it the game pushes the jet
  back for about 8 s (the nose rises and the jet turns).

| Item | F-15 with Real flight data |

## 5. Radar, displays and self-protection

With Weapon data = Real the AN/APG-63 detects at **135 km** (73 NM), targets from 30 m above the ground, in line of
sight. The keys match every jet: **R** radar on (A-A ↔ A-G), **Q** mode (LRS → TWS → ACM), **S** standby, **.** /
**,** range scale, **Return** next contact (locks in LRS), **Backspace** drop the lock, **\\** (hold) boresight.

- **Displays**: the left one starts on the radar, the right one on the RWR (radar warning receiver), the middle one
  on the TSD (tactical map). **T** the TSD, **D** the damage report, **V** / **PgUp** / **PgDn** slide the panel.
- **RWR** (radar warning receiver): the RWR page shows emitters and missiles around you; a new threat sounds a tone, a
  missile launch at you the launch warning. With Extras → *RWR: Real* it also shows the SAM and AAA radars searching you
  before they lock: a steady symbol and the new-threat tone. Drop behind a ridge and the symbol goes.
- **Chaff (Insert)** and **flares (Delete)**: 120 chaff and 75 flares. A flare fools an IR missile with 33 % (50 %
  above 4 g) while the afterburner is off: pull hard, come out of afterburner, then flare.
- **ECM (J)** (electronic countermeasures) works with the **ECM pod** on station 5: each radar-guided missile already
  flying at you is jammed with a 60 % chance. SAMs (surface-to-air missiles) keep guiding.
- **AWACS calls**: from 60 s into the flight the airborne controller calls the enemy aircraft within 35 NM, one scan
  every 12 s. "Alpha, bearing 090, 15 miles, target, heading 270, free to engage": the contact's bearing and distance
  from you, then its heading ("targets are already engaged" in place of "free to engage").
  "Unidentified contacts, please identify": your radar has not seen it yet; find it before you engage. A contact is
  called again, in short, after 60 s or when it moved more than 5 NM. Below 50 m (165 ft) the controller is silent.
- **Flight reports**: the radio reports your side's jets airborne, landed, crashed or ejected, and each kill 5 s after
  it ("Alpha leader shot down MiG 29").

What the F-15 can load on the Arming screen (stations 1 and 9 the wing pylons, 3, 4, 6, 7 the fuselage, 5 the
centreline):

| Store | Stations |
| --- | --- |
| AIM-9L / AIM-9M, Python 3 / Python 4 | 1, 9 (×2 each) |
| AMRAAM | 1, 9 (×2 each), 3, 4, 6, 7 |
| AIM-7M / AIM-7F Sparrow | 3, 4, 6, 7 |
| MK-82 | 2, 8, and ×6 on 5 |
| MK-83 | 2, 8, and ×3 on 5 |
| MK-84 | 2, 5, 8 |
| Fuel tank, 600 gal ("4000 LB") | 2, 5, 8 |
| ECM pod | 5 |

## 6. DASH: aiming by looking

**When**: a target well off the nose, a Python 4 or AIM-9 selected. **Keys**: look aside with **Shift+arrows**,
**Numpad** snap views or padlock (**F3**: the view follows the radar target). The DASH appears once the panel pans
away.

The DASH (Display And Sight Helmet, Elbit) is the IAF's (Israeli Air Force) helmet display on the F-15C/D and F-16C/D,
fielded with the Python 4 in 1993–94. It shows a 20° circle of green symbols on the visor. Its layout is public in
outline; the game draws the symbols of its descendant, the JHMCS (Joint Helmet-Mounted Cueing System), as a
reconstruction [R].

![Looking 40° left at a locked MiG-23 with a Python 4](shots/f15/dash.png)

1. **Aiming cross**: where your head points.
2. **Line-of-sight circle** around the cross.
3. **Gap** in the circle: it points where you look from the nose. A gap at 9 o'clock: looking left.
4. **Target box**: the radar target.
5. **Seeker diamond**: what the missile's seeker tracks.
6. **Heading your head looks at**: 230° here, 40° left of the jet's 270°.
7. **g**.
8. **Airspeed**.
9. **ARM**: weapons live.
10. **Weapon**: four short-range missiles (two on each wing pylon).
11. **Altitude**.
12. **Steerpoint**: distance > number.

**Example**: a MiG-23 is 40° left, 3,000 m away. Lock it on the radar (**Return**), select the Python 4 (**]**) and
look at it (**F3**). The seeker follows your head inside the Python 4's 60° cone (Python 3 30°), and the diamond
settles in the box. Fire (**Space**) on the high tone.

## 7. SRM: AIM-9L / AIM-9M, Python 3, Python 4

**When**: a target inside about 10 NM, best from behind. **Keys**: **]** until the IR missile, **Space** to fire.
With a radar lock (**R**, then **Return**) the seeker is slaved to the locked target.

![An AIM-9L locked on a MiG-23 1.6 NM ahead](shots/f15/hud_srm.png)

1. **Missile circle**: 60 mr (milliradians) around the HUD's centre. Bring the target inside it.
2. **Seeker circle**: on what the seeker tracks; here on the target.
3. **TD box** (target designator): the radar target.
4. **Gun cross**: shown in every weapon mode.
5. **Range scale top**: the scale's range, 10 NM here.
6. **Range caret** with the closure in knots (`490>`).
7. **IN RNG**: the target is between the missile's maximum and minimum range.
8. **Mach**, shown in the air-to-air modes.
9. **G**.

| Missile | Real data |
| --- | --- |
| AIM-9L / AIM-9M | 86 kg, Mach 2.5, 17.7 km |
| Python 3 | 120 kg, Mach 3.5, 15 km, 30° off the nose |
| Python 4 | 120 kg, Mach 3.5, 15 km, 60° off the nose |

**Example**: the MiG is 3,000 m (1.6 NM) ahead. The guide's MiG holds still, so the closure is your own 486 kt,
rounded to `490>`. Against it the AIM-9L reaches 16.1 km (8.7 NM) and needs at least 1.4 km (0.77 NM): the caret sits
low on the 10 NM scale and **IN RNG** shows. A growl, then a high tone: the seeker is locked, fire. The real F-15's
SHOOT cue is a light on the canopy bow; in the game, IN RNG and the high tone are your cue. Beyond half its range the
seeker sees a jet with its afterburner lit.

### AIM-9X (Weapons = Modern)

With Extras → **Weapons = Modern** the Arming screen's AA list adds the **AIM-9X** Block II, two on each wing pylon's
rails (stations 1 and 9), as the USAF's F-15Cs carried it. It flies like the other SRMs, with three differences:
1. **Wide seeker**: look at the target with the DASH (**Shift+arrows** or the **Numpad** snap views, section 6) and
   the seeker locks it up to 90° off the nose. A radar lock slaves it too, and **F3** turns your head onto it, but
   only inside the radar's ±60°.
2. **Turn off the rail**: the missile turns at once toward a target beside or behind your wing line.
3. **Lock-on after launch**: fire without a lock and it flies along your line of sight and locks the first target
   near that line.

![Looking 70° left at a MiG-23 with the AIM-9X](shots/f15/dash_9x.png)

1. **Aiming cross**: where your head points.
2. **Seeker diamond**: the AIM-9X's seeker on the MiG.
3. **Heading your head looks at**: 200°, 70° left of the jet's 270°.
4. **ARM**: weapons live.
5. **Weapon**: four short-range missiles.

**Example**: a MiG-23 is 70° left, 3,000 m away, outside the radar's ±60°. Select the AIM-9X (**]**) and look left
(**Shift+Left**, or the left **Numpad** snap view). The diamond settles on the MiG and the tone rises. Fire
(**Space**): the missile turns hard left off the rail and hits. No radar lock is needed.

## 8. MRM: AIM-7M / AIM-7F and AMRAAM

**When**: a target beyond the IR missiles' reach. **Keys**: radar on (**R**), lock (**Return** in LRS (long-range
search), or click its blip), **]** until the radar missile, **Space** to fire.

![An AIM-7M locked on a MiG-23 8 NM ahead](shots/f15/hud_mrm.png)

1. **ASE circle** (allowable steering error): it grows as you close from MAX toward MIN.
2. **Steering dot**: steer it into the circle.
3. **TD box** on the locked target.
4. **Gun cross**.
5. **Range scale top**: 40 NM here.
6. **MAX tick**: the missile's maximum range against this target.
7. **MIN tick**: its minimum range.
8. **Range caret** with the closure (`490>`).
9. **IN RNG**: between MAX and MIN.
10. **Mach**.

| Missile | Real data |
| --- | --- |
| AIM-7M / AIM-7F Sparrow | 230 kg, Mach 4, 70 km, **semi-active**: it needs your lock until it hits |
| AMRAAM (AIM-120A/B) | 157 kg, Mach 4, 70 km, **active**: it guides itself after the launch |

**Example**: lock the MiG-23 at 8 NM (14.8 km) with an AIM-7M selected. The scale shows MAX near its top, 36 NM
(67.3 km) against this still target, and MIN near its bottom, 0.8 NM (1.5 km). The caret at 8 NM sits low on the
scale, above MIN: **IN RNG** shows and the circle is close to its largest. Put the dot in the circle and fire, then
hold the lock until the hit: **Q**, **R**, **S**, **Return**, a click or **Backspace** breaks a Sparrow's guidance.

### Inside minimum range: the breakaway X

![The MiG-23 at 1,000 m, inside the AIM-7M's minimum range](shots/f15/hud_mrm_break.png)

1. **Breakaway X** across the HUD: the target is inside the missile's minimum range.
2. **Range caret** at the bottom of the scale, under the MIN tick; IN RNG has gone.
3. **TD box** on the target.

**Example**: at 1,000 m the MiG is inside the AIM-7M's 1.5 km minimum. Select the gun (**]**), or turn away and come
back for a new shot.

## 9. Guns: LCOS (A-A) and the A-G gun

**When**: a target inside about 3,000 ft, or strafing. **Keys**: **]** to the gun for air-to-air, **[** for
air-to-ground, **Tab** fires. The M61A1 has 940 rounds at 6,000 rounds/min: about 9 s of firing.

![The LCOS gunsight locked on a MiG-23 at 2,600 ft](shots/f15/hud_gun.png)

1. **Outer ring** with 12 ticks: the reticle, 50 mr across.
2. **Dashed inner circle**.
3. **Pipper**: the lead-computed aim point. Put it on the target and fire.
4. **Range arc**: the radar range, 1,000 ft per tick from 12 o'clock.
5. **TD box** on the target.
6. **Gun cross**.
7. **Mach**.

**Example**: the MiG is 800 m (2,600 ft) ahead. The range arc runs from 12 o'clock past the second tick, about 2.6
ticks. Track the MiG with the pipper for a moment, then hold **Tab** for a 1 s burst (100 rounds).

![The A-G gun in a 20° dive at 1,970 ft above the ground](shots/f15/hud_strf.png)

1. **Pipper** on the predicted impact of the rounds.
2. **Reticle ring**: the same LCOS reticle, carried to the impact point.
3. **Gun cross**.

**Example**: dive at 20° and about 410 kt. With the pipper on the target at 1,970 ft above the ground, fire a short
burst and pull out: the ground comes up at about 240 ft/s.

## 10. Bombs: the fall line

**When**: MK-82, MK-83 and MK-84. **Keys**: **[** to the bombs; hold **Space** to release. The stores page sets the
ripple quantity and spacing.

The F-15A/C aims bombs with a fall line: the line runs from the velocity vector to a square on the predicted impact.

![Six MK-82s in a 45° dive: the fall line to the target square](shots/f15/hud_bomb.png)

1. **Target square**: where the bombs land now.
2. **Bomb fall line**: from the velocity vector to the square. Keep the target on it.
3. **Velocity vector**.
4. **Gun cross**.
5. **Pitch rung**: 45° nose-down.

**Example**: six MK-82s on the centreline, a 45° dive at 2,950 ft above the ground. Roll in with the target above
the velocity vector, let the fall line carry it down, and hold **Space** as it reaches the square: the ripple drops
one bomb per interval while you hold it.

### GBU-31 JDAM (Weapons = Modern)

With Extras → **Weapons = Modern** the AG list adds the **GBU-31** (a 2,000 lb Mk 84 with a GPS tail kit) on the
wing pylons and the centreline (stations 2, 5 and 8), as the IAF's Baz carries three.

**When**: a fixed ground target, from a safe height or distance, in any weather. **Keys**: **[** to the GBU-31;
**K** PRE ↔ VIS, **Shift+K** the next PRE target; **Space** releases.

It has no seeker: it flies to the coordinates it gets when it leaves the jet, about 24 km from 40,000 ft, 8 km from
10,000 ft at 485 kt.
- **PRE** (pre-planned, the default): each station's target, set on the briefing TSD: click the station's row in the
  GPS targets panel (the map's lower-right corner), then a unit, a waypoint or a map point
  ([F-16I guide](f16i.md#setting-the-pre-targets-on-the-briefing-tsd), with a picture). A station without one takes
  the route's target waypoint. **Shift+K** steps through the stored targets and the steerpoints.
- **VIS** (visual): the radar's designated point (the ground map, the HUD's TD box).
- With no point the bomb falls on the target square's point.

The game draws the F-16's JDAM symbols on the F-15's HUD; the F-15's own are not public (inferred).

![A GBU-31 in PRE 18 km from its target: the LAR staple and the time to max range](shots/f15/hud_jdam.png)

1. **TD box**: the bomb's target.
2. **Azimuth steering line**: put the velocity vector on it.
3. **Velocity vector**.
4. **LAR staple**, where the range scale goes: the ranges the bomb can reach, Rmin at its bottom, Rmax at its top.
5. **Range caret** under the scale's top (10 NM): your ground distance to the target, 9.7 NM.
6. **TMR 0:45**: the time to max range; inside the LAR it reads `IN RNG`.
7. **PRE**: the mode, under the G.

**Example**: the picture. The GBU-31's PRE target lies 18 km ahead, 4° right; you dive gently at 486 kt from
10,000 ft. Rmax is about 7 km (3.9 NM): the caret sits above the staple and `TMR 0:45` counts down. Put the velocity
vector on the steering line and hold the dive; when `IN RNG` shows, release anywhere in the staple (**Space**, no
cue to wait for): the bomb leaves at once and glides onto the target. With HUD = Original: a diamond on the target
with a line from the velocity vector, a dot in it and `IN RNG` inside the LAR, and the range `R x.x` NM.

## 11. Landing: the gear-down HUD

**When**: on final approach, gear handle down (**G**). **Keys**: **G** gear, **F** flaps, **B** speed brake in the air
and wheel brakes on the ground, **Ctrl+T** calls the tower near an Israeli base. The approach speeds and touchdown
limits are in section 4.

With the gear down the tapes move lower, the altitude tape expands to a 300 ft window, and the **AoA (angle of attack)
scale** appears inboard of the airspeed tape. The ILS (instrument landing system) to the nearest airbase's runway
shows as the F-15's flight director: the NAV window reads `ILS-N`, and the bank and pitch steering bars form a cross.
Fly the aircraft symbol to the cross.

![Final approach, 2.2 NM out, gear down, 5° AoA](shots/f15/landing_final.png)

1. **Velocity vector**: on the runway threshold here. Keep it there.
2. **AoA caret** on the AoA scale (0–45; the game shows degrees as its units).
3. **Pitch steering bar**: it joins the cross once you reach the 5° glide slope's centre. Before that, within 0.6° of
   it, `GSUP` (fly up) or `GSDN` (fly down) shows under the NAV window. The bar drops when you are high and goes when
   you stray more than 0.6° from the glide slope.
4. **Bank steering bar**: on final it follows the localizer, toward the runway's centre line.
5. **Heading caret**.
6. **Airspeed caret**: about 145 kt here.
7. **Altitude caret**.

**Example**: here the jet flies a 3° path at 5° AoA, more than 0.6° under the 5° glide slope: no pitch steering bar
and no `GSUP` yet. Hold altitude until `GSUP` shows, then fly the bar as it joins the cross. The stall is near 130 kt: keep the approach above about 150 kt, and raise the nose to slow down; the AoA
caret climbs the scale as you do.

## 12. Better cockpit

**When**: any flight. **Keys**: tap **V** for the whole panel (hold it to glance down and back), **PgUp** / **PgDn**
slide it.

With Better cockpit on, the engine and fuel needles show the flight model's own values, the fuel dial counts the fuel in
pounds, and the MFD pages are drawn sharp. The engine needles stay on the dials the 1998 game draws them on: the labels
of the F-15 panel's engine dials are too small to read, so there is no labelled dial to move them to. The needles follow
their values with a 0.3 s instrument lag.

![The F-15 panel with Better cockpit on](shots/f15/better_panel.png)

1. **RPM**, left engine (the right engine's dial is beside it): the engine's real RPM, 60 % at idle, 100 % at
   military. It needs about 3 s from idle to military.
2. **Throttle**, left engine (the right engine's dial is beside it): military at the full mark; in AB it moves on
   past military, further in AB 2.
3. **Fuel needle**: all the fuel you carry, the drop tanks too, over the most the jet holds with them. It falls from
   the first pound burnt (in the 1998 game it stays full until the tanks are empty).
4. **TOTAL LBS counter**: the fuel left, in pounds (`13455`).
5. **MFD pages**: the same pages, drawn at the screen's resolution. The TV and FLIR pictures stay as they are.

**Example**: one engine's RPM falls to 0 while the other reads 100 %: that engine has cut out (damage). With the
tanks carried, watch the counter: at 1,500 lb the HUD shows FUEL.

## 13. Night vision

**When**: night missions (the game's night is 20:00–05:30; mission 214 starts at 01:00), with Graphics → MORE →
Night vision = Goggles. **Keys**: **Shift+N** puts the goggles on and takes them off.

The goggles are ours: the 1998 game has none. They turn the outside view into a brightened green picture; the
cockpit and the HUD stay as you see them under the goggles. Set Graphics → MORE → Night lights = Detailed too, for
the moon, the stars and the lights on the ground.

![01:00 without goggles](shots/f15/night.png)

![The same moment through the goggles](shots/f15/night_nvg.png)

- The ground's relief and texture show, and the horizon is clear: fly by the outside view.
- The goggles set their own gain: the darker the scene, the more they brighten it, and the grainier it gets.
- The HUD shows in its brightest green while the goggles are on; your own level comes back when they come off.
- The panel's lighting suits the goggles from 1996. Before that its glow washes out the picture's lower part.
- A view full of light (a close explosion, daylight) darkens the goggles: take them off by day.
- The goggles follow the mission's year: none before 1972, a grainy Gen II up to 1981, a Gen III from 1982, the
  sharpest from 1996 (the 1998 missions).
- A bright light (a fire, a flare, a runway light) gets a halo around it.
- The picture darkens toward its edges.

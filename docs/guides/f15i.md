# F-15I Ra'am pilot guide (realistic settings)

This guide covers our **F-15I Ra'am** ("Thunder"), the IAF's (Israeli Air Force) two-seat strike Eagle with conformal
fuel tanks, flown with the realistic options on. The Ra'am is our addition to the game (docs/f15i.md): the 1998 game
has no Ra'am. It is today's upgraded F-15I, and where the F-15I has no published figure the game uses the F-15E's
(the jet it is built from), labelled as such. It flies from the F-15's cockpit; with HUD on Real it shows the F-15E's
HUD (head-up display). Each mode has its keys, a picture whose numbered yellow callouts the list under it explains,
and a worked example. Section 11 lists the keys and abbreviations.

**To fly it**: Preferences → Extras → **F-15 version** → **Ra'am (F-15I)**. Every F-15 becomes an F-15I: yours, your
wingmen's, the enemy's and the parked ones, in every mission. With the switch on, the Reference screen's **PILOT
GUIDE** for the F-15 shows this guide; set the switch back to Baz (F-15C) to read the F-15 guide there.

## 1. What's different in this jet

| Area | F-15C Baz (realistic options) | F-15I Ra'am |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Airframe | One seat | Two seats under one canopy, conformal fuel tanks (CFTs) on the intakes' sides |
| Engines | 2 × F100-PW-220: 28,740 / 46,900 lbf | 2 × F100-PW-229: 35,600 / 58,320 lbf |
| Fuel | 13,455 lb internal | 22,665 lb: 12,915 lb internal and 2 × 4,875 lb in the CFTs (F-15E) |
| Weight | 28,476 lb empty | 37,000 lb with the CFTs and crew (F-15E), 81,000 lb maximum |
| HUD | The F-15A/C's moving tapes | The F-15E's digital windows: speed and altitude in boxes, the rest round the edges |
| Radar | AN/APG-63: 135 km (Weapon data = Real) | AN/APG-70I: 104 km (56 NM) |
| Gun | M61A1, 940 rounds (Weapon data = Real) | M61A1, 500 rounds (F-15E) |
| IR missiles | AIM-9L / M, Python 3 / 4 | Also the **Python 5**: 100° off the nose, lock-on after launch |
| Bombs | MK-82, MK-83, MK-84 | Also the **GBU-31** and the **BLU109** (GBU-31(V)3) JDAMs, GPS-guided 2,000 lb bombs, seven per jet, the **GBU-39** (small GPS bombs, four to a rack), the **SPICE1** (a winged 1,000 lb glide bomb, about 100 km) and the **GBU-28** (a 4,700 lb laser-guided penetrator) |
| Flight data | Original or Real | Always the Ra'am's real numbers, whatever Flight data says |

![The F-15I Ra'am: the two-seat canopy, the conformal fuel tanks, Python 5s and GBU-31(V)3s](shots/f15i/external.png)

The game has no second cockpit: you fly from the front seat, and the WSO's (weapon systems officer's) work is yours.

![The F-15I's front cockpit at 10,000 ft: the F-15E HUD over the F-15's panel](shots/f15i/cockpit.png)

1. **HUD**: the F-15E's (section 3).
2. **Left display**: the radar, `OFF` until you switch it on (**R**, section 5).
3. **Middle display**: the TSD (tactical situation display), the map.
4. **Right display**: the RWR (radar warning receiver).

**Voice and HUD warnings.** With the 1998 HUD, Betty (the cockpit voice) calls "Pull up" every 4 s in the A-G gun and
bomb modes while the dive is steeper than 1° per 100 ft above the ground (20° at 2,000 ft), and "Altitude" every 4 s
below 100 ft with the gear up. With the Real HUD there is no voice for the dive or the height (the F-15's voice set
has none in the sources). The F-15E HUD's **break X** takes its place: in the A-G modes a large X across the HUD says
pull up now (section 7).

A greyed-out imagery choice needs its data: convert it with `tools/setup-cli.sh --imagery`.

## 3. NAV: reading the F-15E HUD

**When**: cruising, navigating to a steerpoint, and between attacks. **Keys**: **N** returns to NAV from any weapon
mode. **W** / **Shift+W** pick the next / previous steerpoint. **H** changes the HUD colour.

With HUD on Real the Ra'am shows the F-15E's HUD, not the F-15C's moving tapes: the speed and the altitude in boxes,
the rest in windows round the edges. It is drawn from the F-15E flight manual (TO 1F-15E-1, figs. 1-22 and 1-23); the
F-15I's own HUD software is not public, so the F-15E's stands in for it. With **Real HUD view** on, the view widens so
that its 28° × 21° field fits the glass.

![The F-15E HUD in NAV at 10,000 ft, steerpoint 1 ahead](shots/f15i/hud_nav.png)

1. **Airspeed**: calibrated (the C left of the box), in knots.
2. **AoA** (α, angle of attack), in degrees.
3. **Heading scale**: tens of degrees; the caret under it marks your heading, the two short bars the steerpoint's
   bearing (off the scale, its bearing shows in digits at the scale's end).
4. **Waterline symbol**: where the nose points.
5. **Velocity vector**: where the jet goes. The pitch ladder's rungs bend toward the horizon by half their angle.
6. **Altitude**: barometric (the B left of the box), in feet.
7. **Radar altitude** (R), in feet. Below 1,500 ft a bar at the right edge shows it too (labels in hundreds of feet).
8. **Mach**.
9. **G**: now, and the most you may pull: the Ra'am's limit, 8.1 (section 4).
10. **Steering**: NAV and the steerpoint's number.
11. **Range** to the steerpoint (N), in NM (nautical miles).
12. **Time** to the steerpoint (hours:minutes:seconds).
13. **Bank scale**: the pointer shows your bank against ticks at 10, 20, 30, 45 and 60°.

A vertical **steering line** through the waterline symbol shows the steerpoint's side: bank toward it; on the symbol,
the steerpoint is straight ahead.

**Example**: the picture. At the start of mission 231 steerpoint 1 lies 4.7 NM ahead at 427 kt calibrated: `N4.7`
and a time to go of `00:00:35`. The steerpoint bars sit on the heading caret and the steering line on the waterline
symbol: hold the heading.

The bingo fuel warning comes at 1,500 lb. With 22,665 lb of fuel you may want it higher: Preferences → Extras → Real
HUD bingo (500–5,000 lb).

## 4. Flying the F-15I

These are the game's values, checked by its validation test (the F-15I row) against the published figures. The F-15C
column is the F-15 with Flight data = Real.

| Item | F-15I in the game | F-15C in the game |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Empty weight | 37,000 lb (F-15E, with the CFTs and crew) | 28,476 lb |
| Internal fuel | 22,665 lb | 13,455 lb |
| Weight with full fuel, no stores | 59,665 lb | 41,931 lb |
| Thrust, military / full afterburner | 35,600 / 58,320 lbf | 28,740 / 46,900 lbf |
| 1 g stall speed, sea level, full fuel | 155 kt (F-15E, engine idle, gear and flaps up) | 130 kt |
| Maximum roll rate | 180°/s | 180°/s |
| Maximum speed, sea level / 40,000 ft | 765 kt (Mach 1.16) / 1,133 kt (Mach 1.98) | 805 kt / 1,238 kt |
| Climb at 350 kt, sea level, full afterburner | about 37,300 ft/min | about 42,000 ft/min |
| Instantaneous turn, 420 kt at 10,000 ft | 16.1°/s at 6.4 g | 22.4°/s at 8.7 g |
| g limits | +8.1 / −2.0 (F-15E with the CFTs) | +9 / −3 |
| Fuel flow, military / full afterburner | 27,000 / 113,100 lb/h | 21,000 / 98,000 lb/h |
| Service ceiling | 60,000 ft (F-15E) | 65,000 ft |

The Ra'am's numbers and their sources: docs/f15i.md §1. The top speed at 40,000 ft is fitted to the F-15E's
Mach 2.0 with the CFTs, a secondary figure. The stall speed, the CFTs' drag and the g limits are the F-15E flight
manual's.

What this means in the cockpit:

- **Fuel**: about 12 minutes at full afterburner and about 50 minutes at military power at sea level (the F-15C: 8
  and 38). The Ra'am is built for the long strike. The 600-gallon tanks ("4000 LB") go on stations 2, 5 and 8.
- **Weight**: at full fuel the Ra'am is about 42 % heavier for 24 % more thrust. It climbs and accelerates more slowly
  and turns less hard than the Baz. As the fuel burns down it comes closer.
- **Turns**: the g limit is 8.1, not the Baz's 9 (the F-15E's with the CFTs). At full fuel it reaches 8.1 g only
  from about 440 kt (inferred: √8.1 × the stall speed); fight faster than in the Baz.
- **Top speed**: lower than the Baz's: the CFTs add drag.
- **Mechanical flight controls**: the stick moves the surfaces directly, so the limits are yours. At low speed a hard
  pull stalls the wings, and with Realistic spins on the jet can spin. **Recovery**: stick forward, rudder against the
  spin, then pull out of the dive.
- **Flight-path hold** (Physics): with the stick centred the jet holds its flight path.
- **Ground effect** (Physics): a longer float in the flare. **Real idle thrust on the ground**: at idle the jet taxis.
- **The stick on the keyboard** gives full deflection at once, as in the original; a joystick is smoother.
- **Engine spool-up per real engine** (Physics): the F100-PW-229s go from idle to full afterburner in 3.9 s (the
  F-15E's "less than four seconds"): 3 s from idle to military, the afterburner lighting at once and filling in 0.9 s.
  The Baz's -220s take 5.5 s. Out of afterburner the thrust drops at once.
- **Wind and turbulence** (Physics): the air moves with the tower's wind. The jet crabs into a crosswind and drifts
  downwind, a headwind shortens the take-off run, and light gusts shake it, strongest below 300 m (1,000 ft). Bombs
  drift downwind (the bombing computer allows for it). With a wind of 5 kt or more from behind the main runway the
  tower turns the base to the runway's other end. Gameplay → No wind calms the air.
- **Nose dips under braking** (Physics): the nose dips up to 2° under the wheel brakes and squats up to 1° at full
  thrust on the brakes.
- **Unbalanced stores roll the jet** (Physics): stores left on one wing roll the jet toward it: hold that wing up with
  the stick, trim it out (**Alt+Left / Right**, or the throttle rocker with Devices → Throttle rocker = Roll trim), or
  let the autopilot (**A**) hold it while it flies. As on the Baz, one 2,000 lb bomb on a wing pylon, 3 m out, takes
  about 9 % stick at 290 kt and 1 g; it grows with the g in a pull (four times the stick at 4 g) and as the speed
  falls. The trim moves the stick at most a quarter of its travel. The release order keeps a symmetric load balanced:
  after a release the next store of that weapon comes from the station farthest from the last one. When the load is
  even again the roll stops at once, but the trim stays where you set it: take it back off until the `TRIM L / R`
  reading on the **Ctrl+F12** info line is gone.
- **Bomb release: Real** (Extras): bombs leave the way the real stores management releases them. Stores page OSB 7
  (bottom row, second from left) switches PAIR / SGL; the page shows it as `1 PAIR` or `1 SGL`. The number is the
  release pulses per press and starts at 1. In PAIR each pulse drops one bomb off each wing at the same instant,
  outboard pair first, so the jet stays balanced. In SGL each pulse drops one bomb, alternating wings, left first.
  This jet has no published release-system source, so it follows the F-16's rules.
- **The map edge**: 12 km (6.5 NM) before it the EndWorld warning sounds: turn back. Past it the game pushes the jet
  back for about 8 s.

## 5. Radar and self-protection

The Ra'am's AN/APG-70I has the APG-70's air-to-air modes; its ground maps have a third of the USAF set's
resolution[^wp-e]. It sees a fighter-sized target at 56 NM (104 km)[^wp-e], in line of sight, from 30 m above the
ground.

| Item | F-15I | F-15C |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Detection, Weapon data = Real (LRS and STT) | 104 km (56 NM) | 135 km (73 NM) |
| The AI's sensor, an AI F-15 | 30.8 NM | 40 NM |
| Detection, Weapon data = Original | the F-15's | the F-15's |

With Real weapon data the Ra'am's radar sees less far than the Baz's: the two figures come from different sources
(docs/f15i.md §2). An enemy AI F-15I sees you from 30.8 NM.

- **Keys**: **R** radar on (A-A ↔ A-G), **Q** mode (LRS → TWS → ACM; A-G: GMT ↔ MAP), **S** standby, **.** / **,**
  range scale, **Return** next contact (locks in LRS), **Backspace** drop the lock, **\\** (hold) boresight.
- **Displays**: the left one starts on the radar, the right one on the RWR, the middle one on the TSD. **T** the TSD,
  **D** the damage report, **V** / **PgUp** / **PgDn** slide the panel.
- **RWR**: the RWR page shows emitters and missiles around you; a new threat sounds a tone, a missile launch at you
  the launch warning. With Extras → *RWR: Real* it also shows the SAM and AAA radars searching you before they lock:
  a steady symbol and the new-threat tone. Drop behind a ridge and the symbol goes.
- **Chaff (Insert)** and **flares (Delete)**: 120 chaff and 75 flares. A flare fools an IR missile with 33 % (50 %
  above 4 g) while the afterburner is off: pull hard, come out of afterburner, then flare.
- **ECM (J)** (electronic countermeasures) works with the **ECM pod** on station 5: each radar-guided missile already
  flying at you is jammed with a 60 % chance. SAMs (surface-to-air missiles) keep guiding. The real F-15I carries an
  Elisra suite in place of the F-15E's; the game's pod stands for it (docs/f15i.md §5).
- **AWACS calls**: from 60 s into the flight the airborne controller calls the enemy aircraft within 35 NM, one scan
  every 12 s. "Alpha, bearing 090, 15 miles, target, heading 270, free to engage": the contact's bearing and distance
  from you, then its heading. "Unidentified contacts, please identify": your radar has not seen it yet; find it
  before you engage. Below 50 m (165 ft) the controller is silent.
- **Flight reports**: the radio reports your side's jets airborne, landed, crashed or ejected, and each kill 5 s after
  it ("Alpha leader shot down MiG 29").

## 6. DASH: aiming by looking

**When**: a target well off the nose, an IR missile selected. **Keys**: look aside with **Shift+arrows**, **Numpad**
snap views or padlock (**F3**: the view follows the radar target). The DASH appears once the panel pans away.

The F-15I's crews wear the Elbit DASH (Display And Sight Helmet) in both cockpits[^wp-e]. Its symbols are not public
in full: the game draws those of its descendant, the JHMCS (Joint Helmet-Mounted Cueing System), as a reconstruction,
the same as on the Baz. The Python 5's seeker follows your head up to 100° off the nose (section 7).

![Looking 90° right at a MiG-23 with the Python 5: the seeker follows the helmet](shots/f15i/dash_p5.png)

1. **Aiming cross**: where your head points.
2. **Seeker diamond**: the Python 5's seeker on the MiG.
3. **Heading your head looks at**.
4. **ARM**: weapons live.
5. **Weapon**: four short-range missiles.
6. **SHOOT**: the seeker is locked and the target in range.
7. **Line-of-sight circle** around the cross; its gap points where you look from the nose (here at 3 o'clock:
   looking right).

The Ra'am's own weapons and their sources: IAF photos show the Python 5 on F-15Is[^avi24]; the F-15I carries "up to 20 SDBs or seven GBU-31 series
weapons"[^fpri], and seven BLU-109 JDAMs per jet were seen in 2024[^twzblu]; the GBU-28 was bought for Israel's F-15s[^globes05][^globes10]; the GBU-39 was first

racks: one weighs 4,700 lb, and three bring the jet near its maximum take-off weight with full fuel.

### SRM: AIM-9L / M, Python 3 / 4 and Python 5

**When**: a target inside about 10 NM, best from behind. **Keys**: **]** until the IR missile, **Space** to fire. With
a radar lock (**R**, then **Return**) the seeker is slaved to the locked target.

![A Python 5 locked on a MiG-23 1.6 NM ahead](shots/f15i/hud_srm.png)

1. **Missile circle**: 60 mr (milliradians) around the HUD's centre. Bring the target inside it.
2. **Seeker circle**: on what the seeker tracks; here on the target.
3. **TD box** (target designator): the radar target.
4. **Range scale**: its top the scale's range in NM, half of it at its middle.
5. **Range caret** with the closure in knots.
6. **SHOOT**: the seeker is locked and the target in range.
7. **Missile count**: S (short range) and how many. The Python missiles show no type letter; the AIM-9L / M show L
   / M.

| Missile | Real data |
| --- | --- |
| AIM-9L / AIM-9M | 86 kg, Mach 2.5, 17.7 km |
| Python 3 | 120 kg, Mach 3.5, 15 km, 30° off the nose |
| Python 4 | 120 kg, Mach 3.5, 15 km, 60° off the nose |
| Python 5 | 105 kg, Mach 4, over 20 km, 100° off the nose; seeker lock from behind 20 km, head-on 6 km, head-on against an afterburner 16 km |

The Python 5 locks after launch up to 100° off boresight[^python]. Its seeker lock ranges are not public: the game
uses 1.25 × the Python 4's (inferred). It flies like the other SRMs, with three differences:

1. **Wide seeker**: the seeker follows your head (the DASH) or a radar lock up to 100° off the nose. The radar locks
   only inside ±60°, so beyond that, look at the target.
2. **Turn off the rail**: the missile steers at once and turns hard toward a target beside or behind your wing line.
3. **Lock-on after launch** (LOAL): fire without a seeker lock and the missile flies along your line of sight (where
   you look, or the nose), then locks the first target near that line that its seeker can lock. While it searches
   the DASH flashes **LOAL** under the cross.

**Example: on the nose.** The picture: the MiG is 3,000 m (1.6 NM) ahead. Lock it (**Return**), select the Python 5
(**]**): the seeker circle settles on the TD box, the tone rises and **SHOOT** shows. Fire (**Space**).

**Example: off the nose.** A MiG-23 is 90° right, 3,000 m away, outside the radar's ±60° (section 6's picture).
Select the Python 5 and look right (**Shift+Right**). The diamond settles on the MiG and the tone rises. Fire: the
missile turns hard right off the rail and hits. No radar lock is needed. Looking past 100° puts the diamond on the
field's edge with an X over it: the seeker has stopped at its limit.

**Example: lock-on after launch.** A MiG-29 comes at you head-on, 9 km ahead, its afterburner off. The Python 5's
seeker locks a head-on target only inside 6 km, so there is no tone. Put the nose on the MiG and fire: the Python 5
flies along the line, and once it is inside 6 km of the MiG it locks and chases it.

### MRM: AIM-7M / AIM-7F and AMRAAM

**When**: a target beyond the IR missiles' reach. **Keys**: radar on (**R**), lock (**Return** in LRS (long-range
search), or click its blip), **]** until the radar missile, **Space** to fire.

![An AIM-7M locked on a MiG-23 8 NM ahead: the F-15E's windows and range scale](shots/f15i/hud_mrm.png)

1. **ASE circle** (allowable steering error): steer the dot into it.
2. **Steering dot**.
3. **TD box** on the locked target.
4. **Closure** in knots, at the range caret on the range scale (the scale's NM at its top, half of it at its middle).
5. **Range** to the target (R), in NM.
6. **Target aspect**: tens of degrees off its tail, L or R the side you see (T: tail-on, H: head-on).
7. **Missile count**: M (medium range), how many, the type (A AMRAAM, M AIM-7M, F AIM-7F).
8. **SHOOT**: the target is in range.

| Missile | Real data |
| --- | --- |
| AIM-7M / AIM-7F Sparrow | 230 kg, Mach 4, 70 km, **semi-active**: it needs your lock until it hits |
| AMRAAM (AIM-120A/B) | 157 kg, Mach 4, 70 km, **active**: it guides itself after the launch |

**Example**: lock the MiG-23 at 8 NM (14.8 km) with an AIM-7M selected. The range scale's ticks show the missile's
maximum and minimum range against it; the caret sits between them and **SHOOT** shows. Put the dot in the circle and
fire, then hold the lock until the hit: **Q**, **R**, **S**, **Return**, a click or **Backspace** breaks a Sparrow's
guidance. Inside the minimum range **SHOOT** goes: select the gun (**]**), or turn away and come back for a new shot.

### Gun: LCOS (A-A) and the A-G gun

**When**: a target inside about 3,000 ft, or strafing. **Keys**: **]** to the gun for air-to-air, **[** for
air-to-ground, **Tab** fires. The M61A1 has 500 rounds (Weapon data = Real, the F-15E's count) at 6,000 rounds/min:
about 5 s of firing.

The F-15E's air-to-air gunsight is in a manual that is not public: the game draws the F-15A/C's LCOS (lead-computing
optical sight) on the F-15E HUD (inferred).

![The gunsight locked on a MiG-23 at 2,600 ft](shots/f15i/hud_gun.png)

1. **Outer ring** with 12 ticks: the reticle, 50 mr across.
2. **Pipper**: the lead-computed aim point. Put it on the target and fire.
3. **Range arc**: the radar range, 1,000 ft per tick from 12 o'clock.
4. **TD box** on the target.
5. **Gun cross**: 2° above the waterline, in every weapon mode.
6. **Rounds** left.

**Example**: the MiG is 800 m (2,600 ft) ahead. The range arc runs from 12 o'clock past the second tick, about 2.6
ticks. Track the MiG with the pipper for a moment, then hold **Tab** for a 1 s burst (100 rounds): with 500 rounds,
five such bursts.

**A-G gun**: **[** until the gun. The HUD shows the A-G reticle (a 50 mil circle with a pipper) on the rounds'
predicted impact, `GUN` in the mode window and the rounds left. Dive at about 20°, put the pipper on the target, fire
a short burst and pull out.

### Bombs: CDIP

**When**: MK-82, MK-83 and MK-84, and the GBU-28. **Keys**: **[** to the bombs; hold **Space** to release. The stores
page sets the ripple quantity and spacing.

The F-15E aims bombs with a reticle on the computed impact point (CDIP), not the F-15A/C's fall line.

![Six MK-82s in a 45° dive: the F-15E's CDIP reticle on the target](shots/f15i/hud_bomb.png)

1. **Reticle**: a 50 mil circle on the bombs' impact point (CDIP).
2. **Pipper**: put it on the target and release.
3. **Velocity vector**.
4. **Delivery mode**: CDIP, and the sensor the height comes from (RALT the radar altimeter, BARO without it).

**Example**: six MK-82s on the centreline, a 45° dive at 2,950 ft above the ground. Roll in short of the target, let
the pipper track up to it, and hold **Space** as it reaches the target: the ripple drops one bomb per interval while
you hold it. When the impact point lies below the HUD's field, the release is delayed: put the pipper on the target
and hold **Space**; a diamond marks the target, `TREL` counts down the time to release, and the bombs leave when the
solution comes.

Diving at the ground, a large **X** across the HUD (the break X) says pull up now: a 4 g pull-out would end below
200 ft.

### GPS bombs: GBU-31, BLU109, GBU-39 and SPICE1

**When**: a fixed ground target, from a safe height or distance, in any weather. **Keys**: **[** until the bomb;
**K** PRE ↔ VIS, **Shift+K** the next PRE target; **Space** releases.

| Bomb | Weight | Reach from 10,000 ft / 30,000 ft / 40,000 ft (486 kt, Mach 0.9 at 40,000 ft) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| GBU-31 (a JDAM on the Mk 84) | 946 kg | 8 / 18 / 23 km |
| BLU109 (the GBU-31(V)3, a JDAM on the BLU-109 penetrator) | 981 kg | as the GBU-31 |
| GBU-39 (SDB, with wings) | 129 kg each | 39 / 85 / 111 km |
| SPICE1 (SPICE 1000, a Mk 83 with folding wings) | 453 kg | about 100 km from 40,000 ft |

The weights are the real bombs'[^dsjdam][^sdb]. The reaches are the game's: a JDAM glides 1.6 m for each metre it
loses, the GBU-39 7.6 m and the SPICE1 6.8 m, set so that the reaches from 40,000 ft match the published 24 km,
"over 60 nmi" and 100 km (inferred conditions)[^dsjdam][^sdb][^twzspice]. The reach is from the height above the
target: the more height and speed you bring, the further it goes. The BLU-109 body has less explosive than the Mk 84
(250 against 429 kg), and the game's blast scales with it.

The JDAMs and the GBU-39 have no seeker and never follow a laser spot. They fly to the coordinates they get when
they leave the jet. The SPICE1 flies there by GPS and INS, then matches its camera's picture of the scene to the
target's stored picture and homes onto it; in the game the coordinates have no error, so it lands on the stored point
too. **K** switches the selected bomb between two ways of giving the coordinates; a subtitle shows the new mode:

- **PRE** (pre-planned, the default): each station's target, set on the briefing TSD before the flight (below). A
  station without one takes the route's target waypoint. **Shift+K** steps through the targets: the station's own,
  then the other stations' and the route's steerpoints, each once; a subtitle shows `PRE 2/5`.
- **VIS** (visual): a point you pick in flight with the radar (below). The game's F-15 carries no targeting pod.

With no point (PRE without a target, VIS without a designation) the bomb falls on the CDIP reticle's point.

#### Setting the PRE targets on the briefing TSD

![The briefing TSD: the GPS targets panel with the Ra'am's BLU109s and GBU-39s](shots/f15i/tsd_gps.png)

1. **Panel header**: `GPS TARGETS`; with a row armed it asks for the target (`CLICK A TARGET FOR STA 4`).
2. **Armed row**, boxed in yellow: the next click on the map sets its target.
3. **No target** (`-`): a station you have not set takes the route's target waypoint; this flight's route (mission
   231's Bravo, the F-15s) has none, so these bombs would fall on the CDIP point.
4. **A unit as the target**: station 2 aims at the tank (`Tank`).
5. **A map point as the target**: station 8 (`PT`).

The panel sits in the TSD map's lower-right corner while your flight carries GPS bombs (the Arming screen's load).
Click a station's row, then click the map: a unit takes its type name (`Tank`), a waypoint of your flight its number
(`WP4`), anywhere else the point itself (`PT`). Click the armed row again to disarm it; right-click a row to put the
station back on the default. A GBU-39 rack's row also opens a row per bomb (`STA 4-1` .. `STA 4-4`, bomb 1 the first
to leave): the station's row sets all four, a bomb's row that bomb only. Each target is a yellow box on the map with
the stations (or bombs) aimed at it. The targets are kept until a mission loads.

#### VIS: a target you pick in flight

In VIS you pick the target in flight with the radar. At the moment you release, the bomb takes that point's
coordinates and flies there. After the release it follows neither the radar nor the jet: you can turn away or drop
the radar point.

1. Select the GPS bomb (**[**).
2. Press **K** until the mode window (the right column's last line) reads `VIS`.
3. Put the radar in air-to-ground: press **R** until the radar page shows GMT or MAP, then **Q** until it shows MAP.
4. Click the target's spot on the MAP page, off the contacts (a double-click on a contact locks it instead): the HUD's
   TD box jumps onto it and the steering line runs through it.
5. Put the velocity vector on the steering line.
6. When `IN RNG` shows, press **Space**.

**Backspace** clears the point. **Before you release, check** that the mode window reads `VIS` and that the TD box
sits on your target.

#### GPS bombs on the HUD

![A GBU-31(V)3 in PRE 18 km from its target: the LAR staple and the time to max range](shots/f15i/hud_jdam.png)

1. **TD box**: the bomb's target. Outside the HUD's view it sticks to the edge with an X over it.
2. **Azimuth steering line**: put the velocity vector on it.
3. **Velocity vector**.
4. **Range caret** under the scale's top (10 NM): your ground distance to the target.
5. **TMR 0:45**: the time to max range; inside the LAR it reads `IN RNG`.
6. **PRE**: the mode, with the height sensor.
7. **LAR staple** (launch acceptability region): the ranges the bomb can reach, Rmin at its bottom, Rmax at its top.

There is no single release point: release anywhere while the caret sits inside the staple, without waiting for a
cue. The bomb leaves at once when you press **Space** and turns toward the target at up to 3 g, so the target can lie
off your track. The CDIP reticle is not drawn while the bomb has a target. The game draws the F-16's JDAM symbols on
the F-15E HUD; the F-15E's own are not public (inferred).

- **Rmax** is 90 % of the bomb's reach, less the arc of the turn toward a target off your track.
- **Rmin**: the bomb can't turn down steeply enough onto a target right below it. From 3,000 m at 486 kt it is about
  1.9 km; from twice the turn radius up it is 0.
- **After the release**: the time window shows the last released bomb's time to impact (`3:08 TIMPCT`) until it
  bursts. To watch the bomb from outside, press **F11** (weapon view): it follows the bomb down; **F1** brings back
  the cockpit.

**Example: a BLU109 in PRE.** The picture: two BLU109s on the wing pylons; the PRE target lies 18 km ahead, 4° right;
you dive gently at 486 kt from 10,000 ft. The reach from here is about 8 km, so Rmax is about 7 km (3.9 NM): the caret
sits above the staple and `TMR 0:45` counts down. Hold the velocity vector on the steering line; when `IN RNG` shows,
press **Space**: the bomb leaves at once and glides onto the target.

![A SPICE1 released in PRE 45 km from its target: the time to impact, the second bomb in range](shots/f15i/hud_spice.png)

1. **TD box** on the target, 45 km ahead.
2. **LAR staple**, under the scale's top (40 NM): the second SPICE1 can reach the target too.
3. **IN RNG**: release now and it reaches the target.
4. **TIMPCT**: the released bomb's time to impact, `3:08`.

**Example: a SPICE1 from stand-off range.** The picture: the target lies 45 km ahead and 6,000 m below you at 250 m/s
(485 kt); the SPICE1's staple reaches about 55 km and `IN RNG` shows at once. Press **Space**: the bomb glides for
about three minutes, `TIMPCT` counting down from about `3:08`. You can turn for home while it flies. Before a long
glide, set its PRE target on the briefing TSD; in VIS the radar's point stands for a target planned in the air.

**Example: GBU-39s.** A radar site lies 40 NM (74 km) ahead, outside the SAMs' reach. Set it as a GBU-39 station's
target on the briefing TSD. Climb to 30,000 ft at 486 kt: the GBU-39 reaches 85 km, Rmax 76 km. When the caret drops
into the staple and `IN RNG` shows, release the four bombs of one rack (the stores page sets the ripple), and turn
away while they glide for about five minutes. A Ra'am carries five racks: 20 bombs.

### GBU-28: the laser-guided penetrator

**When**: a buried or hardened target. **Keys**: **[** until the GBU-28; hold **Space** to release.

The GBU-28 is 5.8 m long and carries 286 kg of explosive (the MK-84: 429 kg). It is a laser-guided bomb, but the
game's F-15 carries no targeting pod, so nothing designates for it: aim it as a bomb with the CDIP reticle. Its
guidance steers it onto the point the reticle showed at the release.

![A GBU-28 on the centreline in a 55° dive: the CDIP reticle on the target](shots/f15i/hud_gbu28.png)

1. **Reticle** on the bomb's impact point.
2. **Pipper**: on the target.
3. **Delivery mode**: CDIP.

**Example**: the picture. One GBU-28 on the centreline, a 55° dive about 3,900 ft above the ground at 404 kt
calibrated. Put the pipper on the target and press **Space**: the bomb leaves and steers onto that point. Pull out at
once: the jet is still heavy and the g limit is 8.1. In a shallower dive the impact point drops below the HUD: the
release is then delayed (`TREL`), as with the MK-82s.

## 8. Landing: the gear-down HUD

**When**: on final approach, gear handle down (**G**). **Keys**: **G** gear, **F** flaps, **B** speed brake in the air
and wheel brakes on the ground, **Ctrl+T** calls the tower near an Israeli base. The approach speeds and touchdown
limits are in section 4.

With the gear down the Mach and G windows go and the vertical velocity (`VV`) shows under the altitude. The ILS
(instrument landing system) to the nearest airbase's runway shows as a flight director: the steering window reads
`ILSN`, a vertical bar steers you onto the localizer, and a horizontal bar joins it once you reach the glide slope's
centre. Fly the velocity vector to the cross.

![Final approach, gear down: the F-15E HUD's landing windows](shots/f15i/landing_final.png)

1. **Velocity vector**: keep it on the runway threshold.
2. **AoA** (α), in degrees.
3. **Airspeed**, calibrated.
4. **Altitude**, barometric.
5. **Vertical velocity** (`VV`), in ft/min.
6. **Radar altitude** (R), in feet.
7. **Radar altimeter bar**: filled up to the radar altitude (labels in hundreds of feet).
8. **ILSN**: ILS steering, then the range and time to the runway's steerpoint.
9. **Localizer bar**: through the waterline symbol you are on the centre line; to one side, fly toward it.

**Example**: the picture is a frozen moment, 900 ft up, sinking 800 ft/min at 5° AoA. The jet flies a 3° path,
under the game's 5° glide slope: no horizontal bar yet. Hold altitude until it shows, then fly the cross. The stall is near 155 kt with full fuel: keep the approach above about
170 kt with 4,000 lb of fuel left, and raise the nose to slow down.

## 9. Better cockpit

**When**: any flight. **Keys**: tap **V** for the whole panel (hold it to glance down and back), **PgUp** / **PgDn**
slide it.

The Ra'am uses the F-15's panel. With Better cockpit on, the engine and fuel needles show the flight model's own
values, the fuel dial counts the fuel in pounds, and the MFD pages are drawn sharp.

- **RPM** (one dial per engine): the engine's real RPM, 60 % at idle, 100 % at military. It needs about 3 s from idle
  to military.
- **Throttle** (one dial per engine): military at the full mark; in AB it moves on past military.
- **Fuel needle**: all the fuel you carry, the drop tanks too, over the most the jet holds with them. It falls from
  the first pound burnt.
- **TOTAL LBS counter**: the fuel left, in pounds (`22665` full).
- **MFD pages**: the same pages, drawn at the screen's resolution.

**Example**: one engine's RPM falls to 0 while the other reads 100 %: that engine has cut out (damage). Watch the
counter: at 1,500 lb the HUD shows FUEL.

## 10. Night vision

**When**: night missions (the game's night is 20:00–05:30; mission 214 starts at 01:00), with Graphics → MORE →
Night vision = Goggles. **Keys**: **Shift+N** puts the goggles on and takes them off.

The goggles are ours: the 1998 game has none. They turn the outside view into a brightened green picture; the
cockpit and the HUD stay as you see them under the goggles. The real F-15I's cockpits are lit for night-vision
goggles (docs/f15i.md §4). Set Graphics → MORE → Night lights = Detailed too, for the moon, the stars and the lights
on the ground.

- The ground's relief and texture show, and the horizon is clear: fly by the outside view.
- The goggles set their own gain: the darker the scene, the more they brighten it, and the grainier it gets.
- The HUD shows in its brightest green while the goggles are on; your own level comes back when they come off.
- A view full of light (a close explosion, daylight) darkens the goggles: take them off by day.
- A bright light (a fire, a flare, a runway light) gets a halo around it.

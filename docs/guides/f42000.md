# Kurnass 2000 pilot guide (realistic settings)

This guide covers what changes in the Kurnass 2000 when you switch on the realistic options: the HUD (head-up display)
of the upgrade, the real F-4E's numbers and the real weapons. The Kurnass 2000 is the Israeli Air Force's (IAF) F-4E
Phantom 2000 upgrade, with the APG-76 radar, a wide-angle HUD and Elbit multi-function displays. Each HUD mode has its
own section: when to use it, the keys, a picture whose numbered yellow callouts the list under it explains, and a worked
example. The numbers come from the game's own data and tests. Section 15 explains the abbreviations.

**About this HUD**: the real Kurnass 2000 has a Kaiser / El-Op wide-angle holographic HUD driven by the Elbit ACE-3
mission computer. Its symbology is unpublished, so the game draws a reconstruction [R] in the style of an Elbit HUD of
the late 1980s: boxed digital readouts, a heading scale on top with a steering caret, a straight ladder.

## 1. What's different in this jet

| Area | The 1998 game | With the realistic options |
| --- | --- | --- |
| HUD | One simplified HUD for every jet: speed and altitude tapes with boxes, a heading tape at the top, text rows under the tapes | The Kurnass 2000's own layout [R]: boxed airspeed and altitude, a 30° heading scale with a steering caret to the waypoint, a straight-rung ladder with signed labels |
| Gun sight | A lead-computing pipper with a range arc | An **LCOS** (lead-computing optical sight) ring with a centre dot and a range arc inside the ring |
| IR missiles | A circle and a seeker cue | A missile circle with the seeker diamond and a launch-range scale |
| Radar missiles | A circle and a predicted point | A circle sized by the launch zone, the steering dot, the launch-range scale with Rmax / Rmin and the closure |
| Bombs | A pipper circle with a fall line | The CCIP pipper (a dot in a ring) with the fall line from the flight path marker; the mode window shows `LGB` for laser bombs |
| Flight data | The game's own F-4 | The F-4E (2 × J79-GE-17) that the upgrade kept: 12,060 lb internal fuel, +7.33 / −3 g, a drag chute with real drag |
| Weapon data | The game's own values | APG-76 radar out to 44 km, 639 gun rounds at 6,000 rounds/min, real missile weights, speeds and ranges |
| Physics | The original's quirks: still air, instant thrust | Real touchdown limits, spins with the mechanical flight controls, ground effect, the tower's wind, the engine's real spool-up |

![The 1998 HUD at the start of mission 231](shots/f42000/hud_original.png)

The same moment with the Real HUD is the first picture of section 3. The 1998 HUD shows ground speed (`485G`); the
Real HUD shows calibrated airspeed (427 kt).

**Voice and HUD warnings.** With the 1998 HUD, Betty (the cockpit voice) calls "Pull up" every 4 s in the A-G gun and
bomb modes while the dive is steeper than 1° per 100 ft above the ground (20° at 2,000 ft), and "Altitude" every 4 s
below 100 ft with the gear up. The HUD draws a large X in the same dive, and in place of the missile circle when the
locked target is inside the missile's minimum range (Rmin). With the Real HUD Betty's calls stay the same, and the HUD
draws no X (the real Kurnass 2000's marks are not public).

## 3. NAV: reading the HUD

**When**: cruising, navigating to a waypoint, and between attacks. **Keys**: **N** returns to NAV from any weapon
mode. **W** / **Shift+W** pick the next / previous waypoint. **H** changes the HUD colour.

![NAV at 10,000 ft and 427 kt, waypoint 1 ahead](shots/f42000/hud_nav.png)

1. **Airspeed**: calibrated airspeed in knots, in a box.
2. **Altitude** above sea level, in feet, in a box. Below 5,000 ft above the ground the radar altitude (`R700`)
   appears under it.
3. **g** (load factor).
4. **Mach** number.
5. **Mode**: `NAV`, `SRM`, `MRM`, `GUN`, `STRF`, `CCIP`, `LGB`, `TV` or `HARM`.
6. **Waypoint**: number and distance in nautical miles (NM): `WP1 4.7`.
7. **Time to the waypoint**, minutes:seconds.
8. **Heading**, boxed under the centre of a 30° scale with 5° tics.
9. **Steering caret**: a filled triangle above the scale at the waypoint's bearing. Turn until it sits over the box.
10. **Flight path marker**: where the jet is going.
11. **Pitch ladder**: the horizon line here. Climb rungs are solid, dive rungs dashed, with signed labels at both
    ends; the rungs stay straight.
12. **Waypoint diamond** on the ground; held at the HUD's edge it carries an X.
13. **Dive rung**: −5°.

**Example**: at the start of mission 231 waypoint 1 is 4.7 NM away and 35 s ahead. The caret sits over the heading box:
the waypoint is straight ahead. Its diamond sits at the lower edge with an X: it lies below the view. Lower the nose
until the diamond leaves the edge, then fly the flight path marker onto it.

When the fuel falls below 1,500 lb, **FUEL** appears at the bottom centre.

## 4. Flying the Kurnass 2000 with Real flight data

The Kurnass 2000 kept the F-4E's airframe and engines, so Flight data = Real gives it the F-4E row (the F-4E guide
lists the sources).

| Item | In the game | Real F-4E (the same airframe) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Empty weight | 30,330 lb | about 30,330 lb (Jane's) |
| Internal fuel | 12,060 lb | 1,855 gal = 12,060 lb (block 41+) |
| Thrust, military / full afterburner | 23,740 / 35,800 lbf | 2 × 11,870 / 2 × 17,900 lbf (J79-GE-17) |
| 1 g stall speed | 145 kt (the original's) | about 150–165 kt at 40,000 lb (uncertain) |
| g limits | +7.33 / −3 | +7.33 at 37,500 lb or less / −3 (T.O. 1F-4E-1) |
| Maximum roll rate | 150°/s (uncertain) | about 120–180°/s at 350–450 kt (uncertain) |
| Maximum speed, sea level / 40,000 ft | 780 kt (Mach 1.18) / 1,239 kt (Mach 2.16) | about 750 KIAS placard / Mach 2.2–2.23 |
| Climb at 350 kt, sea level | about 28,300 ft/min | 41,300 ft/min |
| Fuel flow, military / full afterburner | 20,000 / 71,000 lb/h | about 20,000 / about 71,000 lb/h |
| Service ceiling | 58,750 ft | 58,750 ft at maximum power (100 ft/min) |

What changes in the cockpit:

- **Fuel**: about 10 minutes at full afterburner, about 36 minutes at military power. The 3,700 lb tanks ("3700LB") go
  on stations 1, 5 and 9.
- **g**: 7.33 g at most; plan wider turns than in a 9 g fighter.
- **Mechanical flight controls** (Physics: Realistic spins): the stick moves the surfaces directly, so you own the
  limits. At low speed a hard pull stalls the wings and the jet can spin. **Recovery**: stick forward, rudder against
  the spin, then pull out of the dive.
- **Flight-path hold** (Physics): with the stick centred the jet holds its flight path.
- **The stick on the keyboard** gives full deflection at once, as in the original; a joystick is smoother.
- **Engine spool-up per real engine** (Physics): the thrust follows the J79's spool, slowest at first: 5 s from idle to
  military; the afterburner lights 1 s after the core reaches military and fills in 1.5 s. Out of afterburner the thrust
  drops at once. A J79-GE-17 test-cell chart gives 5 s from idle to military at best[^dcsj79]; the afterburner times are
  open.
- **Wind and turbulence** (Physics): the air moves with the tower's wind. The jet crabs into a crosswind and drifts
  downwind, a headwind shortens the take-off run, and light gusts shake it, strongest below 300 m (1,000 ft). The AI
  jets fly in the same air, bombs drift downwind (the bombing computer allows for it: the impact pipper moves downwind),
  and smoke, fire and trails drift with it. With a wind of 5 kt or more from behind the main runway the tower turns the
  base to the runway's other end: the starts, the tower's calls, the taxi signs, the autopilot, the AI and the ILS use
  that end; training missions keep their runway. Gameplay → No wind calms the air.
- **Nose dips under braking** (Physics): the nose dips up to 2° under the wheel brakes and squats up to 1° at full
  thrust on the brakes; it levels within about a second once the jet stops.
- **Unbalanced stores roll the jet** (Physics): stores left on one wing roll the jet toward it: hold that wing up with
  the stick, trim it out (**Alt+Left / Right**, or the throttle rocker with Devices → Throttle rocker = Roll trim),
  or let the autopilot (**A**) hold it while it flies. One 2,000 lb bomb on an
  outboard pylon, 4.1 m out, takes about 11 % stick at 290 kt and 1 g. It grows with the g in a pull, and at half the
  speed it takes four times as much.
- **The map edge**: 12 km (6.5 NM) before it the EndWorld warning sounds: turn back. Past it the game pushes the jet
  back for about 8 s (the nose rises and the jet turns).

### Take-off and landing numbers

| Item | Kurnass 2000 with Real flight data |
| --- | --- |
| Rotate | 145 kt (the game's take-off lesson) |
| Gear up below | 300 kt |
| Downwind / final | 200 kt at 2,000 ft above the ground / 165 kt (the game's landing lesson) |
| 1 g stall | 145 kt (the original's figure) |
| Touchdown limits (Real landing limits, Easy landing off) | sink below 4 m/s (790 ft/min), pitch below 15° |
| Drag chute (**Shift+B**) | the 16 ft ring-slot chute, with real drag; deploy below 200 KIAS (knots indicated airspeed) |

## 5. Radar and self-protection

With Weapon data = Real the AN/APG-76 detects at **44 km** (uncertain: 22–25 NM frontal, from one source), targets from
30 m above the ground, in line of sight.

- **RWR** (radar warning receiver): the RWR page shows emitters and missiles around you; a new threat sounds a tone, a
  missile launch at you the launch warning. With Extras → *RWR: Real* it also shows the SAM and AAA radars searching you
  before they lock: a steady symbol and the new-threat tone. Drop behind a ridge and the symbol goes.
- **Chaff (Insert)** and **flares (Delete)**: 120 chaff and 60 flares. A flare fools an IR missile with 33 % (50 % above
  4 g) while the afterburner is off.
- **ECM (J)** (electronic countermeasures): with the **ECM pod** on station 5 or 6, each radar-guided missile already
  flying at you is jammed with a 60 % chance. SAMs (surface-to-air missiles) keep guiding.
- **AWACS calls**: from 60 s into the flight the airborne controller calls the enemy aircraft within 35 NM, one scan
  every 12 s. "Alpha, bearing 090, 15 miles, target, heading 270, free to engage": the contact's bearing and distance
  from you, then its heading ("targets are already engaged" in place of "free to engage").
  "Unidentified contacts, please identify": your radar has not seen it yet; find it before you engage. A contact is
  called again, in short, after 60 s or when it moved more than 5 NM. Below 50 m (165 ft) the controller is silent.
- **Flight reports**: the radio reports your side's jets airborne, landed, crashed or ejected, and each kill 5 s after
  it ("Alpha leader shot down MiG 29").

## 6. SRM: AIM-9L / AIM-9M, Python 3

**When**: a target close ahead, best from behind. **Keys**: **]** until `SRM`, **Space** to fire. With a radar lock
(**R**, then **Return**) the seeker is slaved to the locked target. Looking aside (**Shift+arrows**, **F3** padlock)
shows the original game's helmet display, and the seeker takes the target you look at, inside the missile's cone
(Python 3 30°, AIM-9L 35°).

![A Python 3 locked on a MiG-23 0.9 NM ahead](shots/f42000/hud_srm.png)

1. **Missile circle**, centred in the HUD.
2. **Target box** on the radar target.
3. **Seeker diamond**: on the target while the seeker tracks it.
4. **SHOOT**: the seeker is locked and the range is inside the launch zone.
5. **Launch-range scale top**: the scale's range, 10 NM here.
6. **Rmin tick**; the Rmax tick sits higher, at 7 NM.
7. **Range caret** with the closure in knots (`490>`).
8. **Target range**: 0.9 NM.
9. **Mode**: `SRM`.

| Missile | Real data |
| --- | --- |
| AIM-9L / AIM-9M | 86 kg, Mach 2.5, 17.7 km |
| Python 3 | 120 kg, Mach 3.5, 15 km, 30° off the nose |

**Example**: the guide's MiG hangs still 1,700 m ahead, so the closure is your own 486 kt (`490>`). Against it the
Python 3's zone runs from 1.53 km to 13.0 km: at 1,700 m the caret sits just above Rmin, and closer than 1.53 km
**SHOOT** goes dark. A growl, then a high tone: **SHOOT** shows, fire. Beyond half its range the seeker sees a
jet with its afterburner lit, and a jet in dry power from closer in.

## 7. MRM: AIM-7M Sparrow

**When**: a target beyond the IR missiles' reach. **Keys**: radar on (**R**), lock (**Return** in LRS (long-range
search), or click its blip), **]** until `MRM`, **Space** to fire. Hold the lock until the hit.

![An AIM-7M locked on a MiG-23 8 NM ahead](shots/f42000/hud_mrm.png)

1. **Target box** on the locked target.
2. **Steering dot**: steer it into the circle.
3. **Missile circle**: it grows as you close from Rmax toward Rmin.
4. **SHOOT**: the dot is inside the circle and the range is inside the launch zone.
5. **Launch-range scale top**: 40 NM here.
6. **Rmax**: the missile's maximum range against this target.
7. **Rmin**: its minimum range.
8. **Range caret** with the closure (`490>`).
9. **Target range**: 8.0 NM.
10. **Mode**: `MRM`.

The AIM-7M (230 kg, Mach 4, 70 km) is **semi-active**: it homes on your radar's reflection, so it needs the lock until
it hits.

**Example**: lock the MiG-23 at 8 NM (14.8 km). The scale reads 40 NM at its top; Rmax sits near the top at 36 NM
(67.3 km) against this still target, Rmin at the bottom at 0.8 NM (1.5 km). The caret at 8 NM sits low on the scale,
well inside the zone, so the circle is close to its largest. The dot is in the circle and **SHOOT** shows: fire, then
keep the target ahead. **Q**, **R**, **S**, **Return**, a click or **Backspace** breaks the lock and the Sparrow's
guidance.

## 8. A-A gun: LCOS

**When**: a target inside about 3,000 ft. **Keys**: **]** to the gun (`GUN`), **Tab** fires. The M61A1 has 639
rounds at 6,000 rounds/min: about 6 s of firing.

![The LCOS sight on a MiG-23 0.4 NM ahead](shots/f42000/hud_gun.png)

1. **LCOS ring**: 25 mr (milliradian) around the aim point.
2. **Pipper dot**: the lead-computed aim point. Put it on the target and fire.
3. **Range arc** inside the ring: a full turn is 12,000 ft, from 12 o'clock clockwise.
4. **Target box** on the radar target.
5. **Target range**: 0.4 NM.
6. **Mode**: `GUN`.
7. **Gun cross**: the gun line, shown in every weapon mode.

**Example**: the MiG is 800 m (2,600 ft) ahead. The range arc runs from 12 o'clock to about 2:30 (2,600 of 12,000 ft).
The pipper sits above the target: the sight leads it for the bullets' drop. Pull the dot onto the MiG and hold **Tab**
for a 1 s burst (100 rounds).

## 9. A-G gun: STRF

**When**: strafing a ground target. **Keys**: **[** to the gun (`STRF`), **Tab** fires.

![STRF in a 20° dive at 1,970 ft above the ground](shots/f42000/hud_strf.png)

1. **Strafe pipper** (a dot in a ring): where the rounds hit. The target sits under it here.
2. **Radar altitude**: 1,970 ft.
3. **Mode**: `STRF`.
4. **Dive rung**: −20°, level with the flight path marker: a 20° dive.

**Example**: dive at 20° and 414 kt. With the pipper on the target at 1,970 ft above the ground, fire a short burst
and pull out: the ground comes up at about 240 ft/s.

## 10. Bombs and rockets: CCIP

**When**: MK-82 / MK-83 / MK-84 / M117, the CBU-87 / CBU-97 clusters and ZUNNI rockets. **Keys**: **[** until
`CCIP`; hold **Space** to release. The stores page sets the ripple (default 2 bombs, 10 m apart).

![CCIP in a 45° dive at 2,300 ft above the ground](shots/f42000/hud_ccip.png)

1. **CCIP pipper** (continuously computed impact point): a dot in a ring where the bomb lands now. A hill between you
   and the impact takes the pipper.
2. **Bomb fall line**: from the flight path marker to the pipper. Keep the target on it.
3. **Flight path marker**.
4. **Mode**: `CCIP`.
5. **Radar altitude**: 2,300 ft.
6. **Dive rung**: −45°.

**Example**: six MK-82s on the centreline, a 45° dive at 413 kt, 2,300 ft above the ground. Roll in with the target
above the marker, let the fall line carry it down, and press **Space** when it reaches the pipper. The game's training
warns against GP (general-purpose) bombs below 2,000 ft above the ground.

**Delayed release**: when the impact point lies below the HUD's lower edge, put the target under the pipper held at
the edge and hold **Space**; the bombs go at 0.9 s to go (positive g, less than 90° of bank).

## 11. Laser bombs, TV weapons and HARM

These use the MFD (multi-function display) pages of the original game. The Kurnass 2000 has two displays: the left one
starts on the radar, the right one on the RWR (radar warning receiver). The HUD adds a diamond at the TV weapon's or
the HARM's target (`TV` / `HARM` in the mode window).

### Laser bombs: MK-82L, MK-83L, MK-84L (LGB)

**Keys**: the FLIR (forward-looking infrared) pod on station 4; **[** to the laser bomb brings up the FLIR page and
`LGB` in the mode window. **Ctrl+arrows** slew the camera, **Numpad +/−** zoom, **L** the laser, **Space** releases.

![The FLIR page, laser on](shots/f42000/mfd_flir.png)

1. **LASER ON**: the bomb flies to the camera's centre point.
2. **WIDE / SPOT** field of view (a page button switches it).
3. **Zoom** (×1 here) with its arrows.
4. **Centre cross**: the laser spot. Release the slew keys and the camera holds this point.
5. **Range** to the point: 3.1 NM.

**Example**: slew the cross onto the target, release the keys, press **L**, and release in a dive or level. The bomb
steers to the spot when the spot is on the ground within 60° of its path; with the laser off it falls as a plain bomb
on the CCIP pipper.

### TV weapons: AGM-65 Maverick, AGM-62, GBU-15, Popeye

![The TV page of an AGM-65](shots/f42000/mfd_tv.png)

1. **TV**: the weapon's camera picture.
2. **RDY**: ready to fire.
3. **Zoom**.
4. **Aim cross**: the weapon's aim point.

**Example**: the Maverick (209 kg, beyond 27 km) homes by itself after **Space**. The AGM-62, GBU-15 and Popeye fly
where the camera's centre points: keep the cross on the target until the hit (`TRA`, then `TER` near the aim). **Z**
makes the picture full screen.

### HARM: AGM-88

![The HARM page with a radar site in range](shots/f42000/mfd_harm.png)

1. **HARM**: the page.
2. **IN RANGE**: the selected site is inside the HARM's range.
3. **Target**: the radar site's symbol.

**Example**: the HARM (360 kg, Mach 2, 150 km) lists the radar sites within ±15° of the nose out to 20 NM, nearest
first. **Ctrl+Return** steps through them. Fire (**Space**) with the HUD diamond near the centre.

## 12. Landing: the gear-down HUD

**When**: on final approach, gear handle down (**G**). **Keys**: **G** gear, **F** flaps, **B** wheel brakes,
**Shift+B** drag chute, **Ctrl+T** calls the tower near an Israeli base. The approach speeds and touchdown limits are in
section 4.

With the gear down the boxes and the left column move down a row, and the left column adds the AoA (angle of attack).
The ILS (instrument landing system) lines guide you to the nearest airbase's runway.

![Final approach, 3.6 NM out at about 700 ft, 141 kt, 5° AoA](shots/f42000/landing_final.png)

1. **Flight path marker**: on the runway threshold here.
2. **ILS glide-slope line**: it crosses the localizer line at the HUD's centre when you are on the runway's centre
   line and on a 5° glide path. It drops when you are high.
3. **ILS localizer line**, held at the HUD's left edge.
4. **AoA** in degrees: `AOA5`.
5. **Radar altitude**: about 700 ft above the ground.
6. **Steering caret held at the scale's end** with the angle still to turn: waypoint 1 is 104° to the right.
7. **Waypoint diamond**, held at the edge with an X.
8. **Climb rung**: +5°.
9. **Airspeed**: 141 kt.

**Example**: here the jet flies a 3° path at 5° AoA and 141 kt. The glide-slope line sits above the HUD's centre: you
are under the 5° path. The localizer line waits at the left edge: the line it measures lies to the left. Keep the
marker on the threshold, touch down, and below 200 kt pull the drag chute (**Shift+B**).

## 13. Better cockpit

**When**: any flight. **Keys**: tap **V** for the whole panel (hold it to glance down and back), **PgUp** / **PgDn**
slide it.

With Better cockpit on, the engine and fuel needles show the flight model's own values (in the 1998 game they read rough
values), and the MFD pages are drawn sharp. The left column of engine dials is the left engine, the right column the
right engine. Each needle already sits on the dial its label names (RPM on PERCENT RPM, the temperature on EXH TEMP, the
throttle on FUEL FLOW), so "Gauges on their labelled dials" changes nothing here. The needles follow their values with a
0.3 s instrument lag.

![The panel with Better cockpit on](shots/f42000/better_panel.png)

1. **Fuel needle**: all the fuel you carry, the drop tanks too, over the most the jet holds with them. It falls from
   the first pound burnt (in the 1998 game it stays full until the tanks are empty).
2. **RPM**: the engine's real RPM, 60 % at idle, 100 % at military. It needs about 5 s from idle to military (3 s with
   Engine spool-up off).
3. **Exhaust temperature**: about 450 °C at idle and 650 °C at military, a little more in AB; 900 °C with an engine
   fire. It rises in about 2 s and falls slowly.
4. **Throttle** (the dials marked FUEL FLOW): military at the full mark; in AB it moves on past military, further in
   AB 2.
5. **MFD pages**: the same pages, drawn at the screen's resolution. The TV and FLIR pictures stay as they are.

**Example**: in AB both throttle needles stand past military and both temperatures a little above 650 °C. One
temperature needle at 900 °C means a fire in that engine.

## 14. Night vision

**When**: night missions (the game's night is 20:00–05:30; mission 214 starts at 01:00), with Graphics → MORE →
Night vision = Goggles. **Keys**: **Shift+N** puts the goggles on and takes them off.

The goggles are ours: the 1998 game has none. They turn the outside view into a brightened green picture; the
cockpit and the HUD stay as you see them under the goggles. Set Graphics → MORE → Night lights = Detailed too, for
the moon, the stars and the lights on the ground.

![01:00 without goggles](shots/f42000/night.png)

![The same moment through the goggles](shots/f42000/night_nvg.png)

- The ground's relief and texture show, and the horizon is clear: fly by the outside view.
- The goggles set their own gain: the darker the scene, the more they brighten it, and the grainier it gets.
- The HUD shows in its brightest green while the goggles are on; your own level comes back when they come off.
- The Kurnass 2000's panel lighting does not suit goggles: its glow washes out the picture's lower part and lowers
  the gain.
- A view full of light (a close explosion, daylight) darkens the goggles: take them off by day.
- The goggles follow the mission's year: none before 1972, a grainy Gen II up to 1981, a Gen III from 1982, the
  sharpest from 1996 (the 1998 missions).
- A bright light (a fire, a flare, a runway light) gets a halo around it.
- The picture darkens toward its edges.

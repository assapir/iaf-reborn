# F-16 pilot guide (realistic settings)

This guide covers what changes in the F-16D Brakeet when you switch on the realistic options: the HUD (head-up display)
of the real F-16, the helmet sight, the real jet's numbers and the real weapons. Each HUD mode has its own section: when
to use it, the keys, a picture whose numbered yellow callouts the list under it explains, and a worked example. The
numbers come from the game's own data and tests. Section 16 explains the abbreviations.

---

## 1. What's different in this jet

| Area | The 1998 game | With the realistic options |
| --- | --- | --- |
| HUD | One simplified HUD for every jet: boxed speed and altitude, a heading tape at the top, three text rows each side | The F-16C HUD from the USAF manual (T.O. 1F-16CJ-34-1-1): moving tapes, a bending pitch ladder, data windows, a roll scale |
| Gun sight | A lead-computing pipper with a range arc | **EEGS** (enhanced envelope gun sight): a funnel while searching; a T-symbol, a pipper and a range circle with a lock |
| IR missiles | A circle and a seeker cue | The AIM-9 reticle, the seeker diamond, the target-aspect triangle and the DLZ (dynamic launch zone) |
| Radar missiles | A circle and a predicted point | The **ASEC** (allowable steering error circle), the steering dot and the DLZ with Rmax / Rmin |
| Bombs | A pipper circle with a fall line | The F-16's CCIP: a 1 mr dot in a 12 mr circle, the fall line from the flight path marker, the heading scale at the top; delayed: a time delay cue, then a steering line and a solution cue |
| Looking aside | The original's helmet repeater | The Elbit **DASH** helmet display: aim a Python 4 by turning your head |
| Flight data | The game's own F-16 | F-16D Block 30/40 (F110-GE-100): 5,800 lb internal fuel (the original 7,000 lb), 280°/s roll, 118 kt stall |
| Weapon data | The game's own values | APG-68 radar out to 80 km, 511 gun rounds at 6,000 rounds/min, real missile weights, speeds and ranges |
| Physics | The original's quirks: still air, instant thrust | The F-16's deep stall, real touchdown limits, ground effect, a flight-path hold with the stick centred, the tower's wind, the engine's real spool-up |

![The 1998 HUD at the start of mission 231](shots/f16/hud_original.png)

The same moment with the Real HUD is the first picture of section 3.

**Voice and HUD warnings.** With the 1998 HUD, Betty (the cockpit voice) calls "Pull up" every 4 s in the A-G gun and
bomb modes while the dive is steeper than 1° per 100 ft above the ground (20° at 2,000 ft), and "Altitude" every 4 s
below 100 ft with the gear up. The HUD draws a large X in the same dive, and in place of the missile circle when the
locked target is inside the missile's minimum range (Rmin). With the Real HUD you get the F-16's own warnings: in every
mode a flashing X and one "Pull up" when the ground clearance advisory predicts that a 4 g pull-up after the pilot's
reaction only just clears the ground (a 45° dive at 413 kt: about 2,270 ft; a 20° strafing dive at 414 kt: about 510
ft), with a U under the flight path marker in the A-G modes that rises to it over the last 10 s before; "Altitude"
every 4 s below 300 ft with the gear up, and `AL 300` under the radar altitude flashing; a steady X in SRM / MRM inside
Rmin.

## 3. NAV: reading the HUD

**When**: cruising, navigating to a steerpoint, and between attacks. **Keys**: **N** returns to
NAV from any weapon mode. **W** / **Shift+W** pick the next / previous steerpoint. **H** changes the HUD colour.

![NAV at 10,000 ft and 427 kt, steerpoint 1 ahead](shots/f16/hud_nav.png)

1. **Airspeed**: calibrated airspeed in knots on a moving tape (labels in tens: `45` = 450 kt). The `C` beside it
   means calibrated.
2. **Current g** (load factor).
3. **Altitude** above sea level, in feet (barometric). Tape labels in hundreds: `10,5` = 10,500 ft.
4. **Heading**, boxed, on a scale with 5° tics. In NAV it sits low in the HUD; in the bombing modes and with the gear
   down it moves to the top. A short tic under the scale marks the steerpoint's bearing.
5. **Roll scale**: tics at 10°, 20°, 30° and 45°; the triangle shows your bank.
6. **Flight path marker**: where the jet is going. Put it on a point and the jet flies there.
7. **Boresight cross**: where the nose points (the gun line).
8. **Pitch ladder**: the horizon line here. The bars above it are solid, the bars below dashed, and they bend toward
   the horizon as the pitch grows; a star marks straight up, an earth symbol straight down.
9. **Mach** number.
10. **Maximum g** of the flight.
11. **Mode**: `NAV`. With a weapon it shows the weapon (`2 SRM`, `2 MRM`, `EEGS`, `STRF`, `CCIP`, `PRE`, `HARM`), with
    `ARM` above it.
12. **Radar altitude**: your height above the ground (`R 9,850`), up to 50,000 ft. Under it `AL 300`, the low
    altitude warning height (ALOW, 300 ft): it flashes below that height, unless you climb faster than 1,200 ft/min.
13. **Slant range**: `B` to the steerpoint, `F` to the radar target, in nautical miles (NM).
14. **Time to the steerpoint**, minutes:seconds.
15. **Distance > number** of the steerpoint: `005>01` = 5 NM to steerpoint 1.
16. **Steerpoint diamond**: drawn on the ground at the steerpoint. Held at the HUD's edge, it carries an X: turn or
    pitch toward it.

**Example**: the picture is the start of mission 231. Steerpoint 1 is 4.7 NM away (`B04.7`) and 35 s ahead
(`00:35`). The diamond sits at the bottom edge with an X, and the bearing tic sits under the heading box: the
steerpoint is straight ahead and below. Lower the nose until the diamond leaves the edge, then put the flight path
marker on it.

When the fuel falls below the bingo, **FUEL** appears in the centre window under the boresight cross. The bingo is
1,500 lb; Preferences → Extras → Real HUD bingo sets it (500–5,000 lb), as the real pilot does.

## 4. Flying the F-16 with Real flight data

These are the game's values with Flight data = Real, checked by its validation test against public F-16C/D figures.

| Item | In the game | Real F-16D |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Empty weight | 19,100 lb | 18,900–19,100 lb |
| Internal fuel | 5,800 lb | 870 US gal ≈ 5,800 lb |
| Thrust, military / full afterburner | 17,400 / 29,000 lbf | 17,000 / 28,900 lbf (F110-GE-100) |
| 1 g stall speed, sea level | 118 kt | about 120–130 kt |
| Corner speed (9 g) | about 355 kt | about 330–350 kt |
| g limit | 9 g up to 25,000 ft, less above | 9 g |
| Maximum roll rate | 280°/s (0 to 90° in 0.55 s) | 240–308°/s |
| Maximum speed, sea level / 40,000 ft | 782 kt (Mach 1.18) / 1,079 kt (Mach 1.88) | about 795 kt / 1,150 kt |
| Climb at 350 kt, sea level, full afterburner | about 41,000 ft/min | about 50,000 ft/min |
| Instantaneous turn, 420 kt at 10,000 ft | 21.7°/s at 8.1 g | 20–26°/s at 9 g |
| Fuel flow, military / full afterburner | about 11,000 / 59,000 lb/h | 9,000–12,000 / 50,000–65,000 lb/h |
| Service ceiling | 50,000 ft | above 50,000 ft |

What changes in the cockpit:

- **Fuel**: about 6 minutes at full afterburner, about 31 minutes at military power at sea level. **FUEL** shows
  below the bingo (1,500 lb unless you set it: Preferences → Extras → Real HUD bingo).
- **Turns**: fight near 355 kt, the speed where the jet turns hardest at 9 g. Above it the g limit caps the turn;
  below it the wing runs out of lift.
- **Flight-path hold** (Physics): with the stick centred the jet holds its flight path at every speed, like the F-16's
  fly-by-wire.
- **Deep stall** (Physics: Realistic spins, Fly-by-wire deep stall): zoom 30° or more nose-high until the speed is
  gone and the jet hangs at about 60° AoA, the nose rocking near the horizon, sinking about 11,000 ft/min. Holding
  the stick forward keeps it there. **Recovery**: rock the stick in phase with the nose, pull while it rises and push
  while it falls, as with the real jet's manual pitch override. After a few cycles (about 8 s and 300 m) the nose falls
  through and the controls take over; pull out of the dive.
- **Ground effect** (Physics): a longer float in the flare. **Real idle thrust on the ground**: at idle the jet taxis at
  about 35–45 kt (30 kt in the real F-16).
- **The stick on the keyboard** gives full deflection at once, as in the original; a joystick is smoother.
- **Engine spool-up per real engine** (Physics): the thrust follows the F110's spool, slowest at first: 3 s from idle to
  military; the afterburner lights 0.5 s after the core reaches military and fills in 2 s. Out of afterburner the thrust
  drops at once. No F110 figure is public, so the game uses the F100's (open)[^tm4320].
- **Wind and turbulence** (Physics): the air moves with the tower's wind. The jet crabs into a crosswind and drifts
  downwind, a headwind shortens the take-off run, and light gusts shake it, strongest below 300 m (1,000 ft). The AI
  jets fly in the same air, bombs drift downwind (the bombing computer allows for it: the impact pipper moves downwind),
  and smoke, fire and trails drift with it. With a wind of 5 kt or more from behind the main runway the tower turns the
  base to the runway's other end: the starts, the tower's calls, the taxi signs, the autopilot, the AI and the ILS use
  that end; training missions keep their runway. Gameplay → No wind calms the air.
- **Nose dips under braking** (Physics): the nose dips up to 2° under the wheel brakes and squats up to 1° at full
  thrust on the brakes; it levels within about a second once the jet stops.
- **Unbalanced stores roll the jet** (Physics): stores left on one wing roll the jet toward it: hold that wing up with
  the stick, trim it out (**Alt+Left / Right**, or the throttle rocker with Devices → Throttle rocker = Roll trim), or
  let the autopilot (**A**) hold it while it flies. One 2,000 lb bomb 3 m out: hands off, the flight controls feed the
  roll rate back and slow the drift to about 18°/s toward the heavy wing. At 290 kt and 1 g about 10 % stick holds it
  (Original flight data 14 %); full rocker trims that in 1 s. It grows with the g in a pull (four times the stick at 4
  g) and as the speed falls: on a 165 kt final it takes 32 % stick (Original 45 %), the drift is 31°/s and the trim
  takes 3 s. The release order keeps a symmetric load balanced: after a release the next store of that weapon comes from
  the station farthest from the last one, and the first press takes the selected station, usually the left wing's outer
  one. In pairs (the stores page's ripple quantity 2, the default) three bombs per wing go left outer + right outer,
  left middle + right middle, left inner + right inner, a fraction of a second apart: level after every pair. Single
  drops leave the jet unbalanced after every odd one, and the next press takes the other wing. When the load is even
  again the roll stops at once, but the trim stays where you set it: take it back off (**Alt** + the other arrow, or the
  rocker) until the `TRIM L / R` reading on the **Ctrl+F12** info line is gone.
- **Bomb release: Real** (Extras): bombs leave the way the real stores management releases them. Stores page OSB 7
  (bottom row, second from left) switches PAIR / SGL; the page shows it as `1 PAIR` or `1 SGL`. The number is the
  release pulses per press and starts at 1. In PAIR each pulse drops one bomb off each wing at the same instant,
  outboard pair first, so the jet stays balanced: six bombs on six stations go in three presses, or set 3 to drop
  them all in one. In SGL each pulse drops one bomb, alternating wings, left first. Rockets work as before.
- **The map edge**: 12 km (6.5 NM) before it the EndWorld warning sounds: turn back. Past it the game pushes the jet
  back for about 8 s (the nose rises and the jet turns).

## 5. Radar and self-protection

With Weapon data = Real the AN/APG-68 detects at **80 km (43 NM)** in LRS and STT (single-target track), in a ±60°
cone, targets from 30 m above the ground, in line of sight. The left MFD shows it.

| Key | Action |
| --- | --- |
| **R** / **Q** / **S** | Radar on, A-A (air-to-air) ↔ A-G (air-to-ground) / mode (LRS → TWS → ACM; GMT ↔ MAP) / standby |
| **.** / **,** | Range scale up / down (5, 10, 20, 40, 80 NM) |
| **Return** / **Shift+Return** | Next / previous contact (in LRS this locks it: STT) |
| **Backspace** | Drop the lock |
| **\\** (hold) | Boresight: a ±12° lock cone straight ahead |

- **ACM** (air combat mode) and **BORE** (boresight) lock the nearest contact out to 10 NM. A lock tells the target it
  is locked (its RWR sounds).
- **RWR** (radar warning receiver): the RWR page shows emitters and missiles around you; a new threat sounds a tone, a
  missile launch at you the launch warning. With Extras → *RWR: Real* it also shows the SAM and AAA radars searching you
  before they lock: a steady symbol and the new-threat tone. Drop behind a ridge and the symbol goes.
- **Chaff (Insert)** and **flares (Delete)**: 90 chaff and 60 flares. A flare fools an IR missile with 33 % (50 %
  above 4 g) while the afterburner is off: pull hard, come out of afterburner, then flare.
- **ECM (J)** (electronic countermeasures), built in: each radar-guided missile already flying at you is jammed with a
  60 % chance. SAMs (surface-to-air missiles) keep guiding.
- **AWACS calls**: from 60 s into the flight the airborne controller calls the enemy aircraft within 35 NM, one scan
  every 12 s. "Alpha, bearing 090, 15 miles, target, heading 270, free to engage": the contact's bearing and distance
  from you, then its heading ("targets are already engaged" in place of "free to engage").
  "Unidentified contacts, please identify": your radar has not seen it yet; find it before you engage. A contact is
  called again, in short, after 60 s or when it moved more than 5 NM. Below 50 m (165 ft) the controller is silent.
- **Flight reports**: the radio reports your side's jets airborne, landed, crashed or ejected, and each kill 5 s after
  it ("Alpha leader shot down MiG 29").

### The real MFDs

Set Extras **MFDs (F-16)** to **Real**. Each MFD then shows the real F-16's formats in place of the 1998 pages:

| Format | Shows | Stands for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| FCR | the radar: targets, the lock's data line, the launch zone; GM / GMT on the ground | the radar page |
| SMS | the stores: the inventory (OSB INV) or the selected weapon's page | the stores page |
| HSD | the route, the SAM rings and the radar's scan around your jet | the TSD |
| WPN | the Maverick's picture, the HARM's threats, a GPS bomb's target and times | the TV and HARM pages |
| TGP | the targeting pod's picture | the FLIR page |
| TEST | the failed systems | the damage page |

- **The bottom row** of every MFD: SWAP (trade the two MFDs), the MFD's three formats (the one shown in a box: click
  another to show it, click the boxed one for the menu of all formats), and DCLT (hide the labels).
- **Ranges**: the triangles at the top left step the FCR's and the HSD's range (15, 30, 60, 120 NM).
- **GPS bombs**: on the SMS and WPN, the button labelled PRE / VIS switches the mode (as K does); the target shows as
  STPT and its number, with its range and bearing, TMR or IN RNG, and TTI after the release.
- The waypoint list, the RWR page and the ADI page are not offered: the real F-16 shows them elsewhere.

## 6. DASH: aiming by looking

**When**: a target well off the nose, a Python 4 or AIM-9 selected. **Keys**: look aside with **Shift+arrows**,
**Numpad** snap views or padlock (**F3**: the view follows the radar target). The DASH appears once the panel pans
away.

The DASH (Display And Sight Helmet, Elbit) is the IAF's (Israeli Air Force) helmet display, fielded with the Python 4
in 1993–94. It shows a 20° circle of green symbols on the visor. Its layout is public in outline; the game draws the
symbols of its descendant, the JHMCS (Joint Helmet-Mounted Cueing System), as a reconstruction [R].

![Looking 40° left at a locked MiG-23 with a Python 4](shots/f16/dash.png)

1. **Aiming cross**: where your head points.
2. **Line-of-sight circle** around the cross.
3. **Gap** in the circle: it points where you look from the nose. A gap at 9 o'clock: looking left.
4. **Target box**: the radar target.
5. **Seeker diamond**: what the missile's seeker tracks.
6. **Heading your head looks at**: 230° here, 40° left of the jet's 270°.
7. **g**.
8. **Airspeed**.
9. **ARM**: weapons live.
10. **Weapon**: two short-range missiles.
11. **Altitude**.
12. **Steerpoint**: distance > number.

**Example**: a MiG-23 is 40° left, 3,000 m away. Lock it on the radar (**Return**), select the Python 4 (**]**) and
look at it (**F3**). The seeker follows your head inside the Python 4's 60° cone (Python 3 30°, AIM-9L 35°), and the
diamond settles in the box. Fire (**Space**) on the high tone.

## 7. SRM: AIM-9L, Python 3, Python 4

**When**: a target inside about 10 NM, best from behind. **Keys**: **]** until `SRM`, **Space** to fire. With a
radar lock (**R**, then **Return**) the seeker is slaved to the locked target.

![An AIM-9L locked on a MiG-23 1.6 NM ahead](shots/f16/hud_srm.png)

1. **AIM-9 reticle**: a circle 3° below the boresight cross.
2. **Target box** on the radar target.
3. **Seeker diamond**: on the target while the seeker tracks it.
4. **Target-aspect triangle**: it rides the reticle; at 6 o'clock you look at the target's tail.
5. **SHOOT**: the seeker is locked and the range is inside the DLZ.
6. **Range caret** with the closure in knots (`490>`).
7. **DLZ scale**: its top is the scale's range (10 NM here). The two ticks on it are the missile's Rmax and Rmin.
8. **Mode**: two SRMs left.
9. **Slant range** to the target: 1.6 NM.

| Missile | Real data |
| --- | --- |
| AIM-9L | 86 kg, Mach 2.5, 17.7 km |
| Python 3 | 120 kg, Mach 3.5, 15 km, 30° off the nose |
| Python 4 | 120 kg, Mach 3.5, 15 km, 60° off the nose |

**Example**: the MiG is 3,000 m (1.6 NM) ahead. The guide's MiG holds still, so the closure is your own 486 kt,
rounded to `490>`. Against it the AIM-9L's Rmax is 16.1 km (8.7 NM) and its Rmin 1.4 km (0.77 NM): the caret sits low
on the 10 NM scale, between the two ticks. A growl, then a high tone: the seeker is locked, **SHOOT** shows, fire.
The seeker locks from behind out to 11 km, head-on out to 3 km, or 8.5 km against a lit afterburner: inside the zone
but outside the lock there is no SHOOT.

### AIM-9X and Python 5 (modern weapons)

With Extras → Weapon data = Real, and F-16 version = Sufa or Extras → Weapons = Modern on the Barak, the Arming
screen's AA list adds the **AIM-9X** and the **PYTH-5** (stations 1–3, 7–9).
They fly like the other SRMs, with three differences:

| Missile | Real data | Off the nose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| AIM-9X Block II | 85 kg, Mach 2.5, about 18 km | 90° |
| Python 5 | 105 kg, Mach 4, over 20 km | 100° |

1. **Wide seeker**: look at the target with the DASH (**Shift+arrows** or the **Numpad** snap views, §6) and the
   seeker locks it up to 90° (AIM-9X) or 100° (Python 5) off the nose. A radar lock slaves it too, and **F3** turns
   your head onto it, but only inside the radar's ±60°.
2. **Turn off the rail**: the missile turns at once toward a target beside or behind your wing line.
3. **Lock-on after launch**: fire without a lock and the missile flies along your line of sight (the nose, or where
   you look in the DASH) and locks the first target it finds near that line.

![Looking 90° left at a MiG-23 with the Python 5](shots/f16/dash_9x.png)

1. **Aiming cross**: where your head points.
2. **Seeker diamond**: the Python 5's seeker on the MiG.
3. **Heading your head looks at**: 180°, 90° left of the jet's 270°.
4. **ARM**: weapons live.
5. **Weapon**: two short-range missiles.

**Example**: a MiG-23 is 90° left (off your wing), 3 km out, outside the radar's ±60°, so **F3** can't help. Select
the Python 5 (**]**) and look left (**Shift+Left**, or the left **Numpad** snap view). The diamond settles on the MiG
and the tone rises. Fire (**Space**): the Python 5 turns hard left off the rail and hits. No radar lock is needed.
An AIM-9X would need the MiG inside 90°: turn 10° toward it first. With HUD = Real the DASH adds **SHOOT** under the
cross once the seeker locks in range, **LOAL** flashing while a missile fired without a lock searches, and a crossed-out
diamond on the edge when you look beyond the missile's reach.

## 8. MRM: AMRAAM and AIM-7M

**When**: a target beyond the IR missiles' reach. **Keys**: radar on (**R**), lock (**Return** in LRS (long-range
search), or click its blip), **]** until `MRM`, **Space** to fire.

![An AMRAAM locked on a MiG-23 8 NM ahead](shots/f16/hud_mrm.png)

1. **Target box** on the locked target.
2. **Steering dot** (attack steering cue): steer it into the circle.
3. **ASEC**: the allowable steering error circle. It grows as you close from Rmax toward Rmin.
4. **Target-aspect triangle** on the circle.
5. **SHOOT**: the dot is inside the circle and the range is inside the DLZ.
6. **DLZ scale top**: 40 NM here.
7. **Rmax**: the missile's maximum range against this target.
8. **Rmin**: its minimum range.
9. **Range caret** with the closure (`490>`).
10. **Slant range**: `F08.0` = 8.0 NM to the radar target.
11. **Mode**: two MRMs left.

| Missile | Real data |
| --- | --- |
| AMRAAM (AIM-120A/B) | 157 kg, Mach 4, 70 km, **active**: it guides itself after the launch |
| AIM-7M Sparrow | 230 kg, Mach 4, 70 km, **semi-active**: it needs your lock until it hits |

**Example**: lock the MiG-23 at 8 NM (14.8 km) with the AMRAAM selected. The DLZ shows Rmax at the upper tick, 36 NM
(67.3 km) against this still target, and Rmin at the lower tick, 1.2 NM (2.2 km). The caret at 8 NM sits low on the
scale, a little above Rmin: well inside the zone, so the circle is close to its largest. The dot is in the circle and
**SHOOT** shows: fire. A target flying at you at 250 m/s reaches farther: 81.9 km, because it flies toward the missile.
With a Sparrow, hold the lock until the hit: **Q**, **R**, **S**, **Return**, a click or **Backspace** breaks its
guidance.

## 9. A-A gun: EEGS

**When**: a target inside about 3,000 ft. **Keys**: **]** to the gun (`EEGS`), **Tab** fires. The M61A1 has 511
rounds at 6,000 rounds/min: about 5 s of firing.

### Searching

![EEGS searching: the funnel and the MRGS lines](shots/f16/hud_eegs_search.png)

1. **Funnel top**: the bullets' path at 600 ft. The funnel's width at any height is a fighter's wingspan at that
   range.
2. **Funnel bottom**: 3,000 ft.
3. **MRGS lines** (multiple reference gunsight lines): drawn for a target 1.5 wingspans long, they sweep toward the
   centre once a second.
4. **Flight path marker**.
5. **Mode**: `EEGS`. The heading scale and the roll scale leave the HUD in this mode.

Fly the target into the funnel until its wings touch both lines, then fire.

### With a radar lock

![EEGS locked on a MiG-23 at 2,600 ft](shots/f16/hud_eegs.png)

1. **Funnel**, lengthened to 6,000 ft.
2. **T-symbol**: the bar across the funnel at the target's range.
3. **1 g pipper** (`+`) in the middle of the T-bar.
4. **4 mr pipper** (milliradian): the lead-computed aim point. Put it on the target and fire.
5. **Range arc** of the target circle: 1,000 ft per clock hour. It is full beyond 2 NM and unwinds inside.
6. **Maximum effective range dot** at 3 o'clock: 3,000 ft.
7. **Slant range**: `F 026` = 2,600 ft (below 1 NM the window counts hundreds of feet).
8. **Closure**: 486 kt.
9. **Mode**: `EEGS`.

**Example**: the MiG is 800 m (2,600 ft) ahead. The range arc ends between 2 and 3 o'clock, short of the 3 o'clock
dot: inside the effective range. Put the 4 mr pipper on the MiG and hold **Tab** for a 1 s burst (100 rounds).

## 10. A-G gun: STRF

**When**: strafing a ground target. **Keys**: **[** to the gun (`STRF`), **Tab** fires.

![STRF in a 20° dive at 1,970 ft above the ground](shots/f16/hud_strf.png)

1. **Strafe pipper** (a dot in a 12 mr circle): where the rounds hit. The target sits under it here.
2. **Heading**: it stays low in STRF.
3. **Mode**: `STRF`.
4. **Radar altitude**: 1,970 ft.

**Example**: dive at 20° and 414 kt. With the pipper on the target at 1,970 ft above the ground, fire a short burst
and pull out: at 20° and 414 kt the ground comes up at about 240 ft/s.

## 11. Bombs and rockets: CCIP

**When**: MK-82 / MK-83 / MK-84 / M117, the CBU-87 / CBU-97 clusters and ZUNNI rockets. **Keys**: **[** until
`CCIP`; hold **Space** to release. The stores page sets the ripple: quantity 1–14, interval 10–200 (default 2 bombs,
10 m apart).

![CCIP in a 45° dive at 2,300 ft above the ground](shots/f16/hud_ccip.png)

1. **CCIP pipper** (continuously computed impact point): a dot in a circle where the bomb lands now. It follows the
   terrain: a hill between you and the impact takes the pipper.
2. **Bomb fall line**: from the flight path marker to the pipper. Keep the target on it.
3. **Flight path marker**.
4. **Heading**, at the top in the bombing modes.
5. **Mode**: `CCIP`.
6. **Radar altitude**: 2,300 ft.
7. **Pull-up anticipation cue** (the U): it rises from below toward the flight path marker as the dive eats the
   height you need to pull out. When it cups the marker the X flashes and Betty calls "Pull up": pull now. Here it
   already touches the marker: 2,300 ft is the last moment for a 45° dive at 413 kt.

**Example**: two MK-82s, a 45° dive at 413 kt, 2,300 ft above the ground. Roll in with the target above the marker,
let the fall line carry it down, and press **Space** when it reaches the pipper. The game's training warns against
GP (general-purpose) bombs below 2,000 ft above the ground.

### Delayed release

When the impact point lies below the HUD's lower edge, the pipper stops at the edge and a time delay cue appears:
the bombs cannot go yet.

![Delayed CCIP: a 15° dive at 4,900 ft above the ground, 6 s to go](shots/f16/hud_ccip_delay.png)

1. **Held pipper**: on the ground under the HUD's lower edge. Put the target under it.
2. **Time delay cue**: a short line across the fall line. It runs from the flight path marker down to the pipper as
   the delay runs out (10 s or more: at the marker).
3. **Bomb fall line**: from the flight path marker to the held pipper.
4. **Flight path marker**.

With the target under the pipper, hold **Space**: this designates the target, and the symbols change.

![Delayed CCIP after designation: Space held, 6 s to release](shots/f16/hud_ccip_designated.png)

1. **Steering line**: through the designated target, up the HUD. Fly the flight path marker onto it.
2. **Solution cue**: a short line on the left of the steering line. It comes down as the time to release runs out
   (10 mr a second; above 10 s it waits at the HUD's top edge).
3. **Flight path marker**: the bombs go when the solution cue reaches it (0.9 s to go). Keep **Space** held.
4. **Time to release**, in place of the time to the steerpoint.

The release needs positive g and less than 90° of bank. Releasing **Space** drops the designation.

## 12. Laser bombs, TV weapons and HARM

These use the MFDs (multi-function displays) of the original game. The HUD adds a diamond at the TV weapon's or the
HARM's target (`PRE` / `HARM` in the mode window).

### Laser bombs: MK-82L, MK-83L, MK-84L

**Keys**: the FLIR (forward-looking infrared) pod on station 3; **[** to the laser bomb brings up the FLIR page.
**Ctrl+arrows** slew the camera, **Numpad +/−** zoom ×1 / 2 / 4 / 8, **L** the laser, **Space** releases.

![The FLIR page, laser on](shots/f16/mfd_flir.png)

1. **LASER ON**: the bomb flies to the camera's centre point.
2. **WIDE / SPOT** field of view (a page button switches it).
3. **Zoom** (×1 here) with its arrows.
4. **Centre cross**: the laser spot. Release the slew keys and the camera holds this point.
5. **Range** to the point: 3.1 NM.

**Example**: slew the cross onto the target, release the keys, press **L**, and release in a dive or level. The bomb
steers to the spot when the spot is on the ground within 60° of its path; with the laser off it falls as a plain
bomb (CCIP).

### TV weapons: AGM-65 Maverick, AGM-62, GBU-15, Popeye (PRE)

![The TV page of an AGM-65](shots/f16/mfd_tv.png)

1. **TV**: the weapon's camera picture.
2. **RDY**: ready to fire.
3. **Zoom**.
4. **Aim cross**: the weapon's aim point.

**Example**: with a radar lock the Maverick (209 kg, beyond 27 km) starts on the locked target and homes by itself
after **Space**. The AGM-62, GBU-15 and Popeye fly where the camera's centre points: keep the cross on the target
until the hit (`TRA`, then `TER` near the aim). **Z** makes the picture full screen.

### JDAM and GBU-39: GPS bombs (modern weapons)

With F-16 version = Sufa, the AG list adds the GPS bombs on stations 3, 4, 6 and 7: **GBU-31** (2,000 lb, Mk 84),
**GBU-31V3** (BLU-109 penetrator), **GBU-32** (1,000 lb), **GBU-38** (500 lb) and **GBU-39** (four small bombs on
one rack). On the Barak they need Extras → **Weapons = Modern**: then it lists the GBU-31 and GBU-38 on stations 3,
4, 6 and 7 and the GBU-39 on stations 3 and 7, the ones its real counterparts carried: the IAF's Block 30 / 40 F-16s
got the JDAM in 2000–2003 ([sources](../real-weapons.md#17-which-jet-carried-which-modern-weapon)).

**When**: a fixed ground target, from a safe height or distance, in any weather. **Keys**: **[** until the bomb;
**K** PRE ↔ VIS, **Shift+K** the next PRE target; **Space** releases.

A GPS bomb has no seeker and never follows a laser spot: it flies to the coordinates it gets when it leaves the jet.

- **PRE** (pre-planned, the default): each station's target, set on the briefing TSD: click the station's row in the
  GPS targets panel (the map's lower-right corner), then a unit, a waypoint or a map point
  ([F-16I guide](f16i.md#setting-the-pre-targets-on-the-briefing-tsd), with a picture). A station without one takes
  the route's target waypoint. On a GBU-39 rack each bomb can have its own: the rack's row opens into rows `3-1` ..
  `3-4`. **Shift+K** steps through the stored targets and the steerpoints.
- **VIS** (visual): a point you pick in flight with the radar or the FLIR pod (below).
- With no point the bomb falls on the CCIP pipper's point.

#### VIS: a target you pick in flight

In VIS you pick the target in flight with a sensor. At the moment you release, the bomb takes that point's coordinates
and flies there. After the release it follows neither the radar nor the laser: you can turn away, drop the radar point
or switch the laser off.

**With the radar** (no pod needed):
1. Select the GPS bomb (**[**).
2. Press **K** until the weapon window (the last line of the HUD's lower-left column) reads `VIS`.
3. Put the radar in air-to-ground: press **R** until the radar page shows GMT or MAP, then **Q** until it shows MAP.
4. Click the target's spot on the MAP page, off the contacts (a double-click on a contact locks it instead): the HUD's
   TD box jumps onto it and the steering line runs through it.
5. Put the flight path marker on the steering line.
6. When `IN RNG` shows, press **Space**.

**Backspace** clears the point.

**With the FLIR pod** (on station 3):
1. Select the GPS bomb and press **K** for `VIS`, as above. Selecting a GPS bomb opens the stores page, not the FLIR
   pod.
2. Press **I**: the FLIR page and the pod's camera ([laser bombs](#laser-bombs-mk-82l-mk-83l-mk-84l)).
3. Slew the cross onto the target with **Ctrl+arrows** (**Cmd+arrows** on a Mac); **Numpad + / −** zooms.
4. Press **L**, laser on: the FLIR pod's point now wins over the radar's, and the TD box moves onto it. The point counts
   only on the ground: a cross on the sky gives none.
5. Steer and release as above. You may switch the laser off once the bomb has left.

**Which point the bomb takes**: the FLIR pod's point with the laser on, else the radar's designated point, else the CCIP
pipper's point (then no TD box: the CCIP symbols show).

**Before you release, check** that the weapon window (the last line of the HUD's lower-left column) reads `VIS` and that
the TD box sits on your target.

![A GBU-31 in PRE 6 km from its target, in range](shots/f16/hud_jdam.png)

1. **TD box**: the bomb's target.
2. **Azimuth steering line**: put the flight path marker on it.
3. **Flight path marker**.
4. **LAR staple**: the ranges the bomb can reach, Rmin at its bottom, Rmax at its top.
5. **Range caret**: your ground distance to the target.
6. **Scale**: 5 NM at the top, 0 at the bottom.
7. **IN RNG**: the caret is inside the staple; outside, `TMR m:ss` counts the time to max range.
8. **PRE**: the mode, in the weapon window.

Release anywhere while `IN RNG` shows: **Space** drops the bomb at once (no CCIP wait), and it turns toward the
target at up to 3 g. The CCIP pipper is not drawn while the bomb has a target. With HUD = Original: a diamond on the
target with a line from the flight path marker, a dot in the diamond and `IN RNG` (row 5) inside the LAR, the range
`R x.x` NM in row 3. After the release `TTI m:ss` counts down the last released bomb's time to impact (the right
column's time row; HUD = Original: a fourth row under the right text column); **F11** follows the bomb from outside.

| Bomb | Weight | Reach |
| --- | --- | --- |
| GBU-31 / GBU-31V3 | 946 / 981 kg | about 24 km from 40,000 ft, 8 km from 10,000 ft at 485 kt |
| GBU-32 | 468 kg | as the GBU-31 |
| GBU-38 | 253 kg | as the GBU-31 |
| GBU-39 | 129 kg each | about 110 km from 40,000 ft (it has wings) |

**Example**: the picture. A GBU-31's PRE target lies 6 km (3.2 NM) ahead, 4° right; you dive at 486 kt from
10,050 ft. Rmax is about 7 km (3.9 NM), Rmin about 1.9 km (1 NM): the caret sits inside the staple and `IN RNG`
shows. Put the flight path marker on the steering line and press **Space**: the GBU-31 glides onto the target.
Further out the staple shows `TMR` and the seconds until you reach Rmax ([F-16I guide](f16i.md#gps-bombs-on-the-hud)).

### HARM: AGM-88

![The HARM page with a radar site in range](shots/f16/mfd_harm.png)

1. **HARM**: the page.
2. **IN RANGE**: the selected site is inside the HARM's range.
3. **Target**: the radar site's symbol.

**Example**: the HARM (360 kg, Mach 2, 150 km) lists the radar sites within ±15° of the nose out to 20 NM, nearest
first. **Ctrl+Return** steps through them. Fire (**Space**) with the HUD diamond near the centre; the HARM then
chases its site to the end, emitting or silent. The Extras switch *HARM targets: Real* fills the list from the RWR's
active emitters.

## 13. Landing: the gear-down HUD

**When**: on final approach, gear handle down (**G**). **Keys**: **G** gear, **F** flaps, **B** wheel brakes, **Ctrl+T**
calls the tower near an Israeli base. The approach speeds and touchdown limits are in section 4.

With the gear down the heading scale moves to the top, and the AoA (angle of attack) bracket appears beside the flight
path marker. The ILS (instrument landing system) lines guide you to the nearest airbase's runway.

![Final approach, 2.2 NM out at about 700 ft, 141 kt, 5° AoA](shots/f16/landing_final.png)

1. **Flight path marker**: on the runway threshold here. Keep it there.
2. **AoA bracket**: the marker at the bracket's top edge is 11°, at its centre 13° (the landing angle), at its bottom
   edge 15°.
3. **ILS glide-slope line**: it crosses the localizer line at the HUD's centre when you are on the runway's centre
   line and on a 5° glide path. It drops when you are high.
4. **Heading**, at the top with the gear down.
5. **Airspeed**.
6. **Altitude**.
7. **Radar altitude**: about 700 ft above the ground.

**Example**: here the jet flies a 3° path at 5° AoA. The glide-slope line sits above the HUD's centre: you are under
the 5° path. The bracket hangs above the marker: the jet is fast. Raise the nose and slow down, and the bracket comes
down around the marker. In this cockpit the HUD glass ends low: from about 6° AoA the marker and the bracket sit under
the glass's lower edge. On a 13° approach, fly the speed: 165 kt on final (the game's landing lesson).

## 14. Better cockpit

**When**: any flight. **Keys**: tap **V** for the whole panel (hold it to glance down and back), **PgUp** / **PgDn**
slide it.

With Better cockpit on, the panel's lamps and engine gauges show the flight model's own values, each engine needle sits
on the dial its label names, and the MFD pages are drawn sharp. In the 1998 game the indexer shows all three symbols
lit, the lamps stay dark, and three engine needles read rough values on the wrong dials (the throttle on OIL, the RPM on
NOZ POS, the temperature on RPM; FTIT stays still). The needles follow their values with a 0.3 s instrument lag.

![The F-16 panel with Better cockpit on](shots/f16/better_panel.png)

1. **AoA indexer**, left of the HUD glass: one symbol lights. Upper chevron: slow (AoA above 14°). Donut: on speed
   (12 to 14°, best 13°). Lower chevron: fast (below 12°). It works with the gear up or down.
2. **AR/NWS lamp**, in the AR status indicator right of the HUD glass: a green `AR NWS` while nose-wheel steering is
   engaged. The game steers the nose wheel with the rudder pedals whenever the jet is on the ground, so the lamp
   lights on the ground and goes out at lift-off. RDY and DISC stay dark: the game has no air refuelling.
3. **FLCS lamp**: a flight-control failure (hydraulics damage, or the flight controls lost).
4. **TO/LDG CONFIG lamp**: below 10,000 ft and 190 kt, descending faster than 250 ft/min, with the gear not down.
5. **OIL**: the engine oil pressure, about 30 psi at idle and 45 psi at military; it falls to 0 with the engine.
6. **NOZ POS**: the exhaust nozzle, in % open: about 85 % at idle with the gear down, 20 % at idle with the gear up,
   5 % at military. It opens to 50 % in AB 1 and 100 % in AB 2.
7. **RPM**: the engine's real RPM, 60 % at idle, 100 % at military. It needs about 3 s from idle to military.
8. **FTIT** (fan turbine inlet temperature): about 450 °C at idle and 650 °C at military, a little more in AB. It
   rises in about 2 s and falls slowly.
9. **MFD pages**: the same pages, drawn at the screen's resolution. The TV and FLIR pictures stay as they are.

The oil pressure and nozzle values are our estimate from the F-16 flight manual's description; the other gauges
follow the flight model. The fuel counter stays in the box right of the ICP, where the 1998 game draws it: the real
jet shows its fuel total on the fuel quantity indicator on the right console, outside the panel picture.

**Example**: the picture is the jet on the runway at idle with the gear down: AR/NWS is lit, NOZ POS reads about 85 %
open (ground idle), OIL about 30 psi, RPM about 60 %, and the lower chevron is lit (fast). On final approach, fly the
donut: the upper chevron means raise the speed, the lower one lower it. When TO/LDG CONFIG lights on the approach,
lower the gear (**G**). Select AB 2 (**8**): NOZ POS swings to 100 % open at once, and RPM stays at 100 %. At
lift-off AR/NWS goes out; after landing it lights again: steer with the rudder.

## 15. Night vision

**When**: night missions (the game's night is 20:00–05:30; mission 214 starts at 01:00), with Graphics → MORE →
Night vision = Goggles. **Keys**: **Shift+N** puts the goggles on and takes them off.

The goggles are ours: the 1998 game has none. They turn the outside view into a brightened green picture; the
cockpit and the HUD stay as you see them under the goggles. Set Graphics → MORE → Night lights = Detailed too, for
the moon, the stars and the lights on the ground.

![01:00 without goggles](shots/f16/night.png)

![The same moment through the goggles](shots/f16/night_nvg.png)

- The ground's relief and texture show, and the horizon is clear: fly by the outside view.
- The goggles set their own gain: the darker the scene, the more they brighten it, and the grainier it gets.
- The HUD shows in its brightest green while the goggles are on; your own level comes back when they come off.
- The panel's lighting suits the goggles from 1996. Before that its glow washes out the picture's lower part.
- A view full of light (a close explosion, daylight) darkens the goggles: take them off by day.
- The goggles follow the mission's year: none before 1972, a grainy Gen II up to 1981, a Gen III from 1982, the
  sharpest from 1996 (the 1998 missions).
- A bright light (a fire, a flare, a runway light) gets a halo around it.
- The picture darkens toward its edges.

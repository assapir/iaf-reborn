# F-16 pilot guide (realistic settings)

This guide is for flying the F-16D (Barak) in this game with every realistic option switched on. The numbers come from
the game's own data and tests. Where the game differs from the real jet, the guide says so.

![The F-16 cockpit on the runway](shots/f16/cockpit_runway.png)

---

## 1. Settings

Set these on the Preferences screen before the flight. The original 1998 game is the default everywhere, so each of
these has to be switched on.

### Extras tab

| Row                    | Set to                            | What it does                                                                                        |
| ---------------------- | --------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Flight data            | Real aircraft                     | The F-16C Block 30/40 (F110-GE-100) numbers: weight, thrust, roll, stall speed, fuel flow, ceiling  |
| Weapon data            | Real weapons                      | Real missile weights, speeds and ranges, the APG-68 radar range, 511 gun rounds at 6,000 rounds/min |
| HUD                    | Real                              | The F-16C HUD from the USAF manuals instead of the 1998 HUD                                         |
| HUD pitch ladder       | Conformal                         | The ladder lies on the real horizon (only matters with the original HUD)                            |
| Sky                    | Atmospheric                       | A physical sky with the sun, and haze that takes its colour                                         |
| Terrain close up       | Detailed                          | Ground detail below about 500 m                                                                     |
| Imagery Israel         | Survey 2 m (or Survey 2 m modern) | 2 m photos of Israel, if setup converted them                                                       |
| Imagery outside Israel | Sentinel-2 (or Sentinel-2 modern) | 10 m satellite imagery outside Israel, if setup converted it                                        |

A greyed-out imagery choice means its data was not converted (`tools/setup.sh --imagery`).

## 2. The jet in numbers

These are the game's values with Flight data = Real, checked by its validation test against public F-16C figures.

| Item                                         | In the game                     | Real F-16C                        |
| -------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| Empty weight                                 | 19,000 lb                       | about 19,000 lb                   |
| Internal fuel                                | 7,000 lb                        | about 7,000 lb                    |
| Thrust, military / full afterburner          | 17,400 / 29,000 lbf             | 17,000 / 28,900 lbf (F110-GE-100) |
| 1 g stall speed, sea level                   | 118 kt                          | about 120–130 kt                  |
| Corner speed (9 g)                           | about 355 kt                    | about 330–350 kt                  |
| g limit                                      | 9 g up to 25,000 ft, less above | 9 g                               |
| Max roll rate                                | 280°/s (0 to 90° in 0.55 s)     | 240–308°/s                        |
| Max speed, sea level                         | 782 kt (Mach 1.18)              | about 795 kt (Mach 1.2)           |
| Max speed, 40,000 ft                         | 1,079 kt (Mach 1.88)            | about 1,150 kt (Mach 2.0)         |
| Climb at 350 kt, sea level, full afterburner | about 41,000 ft/min             | about 50,000 ft/min               |
| Instantaneous turn, 420 kt at 10,000 ft      | 21.7°/s at 8.1 g                | 20–26°/s at 9 g                   |
| Fuel flow, military / full afterburner       | about 11,000 / 59,000 lb/h      | 9,000–12,000 / 50,000–65,000 lb/h |
| Service ceiling                              | 50,000 ft                       | above 50,000 ft                   |

What that means in the cockpit:

- **Fuel.** Internal fuel lasts about 7 minutes at full afterburner, or about 38 minutes at military power at sea
  level. Below 1,500 lb the Real HUD shows **FUEL**. This bingo is fixed: you cannot set it as in the real jet.
- **Speeds the game's own training uses**: rotate at **145 kt**, downwind at **200 kt** and 2,000 ft above the
  ground, base and final at **165 kt**, gear down below **300 kt**. Below about 130 kt you are close to the stall.
- **Drag chute** (Shift+B) is visual only on the F-16. Only the F-4E's chute has drag with Real data.
- **Nose-wheel steering** comes from the rudder pedals (±32°), as on the real jet.

## 3. Start-up, take-off and landing

### On the runway

A mission that starts at an airbase puts you on the runway with the gear down, full flaps, the brakes on and the
throttle at idle. The engine is already running at the lineup point. If you start elsewhere on the base, the first
throttle key starts it, and the RPM then climbs 15 % per second.

### Take-off

This is the procedure from the game's take-off lesson:

1. Check the gear is down (three green lights next to the gear lever) and the flaps are out (**F**).
2. Release the brakes (**B**).
3. Throttle to full afterburner: **8** (AB 2). The afterburner lights only after the engine reaches 100 %, about
   2 s from military power.
4. Keep the runway centreline with the rudder (**Numpad 0** / **Decimal**, or the pedals).
5. At **145 kt**, pull gently back until the flight path marker is above the horizon.
6. Gear up (**G**) and flaps up (**F**). Keep the gear speed below 300 kt.

![Take-off roll, HUD gear down](shots/f16/takeoff_roll.png)

### Landing

This is the circuit from the game's landing lesson:

1. **Downwind**: 2,000 ft above the ground, **200 kt**, the runway off your wing. Gear down (**G**), flaps out (**F**).
2. **Base**: throttle 60–80 % (keys **2** to **4**), about 20° of bank, slow to **165 kt**, aim for a 4° approach.
3. **Final**: **165 kt**. Put the flight path marker on the cross of the ILS lines on the HUD (the localizer and a 4°
   glide slope). The Real HUD keeps the ILS lines.
4. Over the threshold: throttle to idle (**1**) and bring the flight path marker slowly up to the horizon.
5. After touchdown: brakes (**B**).

With Real landing limits and Easy landing off, a touchdown faster than **4 m/s (about 790 ft/min)** or more than
**15° nose-up** is a crash ("sink rate" / "tail strike"). With the gear up the sink limit is a quarter of that.

![Final approach, gear-down HUD with the ILS](shots/f16/landing_final.png)

### Tower and autopilot

- **Ctrl+T** calls the tower near an Israeli base: "proceed to runway" and the landing calls.
- **A** cycles the autopilot: off → level → NAV (follows the waypoints) → off. In NAV the throttle keys are ignored.
  **W** / **Shift+W** pick the next / previous waypoint.

## 4. Handling

- **The stick on the keyboard** is all or nothing: a tap gives full deflection at once, as in the original. A short tap
  is enough for a gentle pull. A joystick is much smoother.
- **Flight-path hold.** With the stick centred the jet holds its flight path, like the F-16's flight controls.
- **Deep stall.** The real F-16's flight controls keep a normal pull from stalling or spinning it. Its one known
  departure is the deep stall. In the game it starts when you zoom nose-high (30° or more) until the speed is gone. The
  jet then hangs at about 60° angle of attack with the nose rocking near the horizon, sinking at about 11,000 ft/min.
  Holding the stick forward does **not** recover it. **Recovery**: rock the stick in phase with the nose, pull while
  it rises and push while it falls, as with the real jet's manual pitch override. After a few cycles (about 8 s and
  300 m) the nose falls through and the controls take over again. Then pull out of the dive.
- **g-effects.** With No blackouts off, a hard pull greys and blacks out the screen.

## 5. The Real HUD

The Real HUD draws the F-16C HUD from the USAF manuals: the airspeed tape on the left, the altitude tape on the right,
the heading scale, the bending pitch ladder and the flight path marker. **H** changes the HUD colour.

![The Real HUD in NAV](shots/f16/hud_nav.png)

### Scales and windows

- **Airspeed** (left): a moving tape with a boxed readout and a "C" (calibrated airspeed).
- **Altitude** (right): a barometric tape (above sea level) with a boxed readout.
- **Radar altitude**: the **R** box under the altitude tape (`R 1,250`) shows your height above the ground, up to
  50,000 ft. There is no ALT switch yet, so the tape itself is always barometric.
- **Left column**: ARM (in a weapon mode), Mach, the maximum g of the flight, and the mode (`NAV`, `6 SRM`, `2 MRM`,
  `EEGS`, `STRF`, `CCIP`, `PRE`, `HARM`).
- **Right column**: the radar altitude, the slant range (B = to the steerpoint, F = to the radar target), the time to
  the steerpoint, and the steerpoint's distance and number (`020>03`).
- **Heading scale**: at the bottom in NAV, at the top in the A-G modes and with the gear down.
- **Bank scale**: tics every 10° to 45° on an arc under the heading scale, just above the glareshield (raised a little
  into the glass so it is not hidden by the panel).
- **Centre window**: `FUEL` below the bingo, `SHOOT` when a missile shot is good.
- **Steerpoint**: a small diamond drawn on the ground at the waypoint.
- **Gear down**: the AoA bracket (the flight path marker at its centre is 13° AoA, the landing angle).

### Not drawn

Not drawn yet: the VVI scale, ALOW, DTOS and CCRP. Neither the game nor the original has DTOS or CCRP: bombing is
CCIP only.

## 6. Radar (AN/APG-68)

With Weapon data = Real the radar detects at **80 km (43 NM)** in LRS and STT. It scans a ±60° cone ahead and does not
see targets lower than 30 m above the ground or behind terrain. The left MFD shows it.

| Key                           | Action                                              |
| ----------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| **R**                         | Radar on: A-A, then toggles A-A ↔ A-G               |
| **Q**                         | Mode: A-A LRS → TWS → ACM; A-G GMT ↔ MAP            |
| **S**                         | Standby                                             |
| **.** / **,**                 | Range scale up / down (5, 10, 20, 40, 80 NM)        |
| **Return** / **Shift+Return** | Next / previous contact (in LRS this locks it: STT) |
| Click a blip                  | Lock that contact                                   |
| **Backspace**                 | Drop the lock                                       |
| **\\** (hold)                 | Boresight: a ±12° lock cone straight ahead          |

- **LRS** (long-range search) lists up to 15 contacts, nearest first. **TWS** keeps a selected track without a hard
  lock. **ACM** and **BORE** lock the nearest contact at once, out to 10 NM.
- **STT** (a lock) follows the target every frame and switches the range scale by itself. Locking a target tells it
  you locked it (and its RWR sounds).
- **GMT** shows moving ground targets, **MAP** the ground. Clicking on the map designates a point (a cross), but no
  weapon uses it: the game has no CCRP.

![The radar page with a locked contact](shots/f16/mfd_radar_stt.png)

## 7. MFDs

The F-16's left MFD starts on the radar, the right one on the TSD. Click the buttons around an MFD, or use:

| Key                        | Page                                 |
| -------------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| **T**                      | TSD (tactical map)                   |
| **D**                      | Damage report (GO / NOGO per system) |
| **I**                      | FLIR (with a FLIR pod)               |
| **Z**                      | Full-screen weapon MFD (TV or FLIR)  |
| **V**, **PgUp** / **PgDn** | Slide the instrument panel up / down |

Picking a weapon brings up its page: the stores page for bombs and rockets, the radar for the radar missiles and
the A-A gun, the HARM page for the HARM, the TV page for TV weapons, and the FLIR page for laser bombs with the pod.
The **stores** page shows each pylon and its count, the gun rounds and fuel. Its buttons pick a station and set the
bomb ripple: quantity 1–14, interval 10–200.

## 8. Self-protection

- **RWR.** The panel's RWR dial shows emitters and missiles around you, and the **ai** and **sam** lamps light for an
  air or ground threat. A new threat sounds a tone, and a missile launch at you sounds the launch warning.
- **Chaff (Insert)** and **flares (Delete)**: the F-16 carries **90 chaff and 60 flares**. Each press releases one.
  At release, every missile of the decoy's kind that was launched at you rolls once: chaff fools a radar missile with
  10 % (30 % above 4 g), a flare fools an IR missile with 33 % (50 % above 4 g), and **never with the afterburner
  lit**. One missile that resists ends the roll for the others. So: pull hard, come out of afterburner, then flare.
- **ECM (J)**: the F-16 has it built in. Switching it on jams each radar-guided missile already flying at you with a
  60 % chance. It has no effect on SAMs.

## 9. Weapons

**M** cycles the master mode NAV → A-A → A-G, and **N** returns to NAV. **]** selects the next air-to-air weapon and
**[** the next air-to-ground one. **Space** fires the selected weapon and **Tab** fires the gun. There is no master
arm, but nothing fires with the gear handle down (the original's Safety cheat that allows the gun is not built).

What the F-16 can load on the Arming screen (stations 1 and 9 are the wingtips, 5 the centreline):

| Python 3                             | 2, 3, 7, 8                                      |
| Python 4                             | 1, 2, 8, 9                                      |
| AMRAAM                               | 2, 3, 7, 8                                      |
| AIM-7M Sparrow                       | 2, 8                                            |
| MK-82 / MK-83 / M117 (×3), MK-84     | 3, 4, 6, 7 (MK-83 / MK-84 single also on 5)     |

| AGM-65 Maverick                      | 2, 3, 4, 6, 7, 8 (×3 on 3, 4, 6, 7)             |

### Gun (M61A1)

With Real data: **511 rounds**, 6,000 rounds/min (20 rounds every 0.2 s), 1,030 m/s. That is about 5 s of firing.

- **A-A gun** (**]** to the gun): the Real HUD's **EEGS**. Without a lock it shows the funnel for 600–3,000 ft and the
  sweeping MRGS lines. With a radar lock it adds the T-symbol, the 4 mr pipper and the range circle, which unwinds
  inside 2 NM. Put the pipper on the target and fire.
- **A-G gun** (**[** to the gun): **STRF**, a strafe pipper on the predicted impact.

![EEGS with a locked target](shots/f16/hud_eegs.png)

### IR missiles: AIM-9L, Python 3, Python 4 (6 SRM)

| Missile  | Real data                                  |
| -------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| AIM-9L   | 86 kg, Mach 2.5, 35.4 km                   |
| Python 3 | 120 kg, Mach 3.5, 15 km, 30° off-boresight |
| Python 4 | 120 kg, Mach 3.5, 15 km, 60° off-boresight |

1. **]** until `SRM`. The seeker looks for a target near the HUD centre: a growl, then a higher tone when it **locks**.
2. **With a radar lock** the seeker is slaved to the locked target within its cone off the nose (AIM-9L 35°, Python 3
   30°, Python 4 60°).
3. **Off-boresight**: in the free-look view (**Shift+arrows**) or padlock (**F3**), the F-16's helmet sight lets the
   seeker take the target you look at, within the missile's cone.
4. The seeker sees a target at up to its full range from behind, but beyond **half** the range only a jet **with its
   afterburner lit**.
5. Fire with **Space** when locked. Locked and in range, the Real HUD shows **SHOOT** in the centre window (where FUEL
   goes); the original HUD shows its shoot cue, a triangle under the target box.

![SRM with the seeker locked](shots/f16/hud_srm.png)

### Radar missiles: AMRAAM, AIM-7M (2 MRM)

| Missile             | Real data                                                             |
| ------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| AMRAAM (AIM-120A/B) | 157 kg, Mach 4, 70 km, active: needs nothing after the launch         |
| AIM-7M Sparrow      | 230 kg, Mach 4, 70 km, **semi-active**: needs your lock until it hits |

1. Radar on (**R**), lock a target (**Return** in LRS, or click its blip).
2. **]** until `MRM`. The Real HUD shows the **ASEC** circle with its steering dot, and on the right the **DLZ**: your
   target's range caret and closure against the missile's maximum and minimum range.
3. Inside the DLZ, steer the dot into the circle: **SHOOT** comes up in the centre window. Fire (**Space**).
4. A **Sparrow** loses its guidance as soon as the lock drops or changes (**Q**, **R**, **S**, **Return**, a click,
   **Backspace**). Keep the lock until it hits. An AMRAAM is on its own after the launch.

The DLZ example from the game: an AMRAAM head-on at 250 m/s against a 250 m/s target has a maximum range of 55.7 km
and a minimum of 2.15 km.

![MRM: ASEC and the DLZ](shots/f16/hud_mrm.png)

### Bombs and rockets: CCIP

MK-82 / MK-83 / MK-84 / M117, the CBU-87 / CBU-97 clusters and ZUNNI rockets.

1. **[** until `CCIP`. The pipper (a dot in a circle) is where the bomb will hit, joined to the flight path marker by
   the fall line. It accounts for the terrain: a hill between you and the impact takes the pipper.
2. Put the pipper on the target and hold **Space**: the ripple releases one store per interval while you hold it.
3. **Delayed release**: if the impact is below the HUD's bottom edge, hold **Space** with the target under the pipper
   at the edge. The steering line and the release cue then run down, and the bombs go when the time-to-go reaches
   0.9 s. The release also needs positive g and less than 90° of bank.
4. The ripple quantity and spacing are set on the stores page (default: 2 bombs, 10 m apart).

The game's training warns against GP bombs below 2,000 ft above the ground. There is no fuse or arming time: a bomb
dropped from any height bursts at the ground.

![CCIP with the pipper on a target](shots/f16/hud_ccip.png)

### Laser bombs: MK-82L, MK-83L, MK-84L

These need the **FLIR pod** on station 3.

1. **[** to the laser bomb: the FLIR page comes up.
2. Slew the FLIR camera with **Ctrl+arrows** (9°/s at zoom 1); release the keys to lock it on the point in the middle.
   **Numpad +/−** (or the page's buttons) zoom ×1 / 2 / 4 / 8, and a page button switches WIDE / SPOT.
3. **L** turns the laser on (`LASER ON`). The bomb then flies to the FLIR camera's centre point, if that point is within
   60° of the bomb's line and on the ground. With the laser off it falls like a normal bomb (CCIP).
4. Release with **Space** as for CCIP.

![The FLIR page, laser on](shots/f16/mfd_flir.png)

### TV weapons: AGM-65 Maverick, AGM-62, GBU-15, Popeye (PRE)

1. **[** to the TV weapon: the TV page shows the weapon's camera, and the HUD shows a diamond at its aim point.
2. Slew with **Ctrl+arrows** and zoom with **Numpad +/−**. A Maverick picked with a radar lock starts on the locked
   target. **Z** makes the TV picture full screen.
3. Fire with **Space**. The **Maverick** (Real: 209 kg, over 27 km) homes on its target by itself. The **AGM-62,
   GBU-15 and Popeye** are flown by the camera: the camera rides the weapon, and wherever the centre of the picture
   points, the weapon goes (TRA, then TER near the aim).

![The TV page](shots/f16/mfd_tv.png)

### HARM: AGM-88

Real data: 360 kg, Mach 2, 150 km.

1. **[** to the AGM-88: the HUD goes to `HARM`, the HARM page comes up and the HARM sensor starts.
2. The HARM page lists the radar sites inside a ±15° cone ahead, out to 20 NM, nearest first. **Ctrl+Return** steps to
   the next target. An Extras switch, _HARM targets: Original / RWR_, can fill the list from the RWR's active emitters
   instead.
3. Fire with **Space** when the target's diamond is near the HUD centre. After the launch the HARM chases its target
   whether or not it keeps emitting.

![The HARM page](shots/f16/mfd_harm.png)

### Jettison

**Shift+C**: the first press drops the fuel tanks, the second the bombs.
